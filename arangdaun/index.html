BabChapter 00

Yang Menuliskan KisahThe Ones Who Wrote the Tale

Pujangga bukan penonton kekuasaan, melainkan salah satu pemeran utama yang ikut membentuknya.The poet was no spectator of power, but one of the leading figures who helped shape it.

Bayangkan seorang empu duduk bersila di serambi griya, cahaya pelita minyak kelapa menggeliat di pinggir lontar. Ia tidak sedang "menulis buku". Ia sedang menyusun sebuah ritual: setiap suku kata berat dan ringan dalam bait adalah langkah menuju kesatuan dengan yang ilahi. Bagi Jawa Kuno, puisi adalah yoga dengan pena.

Itulah sebabnya kata yang dipakai untuk sastra, kalangwan, berarti "keindahan yang membawa keterpesonaan", bukan hiburan. Pujangga adalah pandita, birokrat, penasihat, sekaligus arsitek makna. Ia bukan sekadar penonton kekuasaan; ia salah satu pemeran utamanya, figur kunci dalam sejarah budaya yang membentuk zamannya.

Raja membangun candi dari batu. Pujangga membangun raja dari kata.

Tulisan ini menelusuri jejak itu: dari kakawin abad ke-9 sampai serat abad ke-19, dari lontar Bali sampai rak Leiden. Di beberapa tempat ditambahkan pembacaan interpretatif, dan semuanya ditandai jelas agar pembaca bisa membedakan yang tercatat dari yang ditafsirkan.

Picture an empu, a master poet, sitting cross-legged on the porch of a griya, the light of a coconut-oil lamp flickering along the edge of a lontar leaf. He is not "writing a book." He is composing a ritual: every heavy and light syllable in a verse is a step toward union with the divine. For ancient Java, poetry was yoga practiced with a stylus.

That is why the word for literature, kalangwan, means "beauty that brings enchantment," not entertainment. The poet was priest, bureaucrat, adviser, and architect of meaning all at once. He was no mere onlooker of power; he was one of its leading figures, a key player in the cultural history that shaped it.

Kings build temples from stone. Poets build kings from words.

This essay follows that trail: from the kakawin of the 9th century to the serat of the 19th, from Balinese lontar to the shelves of Leiden. In several places an interpretive reading has been added, and each is clearly marked so that readers can tell what is recorded from what is interpreted.

BabChapter 01

Daun, Pisau, dan JelagaLeaf, Knife, and Soot

Tulisan adalah luka pada daun, dan jelaga membuatnya terbaca.Writing is a wound in the leaf, and soot makes it legible.

Lontar (Borassus flabellifer) direbus, dikeringkan, dipres, lalu digores dengan pisau kecil (pangot). Tulisan sebenarnya adalah luka pada daun; jelaga dari kemiri bakar dioleskan agar luka itu terbaca. Karena daun rapuh oleh serangga dan lembap, sebuah naskah hidup hanya jika disalin ulang. Sastra Nusantara adalah sastra yang bertahan lewat penyalinan, bukan lewat penyimpanan.

Pergantian zaman terbaca dari pergantian media. Kakawin ditulis di lontar untuk para pandita. Ketika Islam dan perdagangan pesisir tumbuh, hadir daluwang (kertas kulit kayu Broussonetia papyrifera) dan kemudian kertas Eropa yang dibawa VOC. Abad ke-19 menambah percetakan dan koran berbahasa Jawa seperti Bramartani. Tiap media mengubah siapa yang boleh membaca.

Penilaian arti

lontar mendorong sastra yang terikat metrum, karena bentuk yang ketat membantu hafalan dan mencegah salah salin. Kertas dan cetak membebaskan bentuk, tetapi juga melepaskan sastra dari aura sakral. Setiap teknologi tulis membeli kemudahan dengan harga kesakralan.

Lontar (Borassus flabellifer) is boiled, dried, pressed, then incised with a small knife (pangot). The writing is really a wound in the leaf; soot from burnt candlenut is rubbed in so that the wound can be read. Because leaves are fragile against insects and damp, a manuscript lives only if it is copied again. The literature of Nusantara is one that survived through copying, not through storage.

A change of era can be read in a change of medium. Kakawin were written on lontar for priests. As Islam and coastal trade grew, daluwang (bark paper from Broussonetia papyrifera) appeared, and later the European paper brought by the VOC. The 19th century added printing and Javanese-language newspapers such as Bramartani. Each medium changed who was allowed to read.

Interpretive reading

lontar encouraged literature bound by meter, since strict form aids memorization and guards against copying errors. Paper and print freed form, but also released literature from its sacred aura. Every writing technology buys convenience at the price of sanctity.

Mataram Kuno dan Kediri: Kelahiran KalangwanOld Mataram and Kediri: The Birth of Kalangwan

Ketika Jawa belajar menerjemahkan dunia, bukan menjiplaknya.When Java learned to translate the world rather than copy it.

Sebelum semua nama empu yang biasa kita hafal, ada Kakawin Ramayana (sekitar abad ke-9, masa Mataram Kuno di Jawa Tengah), gubahan Jawa Kuno paling awal yang mengadaptasi kisah India dengan kebebasan mengejutkan. Ia membuktikan bahwa Jawa tidak menjiplak Sanskerta, melainkan menerjemahkan dunia.

Kakawin Ramayana

Bentuk
Kakawin: puisi naratif Jawa Kuno bermetrum Sanskerta, 26 kanto (sarga).
Bahasa
Jawa Kuno (Kawi) dengan banyak serapan Sanskerta.
Aksara
aksara Jawa Kuno (Kawi); salinan yang bertahan umumnya beraksara Bali pada lontar.
  • Hal yang menarik Para filolog menandai bagian yang dekat dan yang jauh dari Bhattikavya; membaca keduanya berdampingan memperlihatkan cara penyair Jawa berpikir.
  • Titik tolak Soewito Santoso (Ramayana Kakawin) dan edisi Hooykaas; kamus Zoetmulder sebagai pendamping.
  • Penelusuran lebih jauh Di mana penyair memilih menyimpang, dan mengapa?

Mpu Kanwa, Arjuna Wiwaha (± 1030-an)

Di bawah Raja Airlangga, Kanwa menulis kisah Arjuna yang bertapa, digoda para bidadari, lalu diminta dewa menumpas raksasa Niwatakawaca. Banyak sarjana membaca teks ini sebagai cermin Airlangga sendiri: raja yang kehilangan istana, bertapa di hutan, lalu bangkit membangun kembali. Kajian filologi klasik dirintis Poerbatjaraka dan Hooykaas, sementara I. Kuntara Wiryamartana menelusuri bagaimana teks ini bermetamorfosis hingga ke tembang macapat Surakarta.

Sedah dan Panuluh, Bharatayuddha (1157)

Perang Pandawa-Kurawa ditulis saat Kediri dan Jenggala baru selesai saling menghancurkan. Sedah memulai, Panuluh menuntaskan. Panuluh juga menulis Hariwangsa dan Gatotkacasraya. Bagi Zoetmulder (Kalangwan), inilah puncak epos perang Jawa Kuno, dan banyak sejarawan membacanya sebagai alegori politik masa Jayabhaya. Kutipan pembuka perang: tiap kematian menuntut makna, bukan sekadar sorak kemenangan.

Triguna dan Dharmaja

Triguna menulis Kresnayana (masa Kameswara), yang adegannya terpahat di relief Candi Panataran. Dharmaja menulis Smaradahana, kisah Kama dibakar Siwa. Poerbatjaraka mengaitkan teks ini dengan awal lahirnya siklus Panji, yang kelak menjelajah ke Kamboja, Siam, dan Melayu. Catatan hati-hati: hubungan langsung dengan Panji adalah hipotesis kuat, bukan bukti tertutup.

Pertanyaan untuk direnungkan

mengapa kerajaan yang baru selesai berperang saudara justru menghasilkan epos perang saudara terbesar? Mungkin sastra adalah cara pertama sebuah bangsa mengucapkan hal yang belum boleh diucapkan politik.

Before all the names of the empu we usually memorize, there is the Kakawin Ramayana (around the 9th century, in the Old Mataram period of Central Java), the earliest Old Javanese composition to adapt an Indian tale, and with startling freedom. It proves that Java did not copy Sanskrit; it translated the world.

Kakawin Ramayana

Form
Kakawin: an Old Javanese narrative poem in Sanskrit meters, 26 cantos (sarga).
Language
Old Javanese (Kawi) with many Sanskrit loanwords.
Script
Old Javanese (Kawi) script; surviving copies are mostly in Balinese script on lontar.
  • Why read it Philologists mark which passages stay close to the Bhattikavya and which depart from it; reading the two side by side shows how Javanese poets thought.
  • Starting point Soewito Santoso (Ramayana Kakawin) and Hooykaas's edition; Zoetmulder's dictionary as a companion.
  • Further exploration Where did the poet choose to diverge, and why?

Mpu Kanwa, Arjuna Wiwaha (c. 1030s)

Under King Airlangga, Kanwa wrote the story of Arjuna in meditation, tempted by celestial nymphs, then called by the gods to destroy the demon Niwatakawaca. Many scholars read the text as a mirror of Airlangga himself: a king who lost his palace, withdrew to the forest, then rose to rebuild. Classic philological study was pioneered by Poerbatjaraka and Hooykaas, while I. Kuntara Wiryamartana traced how the text metamorphosed all the way into the macapat songs of Surakarta.

Sedah and Panuluh, Bharatayuddha (1157)

The war of the Pandavas and Kauravas was written just as Kediri and Jenggala had finished destroying each other. Sedah began it; Panuluh completed it. Panuluh also wrote the Hariwangsa and the Gatotkacasraya. For Zoetmulder (Kalangwan), this is the peak of the Old Javanese war epic, and many historians read it as a political allegory of Jayabhaya's time. In the opening of the war, every death demands meaning, not just a cheer for victory.

Triguna and Dharmaja

Triguna wrote the Kresnayana (in the time of Kameswara), whose scenes are carved in the reliefs of Candi Panataran. Dharmaja wrote the Smaradahana, the tale of Kama burned by Shiva. Poerbatjaraka linked this text to the beginnings of the Panji cycle, which would later travel to Cambodia, Siam, and the Malay world. A word of caution: a direct link to Panji is a strong hypothesis, not a closed case.

A question to ponder

why would a kingdom that had just finished a civil war produce the greatest civil-war epic? Perhaps literature is the first way a nation says what politics is not yet allowed to say.

Singhasari dan Majapahit: Sastra sebagai NegaraSinghasari and Majapahit: Literature as State

Dua hal yang membuat negara besar: senjata dan cerita.Two things make a great state: weapons and stories.

Mpu Monaguna menulis Parthayajna di masa turbulensi menjelang Singhasari; sejak itu, kisah pengasingan Arjuna terbaca sebagai nurani zaman yang gelisah.

Mpu Prapanca, Desawarnana (1365)

Prapanca, pejabat urusan agama Buddha, menulis Nagarakertagama di masa emas Hayam Wuruk: laporan perjalanan kerajaan, daftar wilayah, tata kota, sekaligus pujian. Yang membuatnya dahsyat bukan hanya isinya, tetapi nasibnya. Sesudah Majapahit surut, naskah ini nyaris hilang dari ingatan. Pada 18 November 1894, J.L.A. Brandes menemukannya di puri Cakranegara, Lombok, saat ekspedisi militer KNIL. Salinan lontarnya menjadi rujukan utama bagi rekonstruksi sejarah Majapahit. Ada ironi tajam di sini: kunci ingatan Nusantara diselamatkan dalam ekor operasi militer kolonial.

Mpu Tantular, Sutasoma

Dalam Sutasoma (pupuh 139, bait 5) lahir rumusan Bhinneka Tunggal Ika: Buddha dan Siwa berbeda, tetapi satu hakikat. Rumusan itu semula formula teologis untuk elite Majapahit, bukan slogan kebangsaan. Kelak, di abad ke-20, ia diangkat menjadi semboyan negara. Artinya, sebuah kalimat yang lahir untuk mendamaikan dua agama istana ternyata cukup lentur untuk memikul ratusan suku.

Penilaian arti

negara besar butuh dua hal: senjata dan cerita. Prapanca memberi Majapahit citra dirinya; Tantular memberi cara mengelola perbedaan. Keduanya terbukti lebih awet daripada tentara.

Mpu Monaguna wrote the Parthayajna in the turbulent years before Singhasari; from then on, the tale of Arjuna's exile could be read as the conscience of an uneasy age.

Mpu Prapanca, Desawarnana (1365)

Prapanca, an official for Buddhist affairs, wrote the Nagarakertagama in the golden age of Hayam Wuruk: a royal travel report, a list of territories, a city plan, and a eulogy all at once. What makes it extraordinary is not only its content but its fate. After Majapahit declined, the manuscript nearly vanished from memory. On 18 November 1894, J.L.A. Brandes found it in the puri of Cakranegara, Lombok, during a KNIL military expedition. The lontar copy became the main foundation for reconstructing Majapahit's history. There is a sharp irony here: the key to Nusantara's memory was rescued in the wake of a colonial military operation.

Mpu Tantular, Sutasoma

In the Sutasoma (canto 139, verse 5) the formula Bhinneka Tunggal Ika was born: Buddha and Shiva are different, yet one in essence. The phrase began as a theological formula for the Majapahit elite, not a national slogan. In the 20th century it was raised to the state motto. A sentence born to reconcile two court religions turned out flexible enough to carry hundreds of ethnic groups.

Interpretive reading

a great state needs two things: weapons and stories. Prapanca gave Majapahit its self-image; Tantular gave it a way to manage difference. Both proved more durable than armies.

Jembatan: Dari Kakawin ke MacapatThe Bridge: From Kakawin to Macapat

Dari menara Sanskerta ke tembang yang bisa dilagukan siapa saja.From the Sanskrit tower to songs anyone could sing.

Peralihan dari Majapahit ke Demak, Pajang, dan Mataram bukan patahan, melainkan metamorfosis. Nilai-nilai Devaraja tidak hilang; raja menjadi Khalifatullah yang tetap membawa wibawa pulung. Wadah lama diisi air baru: tokoh wayang tetap dipakai, tetapi diarahkan ke tauhid, seperti tampak dalam Suluk Wujil yang dikaitkan dengan Sunan Bonang.

Sastra pun turun dari menara. Metrum Sanskerta yang menuntut hafalan berat digantikan macapat, yang bisa dilagukan siapa saja. Seni rupa mengalami stilasi: wayang menjadi pipih, simbolik, dan justru memperoleh kedalaman baru. Pusat kuasa bergeser dari imperium maritim ke agraris pedalaman, dan Babad Tanah Jawi menyambungkan silsilah Mataram ke Majapahit demi legitimasi.

Yang kalah dalam sejarah sering menang dalam ingatan; yang menang sering sibuk menyambung namanya ke yang telah kalah.

The transition from Majapahit to Demak, Pajang, and Mataram was not a rupture but a metamorphosis. The values of the Devaraja did not vanish; the king became Khalifatullah, still carrying the authority of pulung. Old vessels were filled with new water: wayang characters stayed in use but were turned toward tawhid, as seen in the Suluk Wujil, attributed to Sunan Bonang.

Literature came down from the tower, too. Sanskrit meters, which demanded heavy memorization, were replaced by macapat, which anyone could sing. The visual arts were stylized: wayang became flat and symbolic, and gained new depth for it. The center of power shifted from a maritime empire to an agrarian interior, and the Babad Tanah Jawi connected Mataram's genealogy to Majapahit for the sake of legitimacy.

The defeated in history often win in memory; the victors are often busy attaching their names to the defeated.
BabChapter 05

Keraton: Yasadipura hingga RanggawarsitaThe Court: Yasadipura to Ranggawarsita

Pujangga memindahkan medan perang ke dalam bahasa.Poets moved the battlefield into language.

Yasadipura I (± 1729–1803) menggubah ulang Rama, Bratayuda, dan Menak ke bahasa Jawa Baru. Ia bukan penyalin; ia penerjemah peradaban, sehingga wiracarita kuno bisa didengar telinga baru. Putranya (Yasadipura II, juga dikenal sebagai Ranggawarsita I) melanjutkan dan menautkan tasawuf pesisir dengan etika batin Jawa, termasuk dalam Serat Centhini yang disusun pada dekade awal abad ke-19.

Ranggawarsita (1802–1873) adalah pujangga penutup. Lewat Serat Kalatidha ia menulis tentang zaman édan: ketika hendak ikut gila tak tahan, tak ikut gila tak kebagian. Para pengkaji melihatnya sebagai kritik terselubung terhadap birokrasi istana yang tunduk pada Hindia Belanda. Ia juga menulis Jaka Lodhang, Wirid Hidayat Jati, dan bersama C.F. Winter Sr. menyusun Bausastra Kawi.

Nancy Florida dalam Writing the Past, Inscribing the Future meneliti tradisi tulis keraton Surakarta (bukan Yogyakarta) dan menunjukkan bagaimana babad serta ramalan menjadi cara menulis ulang masa lalu demi membentuk masa depan. Adapun tradisi Yogyakarta lebih kuat dalam babad dinasti (Babad Giyanti), tari, dan tata praja. Banyak naskah Yogyakarta terangkut ke Inggris setelah penyerbuan 1812 dan kini tersimpan di British Library.

Penilaian arti

ketika kekuasaan nyata berpindah ke tangan kolonial, pujangga memindahkan medan perang ke bahasa. Ramalan (jangka) adalah cara masyarakat tak berdaya mengatakan: "aku masih punya akhir cerita."

Yasadipura I (c. 1729–1803) recomposed the Rama, Bratayuda, and Menak in New Javanese. He was no copyist; he was a translator of civilizations, so that ancient epics could be heard by new ears. His son (Yasadipura II, also known as Ranggawarsita I) continued the work and joined coastal Sufism with Javanese inner ethics, including in the Serat Centhini, compiled in the early decades of the 19th century.

Ranggawarsita (1802–1873) was the last of the great court poets. In the Serat Kalatidha he wrote of the zaman édan, the age of madness: to join the madness is unbearable, yet not to join means going without a share. Scholars see it as a veiled critique of a court bureaucracy bowing to the Dutch East Indies. He also wrote the Jaka Lodhang and the Wirid Hidayat Jati, and with C.F. Winter Sr. compiled the Bausastra Kawi.

Nancy Florida, in Writing the Past, Inscribing the Future, studied the court writing tradition of Surakarta (not Yogyakarta) and showed how chronicles and prophecies became a way of rewriting the past to shape the future. The Yogyakarta tradition, by contrast, is stronger in dynastic chronicles (Babad Giyanti), dance, and statecraft. Many Yogyakarta manuscripts were carried to Britain after the 1812 assault and are now held in the British Library.

Interpretive reading

when real power passed into colonial hands, poets moved the battlefield into language. Prophecy (jangka) is how a powerless society says: "I still own the ending of the story."

BabChapter 06

Tepian Lain: Sunda, Melayu, BugisOther Shores: Sunda, Malay, Bugis

Bangsa kepulauan berpikir dengan cara berlayar.An archipelagic people think by sailing.

Nusantara bukan hanya Jawa. Di Sunda, Bujangga Manik (akhir abad ke-15) mencatat perjalanan seorang pangeran-petapa dengan nama-nama tempat begitu rinci sehingga menjadi sumber geografi sejarah; naskahnya tersimpan di Bodleian, Oxford, sejak awal abad ke-17. Di dunia Melayu lahir Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai, Sulalatus Salatin, sufi Hamzah Fansuri, hingga Raja Ali Haji (Gurindam Dua Belas, Tuhfat al-Nafis) di abad ke-19. Di Sulawesi, I La Galigo Bugis adalah salah satu karya naratif terpanjang di dunia, dan hidup lewat pelisanan sekaligus penulisan.

Pola bersama terlihat: setiap tradisi menaruh perjalanan di tengah cerita. Bangsa kepulauan berpikir dengan cara berlayar.

Nusantara is not only Java. In Sunda, Bujangga Manik (late 15th century) records the journey of a prince-ascetic with place names so detailed that it has become a source for historical geography; its manuscript has been held in the Bodleian, Oxford, since the early 17th century. The Malay world produced the Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai, the Sulalatus Salatin, the Sufi Hamzah Fansuri, and in the 19th century Raja Ali Haji (Gurindam Dua Belas, Tuhfat al-Nafis). In Sulawesi, the Bugis I La Galigo is one of the longest narrative works in the world, and lives through both oral performance and writing.

A shared pattern shows: every tradition puts a journey at the center of the story. An archipelagic people think by sailing.

Penemuan dan PerampasanDiscovery and Plunder

Siapa yang berhak menafsirkan warisan yang tersimpan jauh dari pemiliknya?Who may interpret a heritage kept far from its owners?

Naskah kita punya dua riwayat: riwayat isi dan riwayat perpindahan. Lombok 1894, jarahan Yogyakarta 1812, koleksi Raffles, Leiden, Paris, Radya Pustaka (museum tertua di Indonesia), Perpustakaan Nasional. Sarjana kolonial menyelamatkan sekaligus mengambil; filolog pribumi seperti Poerbatjaraka lalu membaca dan mengoreksi mereka.

Pertanyaan

ketika warisan disimpan jauh dari pemiliknya, siapa yang berhak menafsirkannya? Digitalisasi kini membuka jalan ketiga: salinan tanpa pemindahan.

Our manuscripts have two histories: the history of their content and the history of their movement. Lombok 1894, the Yogyakarta plunder of 1812, the Raffles collection, Leiden, Paris, Radya Pustaka (the oldest museum in Indonesia), the National Library. Colonial scholars rescued and took in the same motion; native philologists like Poerbatjaraka then read them and corrected them.

A question

when a heritage is kept far from its owners, who has the right to interpret it? Digitization now opens a third path: copies without removal.

BabChapter 08

Sains di Balik SastraThe Science Behind Literature

Dari prosodi biner sampai pemindaian multispektral.From binary prosody to multispectral scanning.

Kakawin memakai metrum berdasarkan pola suku kata guru (berat) dan laghu (ringan), warisan ilmu prosodi India kuno (Pingala). Pola dua-nilai ini, secara struktur, adalah sistem biner. Ini bukan klaim bahwa empu menemukan komputer, tetapi bahwa cara berpikir biner, urutan, dan kombinatorik telah lama berdiam dalam puisi. Di Jawa, ilmu pranata mangsa mencatat musim tanam berdasar bintang, dan Nagarakertagama mencatat kalender, tata ruang, dan ritus dengan ketelitian arsip negara.

Dan kini sains modern kembali: pemindaian multispektral membaca lontar pudar, penanggalan karbon menguji usia naskah, analisis komputasional membandingkan variasi salinan seperti pohon silsilah genetik. Filologi modern memakai logika yang mirip biologi evolusi: mutasi salah salin sebagai penanda garis keturunan teks.

Kakawin use meters based on patterns of guru (heavy) and laghu (light) syllables, an inheritance of ancient Indian prosody (Pingala). This two-valued pattern is, structurally, a binary system. This is not a claim that the empu invented the computer, but that binary thinking, sequence, and combinatorics have long dwelt in poetry. In Java, the science of pranata mangsa recorded planting seasons by the stars, and the Nagarakertagama recorded calendar, spatial order, and ritual with the precision of a state archive.

And now modern science returns: multispectral scanning reads faded lontar, carbon dating tests the age of manuscripts, and computational analysis compares variations among copies like a genetic family tree. Modern philology uses logic much like evolutionary biology: copying errors as mutations that mark the lines of a text's descent.

Proyeksi: Masa Depan NaskahProjection: The Future of Manuscripts

Bagian spekulatif: tentang apa yang mungkin terjadi pada naskah.A speculative section on what may become of manuscripts.

Bagian ini spekulatif, berdasarkan tren yang ada, bukan fakta.

Pertama, model bahasa yang dilatih pada korpus Jawa Kuno dan aksara Kawi mungkin mempercepat transliterasi, tetapi risiko terbesarnya adalah kelancaran palsu: sistem yang terdengar yakin di tempat para ahli ragu. Kedua, tekanan iklim ke arsip daun dan kertas di iklim tropis menuntut digitalisasi lebih cepat daripada pelapukan. Ketiga, pertanyaan restitusi akan memaksa museum Eropa memilih antara memulangkan dan berbagi. Keempat, dan mungkin terpenting: generasi yang tak lagi membaca aksara Jawa akan membutuhkan penerjemah budaya, bukan hanya penerjemah bahasa.

Naskah tidak mati ketika daunnya rapuh. Naskah mati ketika tak ada lagi yang bertanya kepadanya.

This section is speculative, based on current trends, not fact.

First, language models trained on Old Javanese corpora and Kawi script may speed up transliteration, but the greatest risk is false fluency: a system that sounds sure where experts hesitate. Second, climate pressure on leaf and paper archives in the tropics demands digitization faster than decay. Third, questions of restitution will force European museums to choose between returning and sharing. Fourth, and perhaps most important: a generation that no longer reads Javanese script will need cultural translators, not only language translators.

A manuscript does not die when its leaf turns brittle. A manuscript dies when no one asks it questions anymore.

Para Penerjemah KisahTranslators of the Tale

Para sarjana yang sabar membandingkan salinan dan menimbang tiap kata.The scholars who patiently compared copies and weighed every word.

Sebuah naskah tidak berbicara sendiri. Ia menunggu seseorang yang sabar membandingkan puluhan salinan, menimbang tiap kata, lalu berkata: "begini kira-kira maksud sang empu." Berikut mereka yang paling menentukan, beserta pendapatnya.

H. Kern (1833–1917) & J.L.A. Brandes (1857–1905). Kern, sarjana Leiden, mengedit dan menerjemahkan Nagarakertagama dan menempatkan Jawa Kuno dalam keluarga bahasa Austronesia-Sanskerta. Brandes menemukan lontar Nagarakertagama di Lombok (1894) dan menerbitkan Pararaton. Warisan keduanya: fondasi edisi kritis, sekaligus cara pandang kolonial yang memosisikan naskah sebagai "objek ilmu".

N.J. Krom. Dalam Hindoe-Javaansche Geschiedenis (1926) ia merangkai prasasti, candi, dan kakawin menjadi sejarah naratif pertama. Temuan metodenya: sastra boleh dipakai sebagai sumber sejarah, asal dibaca bersama bukti arkeologis.

C.C. Berg. Pengkritik paling tajam. Menurutnya teks seperti Pararaton dan babad tidak ditulis untuk mencatat kronologi, melainkan untuk menjalankan fungsi ritual-magis: memberi legitimasi dan mengatur ulang makna masa lalu. Pendapat ini mengubah cara sarjana membaca "sejarah" Jawa, dan masih diperdebatkan.

R.M.Ng. Poerbatjaraka (1884–1964). Filolog Indonesia pertama yang menembus dominasi Belanda. Ia menulis Kapustakan Djawi, mengkaji Agastya di Nusantara, dan merintis kajian Cerita Panji. Pendapatnya bahwa Smaradahana punya hubungan dengan awal siklus Panji masih menjadi titik tolak diskusi.

P.J. Zoetmulder (1906–1995). Imam Jesuit sekaligus filolog. Kalangwan (1974) memandang kakawin sebagai jalan estetis menuju penyatuan dengan yang ilahi: pujangga adalah yogin keindahan. Kamus Jawa Kuna–Inggris (1982) adalah alat tak tergantikan hingga kini.

Th. Pigeaud. Literature of Java (3 jilid, 1967–1980) mengkatalogkan ribuan naskah, dan Java in the 14th Century menyajikan Nagarakertagama dengan terjemahan serta komentar luas.

A. Teeuw, Stuart Robson, S. Supomo. Supomo menerbitkan edisi Arjunawijaya karya Tantular (1977); Robson menyunting beberapa kakawin bersama Teeuw (terjemahan Prapanca-nya dibahas di bawah). Mereka menunjukkan bahwa kakawin bukan salinan Sanskerta, tetapi karya sastra Jawa yang sadar diri.

Stuart Robson, pembaca ulang Prapanca. Profesor studi Jawa dan Jawa Kuno asal Inggris yang lama mengajar di Monash University dan Leiden University. Karyanya Desawarnana (Nagarakrtagama) by Mpu Prapanca (KITLV, Leiden, 1995) adalah terjemahan Inggris utuh yang disusun lewat pembacaan filologis baru atas teks Jawa Kuno. Hasilnya lebih akurat dan lebih terbaca, dan memperbaiki sejumlah tafsiran para pendahulu, termasuk Pigeaud. Sumbangannya terutama pada ketelitian bahasa: tiap kata dipertimbangkan ulang, bukan sekadar diwariskan.

Nigel Bullough (Hadi Sidomulyo), pembaca di atas tanah. Penjelajah, sejarawan, dan budayawan kelahiran Inggris yang puluhan tahun menetap di Indonesia dan memakai nama Hadi Sidomulyo. Dalam Napak Tilas Perjalanan Mpu Prapanca ia menempuh langsung rute perjalanan Hayam Wuruk di Jawa Timur sebagaimana tercatat dalam kitab, mencocokkan nama desa abad ke-14 dengan lokasi modern. Metodenya berbeda dari filolog perpustakaan: teks diuji oleh medan.

Dua cara membaca: Robson bertanya "apa yang sebenarnya dikatakan kata-kata ini?", Hadi Sidomulyo bertanya "di mana tempat ini sekarang?". Keduanya saling melengkapi: terjemahan yang tepat tanpa peta berhenti sebagai bahasa, peta tanpa terjemahan yang tepat bisa tersesat. Pencocokan lokasi di lapangan tetap hipotesis yang perlu diuji arkeologi dan toponimi, karena nama desa bergeser selama tujuh abad. Satu catatan sejarah: penemu naskah fisik di Puri Cakranegara, Lombok (1894), adalah J.L.A. Brandes, filolog Belanda, bukan Inggris.

I. Kuntara Wiryamartana. Menelusuri Arjunawiwaha dari lontar ke tradisi lisan dan tembang. Argumennya: teks Jawa tidak "berhenti" di satu bentuk, tetapi terus hidup lewat transformasi.

M.C. Ricklefs, Nancy Florida, Denys Lombard. Ricklefs mengajukan "sintesis mistik" Islam-Jawa sebagai pola utama masyarakat Jawa dari abad ke-18; Florida membaca babad dan ramalan Surakarta sebagai tindakan politik lewat penulisan; Lombard (Le Carrefour javanais) memandang Jawa sebagai persimpangan peradaban, bukan pulau yang tertutup.

Pola yang tampak: generasi pertama menyelamatkan teks, generasi kedua membaca isinya, generasi ketiga bertanya siapa yang berhak membaca dan untuk apa. Pertanyaan ketiga baru saja dimulai.

A manuscript does not speak by itself. It waits for someone patient enough to compare dozens of copies, weigh each word, and then say: "this is roughly what the empu meant." Here are those who mattered most, with their views.

H. Kern (1833–1917) & J.L.A. Brandes (1857–1905). Kern, a Leiden scholar, edited and translated the Nagarakertagama and placed Old Javanese within the Austronesian-Sanskrit family. Brandes found the Nagarakertagama lontar in Lombok (1894) and published the Pararaton. Their legacy: the foundations of critical editions, and also a colonial outlook that treated manuscripts as "objects of science."

N.J. Krom. In Hindoe-Javaansche Geschiedenis (1926) he wove inscriptions, temples, and kakawin into the first narrative history. His methodological finding: literature may serve as a historical source, as long as it is read alongside archaeological evidence.

C.C. Berg. The sharpest critic. In his view, texts like the Pararaton and the chronicles were not written to record chronology but to perform a ritual-magical function: granting legitimacy and rearranging the meaning of the past. This view changed how scholars read Javanese "history," and is still debated.

R.M.Ng. Poerbatjaraka (1884–1964). The first Indonesian philologist to break through Dutch dominance. He wrote Kapustakan Djawi, studied Agastya in the archipelago, and pioneered the study of Panji tales. His view that the Smaradahana is related to the beginnings of the Panji cycle is still a starting point for discussion.

P.J. Zoetmulder (1906–1995). A Jesuit priest and philologist. Kalangwan (1974) regards kakawin as an aesthetic path to union with the divine: the poet is a yogin of beauty. His Old Javanese–English Dictionary (1982) remains an irreplaceable tool.

Th. Pigeaud. Literature of Java (3 volumes, 1967–1980) catalogued thousands of manuscripts, and Java in the 14th Century presents the Nagarakertagama with translation and extensive commentary.

A. Teeuw, Stuart Robson, S. Supomo. Supomo published an edition of Tantular's Arjunawijaya (1977); Robson edited several kakawin with Teeuw (his Prapanca translation is discussed below). They showed that kakawin are not copies of Sanskrit but self-aware Javanese literary works.

Stuart Robson, rereader of Prapanca. A British professor of Javanese and Old Javanese who taught for many years at Monash University and Leiden University. His Desawarnana (Nagarakrtagama) by Mpu Prapanca (KITLV, Leiden, 1995) is a complete English translation built on a fresh philological reading of the Old Javanese text. The result is more accurate and more readable, and corrects a number of interpretations by predecessors, including Pigeaud. His contribution lies chiefly in linguistic precision: each word is reconsidered, not simply inherited.

Nigel Bullough (Hadi Sidomulyo), reader on the ground. An English-born explorer, historian, and cultural figure who settled in Indonesia for decades and took the name Hadi Sidomulyo. In Napak Tilas Perjalanan Mpu Prapanca he walked Hayam Wuruk's route through East Java as recorded in the text, matching 14th-century village names to modern locations. His method differs from the library philologist's: the text is tested by the terrain.

Two ways of reading: Robson asks "what do these words actually say?", Hadi Sidomulyo asks "where is this place now?" They complement each other: an exact translation without a map stays language, while a map without an exact translation can get lost. Matching locations in the field remains a hypothesis to be tested by archaeology and toponymy, because village names drift over seven centuries.

I. Kuntara Wiryamartana. Traced the Arjunawiwaha from lontar to oral tradition and song. His argument: Javanese texts do not "stop" at one form, but keep living through transformation.

M.C. Ricklefs, Nancy Florida, Denys Lombard. Ricklefs proposed the Islamic-Javanese "mystic synthesis" as the main pattern of Javanese society from the 18th century; Florida read Surakarta's chronicles and prophecies as political acts carried out through writing; Lombard (Le Carrefour javanais) saw Java as a crossroads of civilizations, not a closed island.

The pattern that emerges: the first generation saved the texts, the second read their contents, the third asks who has the right to read them and for what. The third question has only just begun.

Kidung: Kisah Cinta dan Sejarah dari Majapahit Akhir

Setelah kakawin, muncul kidung: puisi Jawa Tengahan yang memakai metrum Jawa, bukan Sanskerta, sehingga lebih luwes dan dekat pada tutur sehari-hari. Ada dua kelompok besar. Yang pertama adalah kidung Panji, kisah cinta dan pengembaraan sepasang kekasih yang terpisah. Yang kedua adalah kidung sejarah, yang menuturkan Singhasari dan Majapahit hingga ke Bali. Sebagian besar disusun belakangan, pada masa Majapahit akhir atau sesudahnya, jadi tanggal dan pengarangnya banyak yang masih diperdebatkan.

Penilaian arti

dua tema berulang di seluruh daftar ini. Pertama, pengorbanan: Arjuna, Sutasoma, Rama. Kedua, kuasa yang gelisah: Bharatayuddha, Pararaton, Kalatidha. Dua tema itu tampaknya jawaban Nusantara atas pertanyaan yang sama: apa gunanya kuasa jika tak melahirkan teladan?

Kakawin Ramayana (9th century, author unknown). Follows the outline of the Indian Ramayana, and is thought to rest on the Bhattikavya, a Sanskrit poem that also teaches grammar. Rama is exiled, Sita is abducted by Ravana, Hanuman breaches Lanka, war breaks out, Sita returns. Setting: Old Mataram, when Java absorbed Sanskrit as a tool of literature and not merely the language of scripture.

Arjunawiwaha

Form
Kakawin: 36 cantos (pupuh), each in a particular Sanskrit meter.
Language
Classical Old Javanese.
Script
Old Javanese (Kawi) script; surviving copies are mostly in Balinese script on lontar.

(Kanwa, c. 1030s). Arjuna meditates on Mount Indrakila. The nymphs Supraba and Tilottama are sent to tempt him; he does not stir. Shiva tests him in the guise of a hunter, they fight over a wild boar, then Shiva reveals himself and grants the Pasupati arrow. The gods call Arjuna to destroy the demon Niwatakawaca, who threatens heaven; Supraba scouts his weak point, and Arjuna prevails. Setting: Airlangga recovering from the ruin of Medang, read by many scholars as a mirror of himself.

  • Why read it The seduction by the nymphs and the duel with the mysterious hunter are written in language both erotic and meditative. Look for verses that read two ways: as a love story and as asceticism.
  • Starting point Poerbatjaraka (1926), Wiryamartana (1990), and the Kanwa chapter in Kalangwan.
  • Further exploration How far is Arjuna a mask for Airlangga?

Bharatayuddha

Form
Kakawin: an epic poem of hundreds of verses, written by two hands (Sedah, then Panuluh).
Language
Classical Old Javanese.
Script
Old Javanese (Kawi) script; surviving copies are mostly in Balinese script on lontar.

(Sedah & Panuluh, 1157). Focuses on the war at Kurukshetra: the fall of Bhisma, Drona, Karna, up to Shalya, with Krishna as Vishnu's guide. The text names King Jayabhaya as protecting ruler. Setting: the still-fresh Kediri–Jenggala dispute; a civil-war epic becomes the court's way of contemplating its own civil war.

  • Why read it A war without pure heroes. Read the deaths of Karna and Shalya to see a poet who does not let victory feel clean.

  • Further exploration Does the opening of the text truly allegorize Kediri–Jenggala?

Hariwangsa

Form
Kakawin: an Old Javanese narrative poem in Sanskrit meters.
Language
Old Javanese (Kawi) with Sanskrit loanwords.
Script
Old Javanese (Kawi) script; surviving copies are mostly in Balinese script on lontar.

(Panuluh, written while Jayabaya ruled Kediri, 12th century). Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu, wishes to marry Rukmini of Kundina, daughter of King Bhismaka. Rukmini is an avatar of the goddess Sri. On the night before the wedding festivities, Krishna comes to Kundina and carries her off, even though guests from other lands have already arrived. Bhismaka is furious and confers with the kings present. The name hariwangsa means the lineage of Hari (Vishnu), but the poem covers only a small part of the Indian Harivamsa. Setting: two Kediri works, Hariwangsa and Kresnayana, treat the same theme; Hariwangsa is judged simpler and more successful, which has led to the suggestion that Panuluh recomposed an existing tale.

  • Why read it A bold love story: a god-king abducts the bride on the eve of the feast. A good way to read Panuluh beyond the Bharatayuddha.
  • Starting point A. Teeuw's edition (Hariwangsa, 1950); compare with Triguna's Kresnayana.
  • Further exploration In its opening, Panuluh gives his aim as seeking success in composing. Is this a practice piece, as some scholars suppose?

Kidung: Romance and History from Late Majapahit

After the kakawin came the kidung: Middle Javanese poetry in Javanese rather than Sanskrit meters, so more supple and closer to everyday speech. There are two broad groups. The first is the Panji kidung, tales of love and wandering by a pair of separated lovers. The second is the historical kidung, which narrate Singhasari and Majapahit and carry on to Bali. Most were composed later, in late Majapahit or after, so dates and authorship are often still debated.

Beyond the great works, the last glow of Surakarta (the late 18th to the late 19th century) produced many companion books: moral teaching known as sastra wulang, tales of wandering, and the writings of poets working in the shadow of colonial power. Mengapa Ramayana dan Mahabharata?Why the Ramayana and Mahabharata?

Epos bukan ditanam, melainkan diundang.The epics were not planted, but invited.

Hampir setiap kitab di atas bersandar pada dua epos India. Dari Arjuna sampai Rama, dari Gatotkaca sampai Rahwana, Nusantara seperti hanya punya dua pintu menuju cerita. Mengapa? Para sarjana memberi beberapa jawaban yang saling melengkapi.

Bagaimana epos tiba

Perdebatan lama: apakah India "menjajah" budaya Asia Tenggara (Coedès), atau para pedagang membawanya dan penguasa lokal mengambil yang berguna (van Leur, Bosch)? Pandangan yang kini paling berpengaruh, dari O.W. Wolters, adalah lokalisasi: elite setempat memilih unsur asing yang cocok dengan kerangka lokal mereka sendiri. Dengan kata lain, epos bukan ditanam, melainkan diundang. Bukti paling terang ialah kenyataan bahwa versi Jawa sering menyimpang dari sumber Sanskerta.

Lima alasan menurut para pengkaji

1. Cetak biru kerajaan. Rama adalah raja teladan; Arjuna adalah ksatria-petapa. Raja Jawa mengidentifikasi diri dengan tokoh ini. Dalam Bharatayuddha, Jayabhaya disamakan dengan Wisnu. Epos menyediakan kosakata untuk legitimasi.

2. Ensiklopedia yang bisa dipotong. Mahabharata begitu luas sehingga pujangga cukup memilih episode (Arjuna bertapa, Gatotkaca membantu Abimanyu) tanpa menjiplak keseluruhan. Zoetmulder menunjukkan bahwa kakawin adalah kreasi baru di atas kerangka lama, bukan terjemahan, dan Kakawin Ramayana pun diduga berpijak pada puisi tata bahasa Sanskerta (Bhattikavya).

3. Proyek negara. Tradisi menyebut Raja Dharmawangsa Teguh (akhir abad ke-10) memerintahkan penerjemahan Mahabharata ke bahasa Jawa Kuno. Hasilnya adalah parwa (Adiparwa, Wirataparwa, dan lain-lain), prosa Jawa Kuno yang kemudian menjadi bahan baku kakawin. Menerjemahkan epos berarti menulis ulang cakrawala bahasa kerajaan.

4. Epos yang dijawakan. W.F. Stutterheim meneliti relief Ramayana di Prambanan dan Panataran (1925), dan menemukan adaptasi lokal dalam adegan serta tafsir. Di wayang muncul Punakawan (Semar, Gareng, Petruk, Bagong), tokoh Jawa asli yang tak ada dalam epos India; Semar bahkan dipahami sebagai dewa leluhur Jawa yang menyamar sebagai abdi. Bima mencari Dewa Ruci dalam lakon yang juga tanpa padanan India. Benedict Anderson menambahkan bahwa wayang mengajarkan toleransi: Pandawa dan Kurawa tak pernah sepenuhnya hitam-putih.

5. Wadah yang bisa diisi ulang. Setelah Islam tiba, epos tidak ditinggalkan. Tradisi mengisahkan para wali memakai wayang sebagai media dakwah, dan Yasadipura I menulis Serat Menak berdampingan dengan Serat Rama. Raja Mataram bahkan menyambung silsilahnya ke Pandawa lewat Parikesit. Kemampuan epos menampung makna baru adalah rahasia umurnya yang lebih dari seribu tahun.

Penilaian arti

kedua epos bertahan karena ia menyajikan pertanyaan yang tak pernah selesai: apakah perang yang adil bisa ada? Apakah kewajiban boleh dikalahkan oleh cinta? Masyarakat yang berganti agama tiga kali tetap membutuhkan wadah untuk dilema yang sama. Epos bukan pinjaman; ia semacam cermin yang kebetulan datang dari seberang laut.

Almost every book above leans on the two Indian epics. From Arjuna to Rama, from Gatotkaca to Ravana, Nusantara seems to have only two doors into story. Why? Scholars give several answers that complement one another.

How the epics arrived

An old debate: did India "colonize" Southeast Asian culture (Coedès), or did merchants bring it and local rulers take what was useful (van Leur, Bosch)? The now most influential view, from O.W. Wolters, is localization: local elites chose foreign elements that fit their own frameworks. In other words, the epics were not planted but invited. The clearest evidence is that Javanese versions often deviate from Sanskrit sources.

Five reasons, according to scholars

1. A royal blueprint. Rama is the model king; Arjuna the warrior-ascetic. Javanese kings identified with these figures. In the Bharatayuddha, Jayabhaya is likened to Vishnu. The epics supplied a vocabulary of legitimacy.

2. An encyclopedia that can be cut. The Mahabharata is so vast that poets need only pick episodes (Arjuna in meditation, Gatotkaca helping Abhimanyu) without copying the whole. Zoetmulder showed that kakawin are new creations on an old frame, not translations, and even the Kakawin Ramayana is thought to rest on a Sanskrit grammar poem (Bhattikavya).

3. A state project. Tradition says King Dharmawangsa Teguh (late 10th century) ordered the Mahabharata translated into Old Javanese. The result was the parwa (Adiparwa, Wirataparwa, and others), Old Javanese prose that later became raw material for kakawin. To translate an epic is to rewrite the language horizon of a kingdom.

4. Epics made Javanese. W.F. Stutterheim studied the Ramayana reliefs at Prambanan and Panataran (1925) and found local adaptations in scenes and interpretation. In wayang appear the Punakawan (Semar, Gareng, Petruk, Bagong), authentically Javanese figures absent from the Indian epics; Semar is even understood as a Javanese ancestral god disguised as a servant. Bima seeks Dewa Ruci in a play with no Indian counterpart either. Benedict Anderson added that wayang teaches tolerance: the Pandavas and Kauravas are never wholly black and white.

5. A refillable vessel. After Islam arrived, the epics were not abandoned. Tradition tells that the wali used wayang as a medium of preaching, and Yasadipura I wrote the Serat Menak alongside the Serat Rama. Mataram kings even linked their genealogy to the Pandavas through Parikesit. The epics' capacity to hold new meaning is the secret of their more than thousand years of life.

Interpretive reading

the two epics endure because they pose questions that are never finished: can a just war exist? May duty be defeated by love? A society that changed religion three times still needs a vessel for the same dilemmas. The epics are not a loan; they are a kind of mirror that happened to come from across the sea.

Three patterns across the islands. (1) Genealogy as law: from Melaka to the Bugis, chronicles bind king and subject through covenants, not only praise. (2) Islam as a new language: hikayat and syair use Jawi, tie dynasties to Iskandar Zulkarnain, and move Nusantara from the kakawin system into the wider world of the Indian Ocean. (3) The sea as plot: Bujangga Manik, Sawerigading, Hamzah Fansuri, and Raja Ali Haji all move by voyage. If Javanese literature often turns on court and cosmos, the literature of the other islands often turns on harbor and treaty.

Titik Tolak bagi Pembaca dan PenelitiStarting Points for Readers and Researchers

Jalur bertahap yang bisa ditempuh siapa pun.A staged path that anyone can take.

Naskah-naskah ini tidak harus dibaca dari nol. Ada jalur bertahap yang bisa ditempuh siapa pun.

Jalur tiga langkah.
1. Mulai dari terjemahan modern dan sinopsis, supaya cerita dan nada terasa dahulu.
2. Dampingi dengan kamus Zoetmulder (Old Javanese-English Dictionary) dan pelajari dasar aksara Jawa, Bali, atau Jawi.
3. Baca edisi kritis dan lihat aparat kritiknya, tempat para filolog menimbang variasi antar-salinan.

Tempat mencari naskah. Khastara (Perpustakaan Nasional RI), Leiden University Libraries Digital Collections, British Library (koleksi Raffles), Bodleian Libraries (Bujangga Manik), dan DREAMSEA (repositori naskah Asia Tenggara yang terancam). Ketersediaan tiap koleksi sebaiknya dicek langsung.

Gagasan riset. Membandingkan versi Kakawin Ramayana dengan Serat Rama; memetakan toponim Bujangga Manik dan Nagarakertagama; menganalisis bagaimana Bubat diceritakan dalam tiga sumber; menyunting ulang karya yang jarang dibaca seperti Parthayajna; mencari suara perempuan dalam naskah.

Ajakan: sastra klasik bukan sekadar warisan, tetapi juga undangan. Setiap lontar yang belum diterjemahkan adalah bab yang belum dibaca, dan mungkin bab itu menunggu pembaca berikutnya.

These manuscripts need not be read from zero. There is a staged path anyone can take.

A three-step path.
1. Begin with modern translations and synopses, so that the story and tone are felt first.
2. Pair them with Zoetmulder's dictionary (Old Javanese-English Dictionary) and learn the basics of Javanese, Balinese, or Jawi script.
3. Read the critical editions and look at the critical apparatus, where philologists weigh variation among copies.

Where to find manuscripts. Khastara (National Library of Indonesia), Leiden University Libraries Digital Collections, the British Library (Raffles collection), the Bodleian Libraries (Bujangga Manik), and DREAMSEA (a repository of endangered Southeast Asian manuscripts). The availability of each collection should be checked directly.

Research ideas. Compare versions of the Kakawin Ramayana with the Serat Rama; map the toponyms of Bujangga Manik and the Nagarakertagama; analyze how Bubat is told in three sources; re-edit rarely read works such as the Parthayajna; look for women's voices in the manuscripts.

An invitation: classical literature is not merely a heritage but also an invitation. Every untranslated lontar is a chapter not yet read, and perhaps that chapter is waiting for its next reader.

Huruf yang Menyimpan KisahLetters That Keep the Tale

Pilihan huruf sering memberi tahu siapa pembacanya.The choice of script often tells who the readers were.

Setiap kitab di atas ditulis dengan huruf tertentu, dan pilihan huruf sering memberi tahu siapa pembacanya: pandita, istana, pesantren, atau pedagang. Berikut peta singkat aksara yang dipakai, berurutan dari yang paling tua.

Pallawa (abad ke-4/5 dan seterusnya). Aksara dari India Selatan yang tertulis pada Yupa Kutai dan prasasti awal Sriwijaya. Dari sinilah sebagian besar aksara Nusantara berasal.

Jawa Kuno (Kawi) dan Bali. Aksara turunan Pallawa untuk kakawin dan prasasti Jawa. Karena Bali menjaga tradisi lontar, sebagian besar naskah Jawa Kuno yang bertahan kini tertulis dalam aksara Bali.

Jawa (hanacaraka). Aksara suku kata untuk bahasa Jawa Baru pada dluwang dan kertas. Tradisi menjelaskan urutannya lewat legenda Aji Saka, dua puluh huruf yang dibaca sebagai kalimat tentang empat abdi yang saling membunuh karena kesetiaan. Legenda ini memberi huruf sebuah cerita asal.

Sunda Kuno, Batak, dan Sumatra Kuno (Kerinci). Aksara kerabat Pallawa untuk lontar Sunda, pustaha Batak, dan naskah Tanjung Tanah. Masing-masing berkembang di komunitasnya sendiri.

Lontara (Bugis-Makassar). Aksara suku kata untuk epos La Galigo, kronik, dan nasihat. Bentuknya yang bersudut halus membuatnya mudah dikenali.

Jawi dan Pegon. Huruf Arab yang disesuaikan untuk Melayu (Jawi) dan Jawa atau Sunda (Pegon). Bersama Islam, huruf ini membawa hikayat, syair, dan kitab pesantren; Jawi menjadi huruf utama dunia Melayu.

Latin. Dibawa percetakan kolonial dan misi, lalu menjadi huruf tulis nasional. Edisi kritis modern hampir seluruhnya memakai Latin, sehingga setiap pembaca hari ini membaca Nusantara lewat transliterasi.

Penilaian arti

pergantian aksara adalah pergantian pembaca. Dari Pallawa ke Jawi ke Latin, siapa yang boleh membaca ikut berubah, dan setiap pergantian membuat sebagian naskah lama makin sunyi. Bagi peneliti, belajar aksara berarti membuka pintu yang kini hanya dilewati segelintir orang.

Every book above was written in a particular script, and the choice of script often tells who the readers were: priests, the court, the pesantren, or merchants. Here is a short map of the scripts used, from the oldest.

Pallava (4th/5th century onward). A script from South India written on the Yupa of Kutai and the early Sriwijaya inscriptions. Most Nusantara scripts descend from it.

Old Javanese (Kawi) and Balinese. Scripts descended from Pallava for kakawin and Javanese inscriptions. Because Bali kept the lontar tradition, most surviving Old Javanese manuscripts are now written in Balinese script.

Javanese (hanacaraka). A syllabic script for New Javanese on dluwang and paper. Tradition explains its order through the legend of Aji Saka: twenty letters read as a sentence about four servants who kill one another out of loyalty. The legend gives the letters a story of origin.

Old Sundanese, Batak, and Old Sumatran (Kerinci). Scripts related to Pallava for Sundanese lontar, Batak pustaha, and the Tanjung Tanah manuscript. Each developed in its own community.

Lontara (Bugis-Makassar). A syllabic script for the La Galigo epic, chronicles, and counsel. Its softly angular form makes it easy to recognize.

Jawi and Pegon. Arabic letters adapted for Malay (Jawi) and for Javanese or Sundanese (Pegon). With Islam, these scripts carried hikayat, syair, and pesantren books; Jawi became the main script of the Malay world.

Latin. Brought by colonial printing and missions, then became the national script. Modern critical editions almost entirely use Latin, so every reader today reads Nusantara through transliteration.

Interpretive reading

a change of script is a change of readers. From Pallava to Jawi to Latin, who may read changed along with it, and each change left some old manuscripts quieter. For researchers, learning a script means opening a door that only a few now walk through.

Yang Masih Menunggu DigaliStill Waiting to Be Unearthed

Sejarah yang hilang hanya menunggu seseorang yang sabar.A lost history only waits for a patient someone.

Semua yang tertulis di atas hanyalah potongan. Sejarah Nusantara lebih mirip lantai balai yang separuh ubinnya sudah hilang: sebagian pola masih terbaca, sebagian lagi hanya bisa ditebak dari bekas yang tersisa.

Waktu bekerja pelan tetapi tak pernah berhenti. Daun lontar lapuk dimakan lembap dan serangga, kayu terbakar bersama istana, perpustakaan berpindah tangan lewat perang, dan banyak kisah tidak pernah sempat dituliskan sebelum penuturnya wafat. Di tanah tropis, bahan yang paling sering dipakai untuk menulis juga bahan yang paling cepat hancur. Karena itu, banyak episode penting hanya meninggalkan satu-dua kalimat di prasasti, sepotong nama dalam kitab lain, atau sama sekali tidak meninggalkan apa pun.

Bukti arkeologis pun terbatas. Tidak semua situs sudah digali, tidak semua temuan bisa dibaca dengan pasti, dan banyak bangunan kuno tertimbun, dijarah, atau terpotong oleh pembangunan baru. Akibatnya, ada rentang waktu dan ada wilayah yang nyaris tak bisa diceritakan, bukan karena tak pernah ada peristiwa, melainkan karena jejaknya sudah lenyap.

Justru di situlah ruang yang terbuka. Masih banyak naskah yang belum dikatalogkan, belum dibaca, atau belum diterjemahkan. Masih banyak tradisi lisan yang belum direkam, banyak situs yang menunggu penggalian, dan banyak pertanyaan yang baru bisa dijawab dengan perpaduan filologi, arkeologi, bahasa, serta teknologi baru. Setiap pembaca, mahasiswa, atau peneliti yang bersedia menekuni salah satunya ikut menyambung rangkaian yang terputus.

Sejarah yang hilang bukan berarti tak pernah ada; ia hanya sedang menunggu seseorang yang sabar untuk mencarinya.

Everything written above is only fragments. The history of Nusantara is more like the floor of a pavilion whose tiles are half gone: some patterns can still be read, others can only be guessed from the traces left behind.

Time works slowly but never stops. Lontar leaves rot from damp and insects, wood burns along with palaces, libraries change hands through war, and many stories were never written down before their tellers died. In tropical lands, the material most often used for writing is also the material that perishes fastest. For that reason, many important episodes leave only a sentence or two on an inscription, a fragment of a name in another book, or nothing at all.

Archaeological evidence is limited too. Not every site has been excavated, not every find can be read with certainty, and many ancient buildings were buried, looted, or cut through by new construction. As a result, there are stretches of time and regions that can hardly be narrated, not because nothing ever happened, but because the traces have vanished.

Precisely there lies the open space. Many manuscripts are not yet cataloged, not yet read, or not yet translated. Many oral traditions are not yet recorded, many sites await excavation, and many questions can only be answered by a combination of philology, archaeology, linguistics, and new technology. Every reader, student, or researcher willing to commit to one of them helps join a broken chain.

A lost history does not mean it never existed; it is only waiting for a patient someone to search for it.

Catatan Kaki: Kasunyan dalam Sutasoma dan ArjunawiwahaFootnote: Kasunyan in the Sutasoma and Arjunawiwaha

Satu bait yang menutup perselisihan, dan dua kitab yang menjawabnya.One verse that ends a quarrel, and two books that answer it.

Catatan kaki ini kembali ke dua kitab yang sudah singgah di bab-bab sebelumnya dan bertanya pada keduanya: apa yang mereka katakan tentang kekosongan (kasunyan), tentang pilihan, tentang maut, dan tentang pengorbanan? Para pujangga tidak memakai istilah yang kita pakai hari ini. Mereka menjawab lewat adegan, tokoh, dan satu bait yang sampai kini dikenal hampir semua orang Indonesia.

Dua pujangga menjadi pemandu. Mpu Tantular menggubah Sutasoma pada abad ke-14, di masa Rajasanagara, tentang pangeran yang menundukkan amarah agar sanggup tidak melukai. Mpu Kanwa menggubah Arjunawiwaha sekitar 1030-an, tentang pertapa yang menundukkan hasrat agar sanggup bertindak. Keduanya memberi jawaban berbeda atas persoalan yang sama.

Sutasoma dan latarnya

Sutasoma terdiri dari 148 pupuh dan sekitar 1.200 bait, dalam puluhan metrum. Kerangka ceritanya berasal dari kisah jataka Pali tentang Sutasoma, yang oleh Tantular diolah dengan unsur-unsur lain. Para peneliti membaca kitab ini bercorak Buddha Tantris, dan tokohnya, Sutasoma, dilukiskan sebagai penjelmaan Jina-Buddha Wairocana. Sebagian peneliti menambahkan bahwa kitab ini lahir di istana yang menyaksikan Siwa dan Buddha berdampingan, sehingga ia juga dibaca sebagai ikhtiar merukunkan keduanya.

Kasunyan: kosong yang bukan hampa

Kasunyan, dari kata sunya, biasa diterjemahkan sebagai kekosongan. Dalam kerangka Buddha yang melatari Sutasoma, kosong tidak berarti tiada, melainkan tiadanya hakikat yang berdiri sendiri dan tetap: segala sesuatu ada karena saling bergantung. Gagasan ini tidak diuraikan sebagai ajaran, melainkan diperlihatkan lewat tindakan seorang pangeran yang tidak menggenggam apa pun, bahkan tubuhnya sendiri.

Di sinilah kitab ini menjadi hidup. Sutasoma lahir sebagai putra mahkota, tetapi yang membuatnya Sutasoma adalah pilihan-pilihannya: menolak membunuh, meninggalkan istana, menyerahkan tubuh kepada harimau betina yang lapar. Kekosongan di sini bukan keadaan, melainkan cara bertindak: memberi tanpa menuntut hasil.

Di tepi maut

Kisah ini berulang kali mempertemukan Sutasoma dengan maut. Para pertapa memintanya membunuh Purusada, raja pemakan manusia, dan ia menolak. Raksasa berwajah gajah, Gajahwaktra, hendak memangsanya, tetapi Sutasoma membujuknya hingga raksasa itu menjadi muridnya. Pada puncaknya, Purusada menyerang, Sutasoma tidak melawan, dan ia dipersembahkan kepada Kala. Kala tak sanggup menelannya dan tak sanggup memuntahkannya, sampai ia sendiri melepaskan amarahnya.

Kala tak sanggup menelan dan tak sanggup memuntahkan: titik ketika kekerasan kehabisan jalan.

Dua baris bertanya, dua baris menjawab. Baris pertama dan kedua mengajukan persoalannya: Buddha dan Siwa disebut berbeda, dan memang berbeda, lalu bagaimana keduanya dikenali? Baris ketiga dan keempat menjawab: yang satu adalah hakikat (tatwa), bukan rupa.

Perbedaan diakui lebih dulu. Tantular tidak menyangkal bahwa dua jalan itu berlainan. Ia tidak meleburkan keduanya menjadi serupa. Kesatuannya diletakkan di tingkat yang lebih dalam, pada hakikat, sehingga perbedaan di permukaan tidak perlu dihapus untuk dapat berdamai.

Kebenaran tidak mendua. Frasa terakhir, tan hana dharma mangrwa, berarti tak ada dharma yang rangkap. Jika kebenaran ada dua, salah satunya pasti palsu. Dengan menolak kemungkinan itu, penyair menempatkan semua jalan yang tulus pada satu muara. Dalam kerangka kasunyan, nama-nama seperti Buddha dan Siwa dipahami sebagai sebutan bagi yang tak terhingga.

Dibaca bersama kisahnya. Dalam cerita, Kala adalah bentuk dahsyat Siwa dan Sutasoma adalah Buddha. Kedua pihak tidak berdamai karena berdebat, melainkan karena Sutasoma menawarkan dirinya dan Kala berhenti. Rumus kesatuan itu karenanya lahir dari welas asih yang diuji, bukan dari kesepakatan di meja perundingan.

Parartha: tindakan demi yang lain

Tiap kali Sutasoma berkorban, tujuannya adalah orang lain: harimau betina yang lapar, raja yang kehilangan jalan. Gagasan parartha, bertindak demi kebaikan makhluk lain, menjadikan pembebasan sesuatu yang dibagikan. Sutasoma tidak mencapai pencerahan dengan menyingkir, melainkan dengan hadir di tempat yang paling berbahaya.

Kanwa: pertapaan yang digoda

Mpu Kanwa mengajukan jawaban yang tampaknya berlawanan. Arjuna bertapa di Indrakila, tidak bergeming oleh rayuan para bidadari, lalu diuji Siwa yang menyamar sebagai pemburu. Hasratnya ditundukkan bukan supaya ia menjauh dari dunia, melainkan supaya ia sanggup bertindak: para dewa memanggilnya untuk membinasakan Niwatakawaca yang mengancam surga. Jika Sutasoma menundukkan amarah agar tidak melukai, Arjuna menundukkan hasrat agar sanggup berperang demi dunia. Dua jawaban itu berbeda arah, tetapi berangkat dari keyakinan yang sama: tindakan yang sejati lahir dari diri yang sudah selesai dengan keinginannya sendiri.

Penilaian arti

Bagian ini adalah pembacaan atas dua kitab, bukan satu-satunya pembacaan. Para peneliti masih berbeda pendapat tentang corak keagamaan Sutasoma, tentang tujuan penyusunannya, dan tentang cara menerjemahkan beberapa istilah kuncinya. Terjemahan bait 139.5 di atas mengikuti pemahaman umum dalam kajian Indonesia.

Pertanyaan

Jika kesatuan lahir dari pengorbanan, bukan dari kesepakatan, apa yang dapat dipelajari dari Sutasoma tentang cara menjaga perbedaan tetap hidup berdampingan?

Penutup buku: arang di atas daun

Judul buku ini kini bisa dibaca dua lapis. Arang adalah jelaga yang membuat luka pada daun terbaca; daun adalah medium yang pasti lapuk. Menulis di atas daun berarti menulis sambil tahu bahwa tulisan itu tidak abadi, dan tetap menuliskannya. Para empu tidak menunggu jaminan kekekalan. Mereka menggurat, menyalin, dan memercayakan kisahnya ke tangan penyalin berikutnya. Itulah kasunyan yang bekerja: kosong sebagai latar, goresan sebagai tindakan.

Daun akan lapuk dan arang akan pudar. Yang bertahan adalah goresan yang diteruskan dari tangan ke tangan, dan seseorang yang bersedia membacanya.

Dari Kakawin Ramayana hingga Kalatidha, dari lontar Lombok hingga rak Leiden, benang yang sama membentang: pertanyaan tentang kuasa, kematian, dan makna terus ditulis ulang di atas bahan yang rapuh. Kekosongan bukan akhir tulisan, melainkan ruang tempat tulisan berikutnya mungkin lahir.

This footnote returns to two books that already appeared in earlier chapters and asks them: what do they say about emptiness (kasunyan), choice, death, and sacrifice? The poets did not use the terms we use today. They answered through scene, character, and a single verse that nearly every Indonesian still knows.

Two poets serve as guides. Mpu Tantular composed the Sutasoma in the 14th century, in the time of Rajasanagara, about a prince who masters anger so that he can do no harm. Mpu Kanwa composed the Arjunawiwaha around the 1030s, about an ascetic who masters desire so that he can act. Each gives a different answer to the same problem.

The Sutasoma and its setting

The Sutasoma consists of 148 cantos and roughly 1,200 verses, in dozens of meters. Its story frame comes from the Pali jataka of Sutasoma, which Tantular reworked with other elements. Scholars read the book as Tantric Buddhist in tone, and its hero, Sutasoma, is portrayed as an incarnation of the Jina-Buddha Vairocana. Some scholars add that the book was born in a court that saw Shiva and Buddha side by side, so it is also read as an effort to reconcile the two.

Kasunyan: emptiness that is not void

Kasunyan, from the word sunya, is usually translated as emptiness. In the Buddhist frame behind the Sutasoma, empty does not mean nothing, but the absence of an essence that stands alone and unchanging: everything exists through dependence on everything else. The idea is not set out as a doctrine, but shown through the actions of a prince who clutches nothing, not even his own body.

Here the book comes alive. Sutasoma is born a crown prince, but what makes him Sutasoma is his choices: refusing to kill, leaving the palace, giving his body to a hungry tigress. Emptiness here is not a state but a way of acting: giving without demanding a result.

At the edge of death

The story brings Sutasoma face to face with death again and again. The ascetics ask him to kill Purusada, the man-eating king, and he refuses. The elephant-faced giant Gajahwaktra tries to devour him, but Sutasoma persuades him until the giant becomes his disciple. At the climax, Purusada attacks, Sutasoma does not resist, and he is offered to Kala. Kala can neither swallow him nor spit him out, until Kala himself lets go of his rage.

Kala can neither swallow nor spit out: the point at which violence runs out of road.

Verse 139.5: text, translation, and meaning

Near the end of the book (canto 139 of 148), after Kala and Sutasoma are reconciled, comes the verse that later became the national motto. It is not a decoration on a cover, but the close of a quarrel built up across the whole story. Here is its Kawi text in full, with translation. Spelling and word division may differ slightly between editions.

Kawi text · Sutasoma 139.5

Rwāneka dhātu winuwus Buddha Wiśwa,
Bhinnêki rakwa ring apan kena parwanosen,
Mangka ng Jinatwa kalawan Śiwatatwa tunggal,
Bhinnêka tunggal ika tan hana dharma mangrwa.

Translation

It is said that Buddha and Shiva are two different substances.
They are indeed different, yet how can they be recognized?
For the truth of the Jina (Buddha) and the truth of Shiva is one.
Different, yet one: there is no truth that is twofold.

Two lines ask, two lines answer. The first and second lines pose the problem: Buddha and Shiva are called different, and they are indeed different, so how are they recognized? The third and fourth lines answer: what is one is the essence (tatwa), not the outward form.

Difference is acknowledged first. Tantular does not deny that the two paths are distinct. He does not melt them into sameness. Their unity is placed at a deeper level, in essence, so that surface difference need not be erased in order to make peace.

Truth is not twofold. The final phrase, tan hana dharma mangrwa, means there is no dharma that is double. If truth were two, one of them would have to be false. By refusing that possibility, the poet places every sincere path at one destination. In the frame of kasunyan, names such as Buddha and Shiva are understood as labels for what cannot be bounded.

Read with the story. In the tale, Kala is the fearsome form of Shiva and Sutasoma is the Buddha. The two sides do not reconcile by arguing, but because Sutasoma offers himself and Kala stops. The formula of unity is therefore born of compassion put to the test, not of agreement at a negotiating table.

Parartha: acting for others

Each time Sutasoma sacrifices himself, the aim is someone else: a hungry tigress, a king who has lost his way. The idea of parartha, acting for the good of other beings, makes liberation something that is shared. Sutasoma does not reach enlightenment by withdrawing, but by being present in the most dangerous place.

Kanwa: asceticism under temptation

Mpu Kanwa offers an answer that seems opposite. Arjuna meditates on Indrakila, unmoved by the nymphs' seduction, and is then tested by Shiva disguised as a hunter. His desire is mastered not so that he may withdraw from the world, but so that he can act: the gods summon him to destroy Niwatakawaca, who threatens heaven. If Sutasoma masters anger so as to do no harm, Arjuna masters desire so as to fight for the world. The two answers point in different directions, yet rest on the same conviction: true action comes from a self that has finished with its own wanting.

Interpretive reading

This section is one reading of two books, not the only reading. Scholars still differ on the religious character of the Sutasoma, on the purpose of its composition, and on how to translate some of its key terms. The translation of verse 139.5 above follows the common understanding in Indonesian scholarship.

A question

If unity is born of sacrifice rather than agreement, what can the Sutasoma teach about keeping differences alive side by side?

Closing the book: charcoal on the leaf

The title of this book can now be read in two layers. Charcoal is the soot that makes the wound in the leaf legible; the leaf is a medium certain to decay. To write on a leaf is to write while knowing the writing will not last, and to write it anyway. The empu did not wait for a guarantee of permanence. They incised, copied, and entrusted the tale to the next copyist's hand. That is kasunyan at work: emptiness as the ground, the stroke as the act.

The leaf will rot and the charcoal will fade. What endures is the stroke passed from hand to hand, and someone willing to read it.

From the Kakawin Ramayana to the Kalatidha, from the Lombok lontar to the Leiden shelf, the same thread runs through: questions about power, death, and meaning keep being rewritten on fragile material. Emptiness is not the end of the writing, but the space where the next writing may be born.
