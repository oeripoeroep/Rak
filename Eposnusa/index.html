PROLOG: BUKAN SEKADAR SEJARAHPROLOGUE: MORE THAN HISTORY

Nusantara tidak lahir sebagai sebuah negara. Ia lahir sebagai ruang pertemuan: antara gunung dan laut, sawah dan pelabuhan, pedalaman dan pesisir, dunia leluhur dan dunia dagang, kerajaan dan komunitas desa. Karena itu, sejarahnya tidak bergerak seperti garis lurus menuju Indonesia. Ia bergerak sebagai rangkaian pergeseran pusat gravitasi.

The Nusantara was not born as a nation-state. It was born as a meeting place: between mountain and sea, rice field and harbour, hinterland and coast, the world of ancestors and the world of trade, kingdom and village community. Its history therefore does not move in a straight line toward "Indonesia." It moves as a series of shifting centers of gravity.

Di satu zaman, pusat kekuasaan berada di lembah sungai dan sawah. Di zaman lain, ia berpindah ke pelabuhan yang menghadap jalur perdagangan internasional. Kemudian ia kembali ke pedalaman, sebelum akhirnya kekuatan Eropa membangun jaringan kekuasaan yang menghubungkan perkebunan, pelabuhan, kantor dagang, rel kereta api, dan pasar dunia.

In one age, the center of power sat in river valleys and rice fields. In another, it moved to harbours facing the international trade routes. Then it retreated back inland, before European powers finally built a network of power linking plantations, ports, trading houses, railways, and world markets.

Inilah tesis utama Epos Nusantara: yang berubah bukan hanya siapa yang menjadi raja, tetapi cara kekuasaan memperoleh makanan, uang, legitimasi, tenaga manusia, dan informasi.

This is the central thesis of Epos Gelombang Nusantara: what changes is not merely who becomes king, but the way power acquires food, money, legitimacy, human labour, and information.

Candi, prasasti, babad, keris, pelabuhan, rempah, pajak, benteng, kereta api, sekolah, dan koran adalah bagian dari satu sistem sejarah yang sama. Mereka adalah teknologi kekuasaan dalam bentuk yang berbeda.

Temples, inscriptions, court chronicles, keris, harbours, spices, taxes, forts, railways, schools, and newspapers all belong to one and the same system of history. They are technologies of power in different forms.

Buku ini karena itu membaca sejarah dalam tiga lapisan sekaligus:

This book therefore reads history in three layers at once:

1. Lapisan peristiwa — apa yang terjadi dan siapa yang bertindak.

1. The layer of events — what happened and who acted.

2. Lapisan struktur — ekonomi, ekologi, teknologi, jaringan dagang, dan institusi yang membuat tindakan itu mungkin.

2. The layer of structure — the economy, ecology, technology, trade networks, and institutions that made such action possible.

3. Lapisan makna — mitos, agama, ramalan, kehormatan, ingatan kolektif, dan gagasan tentang legitimasi.

3. The layer of meaning — myth, religion, prophecy, honour, collective memory, and ideas of legitimacy.

Pembaca akan menemukan bahwa sebuah cerita dapat benar secara kultural tanpa harus diperlakukan sebagai fakta literal. Babad, Pararaton, prasasti, laporan Portugis, arsip VOC, dan kajian modern memiliki fungsi yang berbeda. Karena itu, ketika sumber bertentangan, perbedaan tersebut tidak selalu harus dihapus; justru di situlah sejarah menjadi menarik.

Readers will find that a story can be culturally true without being treated as literal fact. The Babad, the Pararaton, inscriptions, Portuguese reports, VOC archives, and modern scholarship each serve a different function. So when sources contradict one another, that difference need not always be erased — it is often precisely where history becomes interesting.

Epos bukan berarti semua kisah harus dianggap benar secara harfiah. Epos berarti manusia mengingat sejarah melalui tokoh, konflik, simbol, dan makna. Epic does not mean every tale must be taken as literally true. Epic means that people remember history through figures, conflict, symbol, and meaning.

Dari Sanjaya sampai Diponegoro, dari pelabuhan rempah sampai rel kereta api, benang merahnya adalah satu pertanyaan: bagaimana manusia Nusantara berusaha mengendalikan ruang, waktu, sumber daya, dan nasibnya sendiri?

From Sanjaya to Diponegoro, from spice harbours to railway lines, the common thread is one question: how have the peoples of the Nusantara sought to control space, time, resources, and their own fate?

Untuk menangkap skala pertanyaan itu, buku ini juga akan sesekali menoleh keluar jendela Nusantara. Di setiap simpul penting cerita, pembaca akan menemukan boks kecil bertajuk “Cermin Dunia” — jendela singkat ke peristiwa yang tengah berlangsung di belahan bumi lain pada saat yang (kurang lebih) bersamaan. Tujuannya bukan untuk mengecilkan Nusantara di hadapan dunia, melainkan sebaliknya: memperlihatkan bahwa apa yang terjadi di lembah Progo, di pelabuhan Tuban, atau di alun-alun Kartasura, terjadi dalam satu napas zaman yang sama dengan kejatuhan Konstantinopel, gemuruh Revolusi Prancis, atau lonceng-lonceng katedral Eropa. Nusantara bukan panggung pinggiran sejarah dunia; ia adalah salah satu ruangnya yang paling ramai.

To grasp the scale of that question, this book will also occasionally glance out of the Nusantara's window. At every important node of the story, readers will find a small box titled "World Mirror" — a brief window onto events unfolding elsewhere on the globe at roughly the same time. The aim is not to diminish the Nusantara before the world, but the opposite: to show that what happened in the Progo valley, at the port of Tuban, or in the square of Kartasura, occurred in the same breath of an age as the fall of Constantinople, the rumble of the French Revolution, or the bells of European cathedrals. The Nusantara is not a sideshow of world history; it is one of its busiest rooms.

CATATAN METODOLOGIS: MEMBACA ANTARA MITOS DAN ARSIPA METHODOLOGICAL NOTE: READING BETWEEN MYTH AND ARCHIVE

Naskah ini diposisikan sebagai sejarah naratif-interpretatif, bukan kronik akademik murni. Beberapa kisah berasal dari tradisi babad atau sumber sastra yang ditulis jauh setelah peristiwa yang diceritakan. Karena itu, istilah seperti konon, dalam tradisi, atau menurut babad sengaja dipertahankan ketika status faktualnya tidak setara dengan bukti epigrafi.

This text is positioned as interpretive narrative history, not a purely academic chronicle. Some stories come from the babad tradition or literary sources written long after the events they describe. Terms such as it is said, in tradition, or according to the babad are therefore deliberately retained wherever the factual status of a claim does not match epigraphic evidence.

Prinsip penyuntingan versi ini:Editorial principles of this edition:

  • Prasasti dan dokumen sezamanInscriptions and contemporaneous documents diperlakukan sebagai bukti primer, tetapi tetap dibaca kritis karena setiap dokumen dibuat dari sudut pandang tertentu.are treated as primary evidence, yet still read critically, since every document is produced from a particular point of view.
  • Babad dan kronikBabad and chronicles dibaca sebagai sumber sejarah sekaligus artefak politik: ia menunjukkan bagaimana suatu masyarakat ingin mengingat masa lalunya.are read as historical sources and as political artefacts at once: they show how a society wished to remember its past.
  • LegendaLegends tidak dibuang hanya karena tidak dapat diverifikasi secara literal. Nilainya sering terletak pada fungsi sosial dan politiknya.are not discarded merely because they cannot be verified literally. Their value often lies in their social and political function.
  • Angka korban, tanggal, atribusi dokumen, dan hubungan genealogisCasualty figures, dates, document attributions, and genealogical claims yang masih diperdebatkan harus diperlakukan sebagai bagian yang memerlukan fact-check akademik sebelum edisi final.still under debate must be treated as requiring academic fact-checking before any final edition.
  • Narasi dramatis digunakan untuk menghidupkan pengalaman manusia, tetapi tidak dimaksudkan untuk menyamarkan batas antara fakta, interpretasi, dan tradisi.Dramatic narration is used to bring human experience to life, but is not meant to blur the line between fact, interpretation, and tradition.

Dengan pendekatan ini, Epos Nusantara tidak hanya bertanya “apa yang terjadi?”, tetapi juga “mengapa kisah ini diwariskan dengan cara seperti itu?”

With this approach, Epos Gelombang Nusantara asks not only "what happened?", but also "why was this story passed down in this particular way?"

KERANGKA BESAR: DARI KOSMOLOGI KE NEGARA MODERNTHE BIG FRAME: FROM COSMOLOGY TO THE MODERN STATE

Jika seluruh perjalanan buku dibaca sebagai satu sistem, tampak lima transformasi besar. Pertama, tanah menjadi sumber legitimasi dan surplus. Kedua, pelabuhan mengubah Nusantara menjadi simpul perdagangan dunia. Ketiga, senjata dan organisasi memperluas skala kekuasaan. Keempat, monopoli dan administrasi kolonial mengubah jaringan dagang menjadi mesin ekstraksi. Kelima, pendidikan, percetakan, dan birokrasi modern justru menyediakan infrastruktur mental bagi lahirnya nasionalisme.

Read as a single system, the whole journey of this book reveals five great transformations. First, land becomes a source of legitimacy and surplus. Second, the harbour turns the Nusantara into a node of world trade. Third, weapons and organisation expand the scale of power. Fourth, monopoly and colonial administration turn trade networks into machines of extraction. Fifth, education, printing, and modern bureaucracy ironically supply the mental infrastructure for the birth of nationalism.

Dengan demikian, kolonialisme bukan sekadar kisah bangsa asing datang lalu menaklukkan Nusantara. Ia adalah perubahan arsitektur sistem: dari kerajaan yang menguasai manusia dan wilayah secara bertingkat, menuju negara kolonial yang menguasai arus komoditas, pajak, tenaga kerja, informasi, dan hukum dalam skala yang jauh lebih terstandardisasi.

Colonialism, then, is not simply a story of a foreign nation arriving and conquering the Nusantara. It is a change in the architecture of the system: from kingdoms that controlled people and territory in graduated layers, to a colonial state that controlled the flow of commodities, taxes, labour, information, and law on a far more standardised scale.

Ironinya, mesin yang dibangun untuk mengeksploitasi Hindia kemudian ikut menciptakan kelas terdidik, jaringan transportasi, ruang publik, dan kesadaran politik yang memungkinkan penduduk koloni membayangkan dirinya sebagai satu bangsa.

Ironically, the very machine built to exploit the Indies went on to create an educated class, a transport network, a public sphere, and a political consciousness that allowed the colony's inhabitants to imagine themselves as a single nation.

Gunung, Gunung Api, dan Prasasti TembagaMountains, Volcanoes, and the Copper Inscription

Pada mulanya, Jawa adalah sebilah papan kayu yang mengapung liar di atas Samudra Aloka. Tanah ini berguncang tanpa henti, diombang-ambingkan oleh gelombang pasang dan kemurkaan api dari perut bumi. In the beginning, Java was a plank of wood floating wildly upon the Ocean of Aloka. The land shook without end, tossed by tidal waves and the fury of fire from the earth's belly. In the mythology later carved into the collective memory of the interior peoples, the gods descended from Mount Mahameru bearing a giant nail. They drove it into the island's centre — raising mountains as cosmic anchors. From the volcanic ash spewed by those fire-giants, the land was reborn: rich, black, and immensely fertile.

I. Drama & Intrik Istana KunoI. Drama and Intrigue of the Ancient Court

Di balik bentangan tanah subur di Lembah Progo, berhembus intrik berdarah antara takhta dan pengkhianatan. Before Sanjaya raised his lingga at Gunung Wukir temple, there stood King Sanna — Sanjaya's uncle and protector. In the Carita Parahyangan manuscript, Sanna was deposed by a creeping coup led by Purbasora, his own kinsman who ruled Galuh. The young Sanjaya, carrying the wound of his uncle's defeat, quietly built up strength in the agrarian interior. The first civil war in Javanese history then broke out: Sanjaya stormed Galuh, avenged Sanna, and reunited the throne under the banner of Shiva.

Secara epigrafi, pembentukan legitimasi ini tertuang dalam Prasasti Canggal (732 M). Epigraphically, this act of legitimation is recorded in the Canggal Inscription (732 CE). Written in Pallava script and Sanskrit, it records that Sanjaya raised a lingga upon the hill as a symbol of cosmic victory over chaos, proclaiming himself ruler of Yawadwipa, an island rich in rice and gold.

Struktur ekonomi-politik kerajaan kuno ini bertumpu pada penetapan status Sima (tanah swatantra yang dibebaskan dari pajak kerajaan untuk pemeliharaan bangunan suci dan irigasi). Sejarawan B.J.O. Surplus beras pedalaman digunakan untuk memberi makan ribuan tenaga kerja yang memahat batu Andesit, sementara sistem keagamaan memberikan stempel kekal bahwa raja adalah perwujudan dewa di bumi.

The political economy of this ancient kingdom rested on the institution of Sima status — self-governing land exempted from royal tax in exchange for maintaining sacred buildings and irrigation. Historian B.J.O. Schrieke underlined that the strength of Ancient Mataram lay in its river ecology, the Progo and Elo watersheds. Surplus rice from the interior fed the thousands of labourers who carved andesite stone, while the religious system stamped the king, eternally, as a god made manifest on earth.

In almost the same year that Sanjaya raised his victory lingga at Gunung Wukir, Frankish forces under Charles Martel halted the advance of the Umayyad Caliphate at the Battle of Tours in central France — an event later regarded by European historians as the northern limit of Islamic conquest in the West. At the world's eastern edge, Tang-dynasty China was enjoying the height of its cosmopolitanism under Emperor Xuanzong, with the capital Chang'an the largest city on earth. These three civilisations — Java, Frankish Europe, and Tang China — knew nothing of one another, yet were all, at the same time, rearranging who had the right to call themselves the centre of the world.

Lembah Kedu dan Prambanan kemudian berkembang menjadi lanskap arsitektural paling spektakuler di Asia Tenggara. Dua dinasti—Sanjaya yang Siwaistik dan Syailendra yang menganut Buddha Mahayana—tidak bertempur dalam perang pemusnahan, melainkan berinteraksi dalam simbiotis-politik yang rumit. The Kedu Valley and Prambanan later grew into the most spectacular architectural landscape in Southeast Asia. Two dynasties — the Shivaite Sanjaya and the Mahayana Buddhist Shailendra — did not fight a war of annihilation, but engaged in an intricate political symbiosis. The peak of this achievement was carved in stone: Borobudur as a Mahayana cosmic mandala, and the Prambanan complex as a Shivaite macrocosm.

I. Perang Saudara Sang Pangeran BuanganI. The Civil War of the Exiled Prince

Pernikahan politik antara Rakai Pikatan (Sanjaya) dan Pramodhawardhani (Syailendra) dimaksudkan untuk mengakhiri persaingan dua mazhab besar. Namun, persekutuan ini memicu api cemburu di dalam tubuh Dinasti Syailendra sendiri. The political marriage of Rakai Pikatan (Sanjaya) and Pramodhawardhani (Shailendra) was meant to end the rivalry of the two great schools. Yet the alliance lit a fire of jealousy within the Shailendra dynasty itself. Balaputradewa, Pramodhawardhani's younger brother, refused to accept his family's submission to the Shivaite Rakai Pikatan.

Pertempuran sengit meletus di perbukitan Ratu Boko (Prasasti Wantil/Siwagrha, 856 M). Rakai Pikatan membangun benteng-benteng batu sementara Balaputradewa bertahan sengit. Setelah menderita kekalahan tragis, Balaputradewa terusir dari tanah kelahirannya dan melarikan diri ke Sumatra, di mana ia menaiki takhta Srivijaya. Fierce fighting broke out on the hills of Ratu Boko (Wantil/Siwagrha Inscription, 856 CE). Rakai Pikatan built stone fortifications while Balaputradewa fought on desperately. After a tragic defeat, Balaputradewa was driven from his homeland and fled to Sumatra, where he ascended the throne of Srivijaya. The bitter grudge of this exiled prince would fuel inter-island hostility for years to come.

II. Bencana dan Malapetaka MerapiII. Catastrophe and the Wrath of Merapi

Kejayaan arsitektural di Jawa Tengah runtuh secara mendadak pada awal abad ke-10. Analisis geologis dan historiografi (karya H. Mpu Sindok terpaksa memboyong pusat kerajaan ke lembah Sungai Brantas, Jawa Timur, pada tahun 929 M.

Central Java's architectural glory collapsed suddenly in the early tenth century. Geological and historiographical analysis (by H. Kern and R.W. van Bemmelen) suggests that a violent eruption of Mount Merapi — accompanied by earthquakes and volcanic ash that buried the temples — destroyed the rice-field irrigation systems and triggered massive economic decline. Mpu Sindok was forced to move the kingdom's centre to the Brantas River valley in East Java in 929 CE.

Dalam tradisi Babad Tanah Jawi, keruntuhan dan perpindahan ini dikenang sebagai terbelahnya takhta dan kutukan, sebagaimana terwujud dalam alegori dongeng Bandung Bandawasa dan Rara Jonggrang—kegagalan membangun seribu candi dalam semalam yang berujung pada pengutukan menjadi batu.

In the Babad Tanah Jawi tradition, this collapse and relocation is remembered as a sundered throne and a curse, embodied in the folk allegory of Bandung Bandawasa and Rara Jonggrang — the failure to build a thousand temples in one night, ending in a curse turned to stone.

Ketika para pemahat Syailendra dan Sanjaya sedang menyusun jutaan blok batu Borobudur dan Prambanan, di Eropa Barat, Paus Leo III menobatkan Charlemagne sebagai Kaisar Romawi Suci pada malam Natal tahun 800 M di Roma — usaha menyatukan kembali kekaisaran yang runtuh tiga abad sebelumnya. As the Shailendra and Sanjaya masons were laying millions of stone blocks for Borobudur and Prambanan, in Western Europe, Pope Leo III crowned Charlemagne Holy Roman Emperor on Christmas night of the year 800 in Rome — an attempt to reunite an empire that had collapsed three centuries earlier. Both Java and Europe, in the same age, were raising giant stone monuments as physical proof that their power was blessed by heaven. The difference is that while Borobudur and Prambanan stand side by side as a symbol of reconciliation between two faiths, Charlemagne's crown instead became the seed of centuries of conflict between Church and throne in Europe.

Raungan Sang Jung Jawa dan Eksodus ke MadagaskarThe Roar of the Javanese Jung and the Exodus to Madagascar

Ketika Balaputradewa melarikan diri dari reruntuhan kekalahannya di Ratu Boko dan menaiki takhta Sriwijaya, ia sesungguhnya tidak sedang mengungsi ke sebuah kerajaan kecil di pinggiran. Ia sedang mewarisi salah satu kekuatan maritim paling disegani di muka bumi pada masanya—sebuah imperium yang, jauh sebelum nama Sanjaya diukir di prasasti tembaga, telah menguasai lautan dari Selat Malaka hingga pesisir Afrika Timur. Untuk memahami betapa besar warisan yang jatuh ke tangan sang pangeran buangan itu, kita perlu menoleh mundur beberapa abad, ke masa ketika kapal-kapal Nusantara adalah raksasa yang paling ditakuti—dan paling dipercaya—di sepanjang Samudra Hindia.

When Balaputradewa fled the ruins of his defeat at Ratu Boko and ascended the throne of Srivijaya, he was not fleeing into some small, peripheral kingdom. He was inheriting one of the most respected maritime powers on earth in its day — an empire that, long before Sanjaya's name was carved into any copper inscription, already ruled the seas from the Strait of Malacca to the coast of East Africa. To grasp how vast an inheritance fell into this exiled prince's hands, we must look back several centuries, to a time when the ships of the Nusantara were the most feared — and most trusted — giants along the whole of the Indian Ocean.

I. Sutra, Rempah, dan Jalan Darat yang Mulai SesakI. Silk, Spice, and an Overland Road Growing Crowded

Sejak abad ke-3 Masehi, dunia lama telah terhubung oleh Jalur Sutra—rute darat sepanjang lebih dari 6.400 kilometer yang membentang dari Chang'an di jantung Tiongkok hingga pelabuhan-pelabuhan Mediterania. Namun jalur ini bukan satu jalan tunggal yang dilalui satu kafilah dari ujung ke ujung; ia adalah rantai estafet yang disambung oleh perantara demi perantara—pedagang Parthia di Persia, pedagang Sogdiana di Asia Tengah, pedagang Arab di jazirahnya sendiri—masing-masing menyimpan rahasia dagang mereka rapat-rapat. Tiongkok merahasiakan teknik pemintalan sutra selama berabad-abad; dunia Islam menyimpan rahasia pembuatan kertas (papier-mâché) yang dicuri dari tawanan Tiongkok pasca-Pertempuran Talas (751 M). Di sepanjang jalur inilah agama-agama menyebar berdampingan dengan barang dagangan—Buddhisme merambat dari India ke Asia Tengah dan bertaut dengan seni Yunani-Baktria, sementara pada abad ke-14 jalur yang sama juga menjadi lorong bagi Black Death menular dari padang stepa Asia hingga pelabuhan-pelabuhan Eropa.

Since the 3rd century CE, the old world had been linked by the Silk Road — an overland route of more than 6,400 kilometres stretching from Chang'an in the heart of China to the ports of the Mediterranean. But this was never a single road walked end-to-end by one caravan; it was a relay chain passed from intermediary to intermediary — Parthian merchants in Persia, Sogdian merchants in Central Asia, Arab merchants on their own peninsula — each guarding their trade secrets closely. China kept the technique of silk spinning secret for centuries; the Islamic world held onto the secret of papermaking, stolen from Chinese prisoners after the Battle of Talas (751 CE). Along this same road, religions spread alongside merchandise — Buddhism travelled from India into Central Asia and fused with Greco-Bactrian art, while in the 14th century the very same route also became the corridor through which the Black Death spread from the Asian steppe to the ports of Europe.

Tetapi jalur darat ini memiliki kelemahan fatal: ia bergantung pada stabilitas politik sepanjang ribuan kilometer yang dilintasinya. Titik baliknya tiba pada 1453 M, ketika Konstantinopel jatuh ke tangan Kesultanan Utsmaniyah. Blokade cukai yang diberlakukan Utsmaniyah atas barang-barang dari timur memaksa para pedagang Eropa mencari "pintu belakang"—jalur laut langsung menuju sumber rempah, tanpa perlu membayar pajak berlapis-lapis kepada perantara mana pun. Pencarian pintu belakang inilah yang, satu abad kemudian, akan mengantarkan kapal-kapal Portugis ke Malaka dan Banda—sebagaimana telah dikisahkan di bab-bab berikutnya epos ini.

But this overland route had a fatal weakness: it depended on political stability across the thousands of kilometres it crossed. The turning point came in 1453, when Constantinople fell to the Ottoman Sultanate. The customs blockade the Ottomans imposed on goods from the East forced European merchants to search for a "back door" — a direct sea route to the source of the spices, without paying layered taxes to any middleman. It was this search for a back door that, a century later, would carry Portuguese ships to Malacca and Banda — as told in later chapters of this epic.

II. Raksasa Samudra dari NusantaraII. Ocean Giants from the Nusantara

Namun jauh sebelum Eropa mencari jalan pintas ke timur, Nusantara telah memiliki jalan tolnya sendiri di lautan—dan kapal-kapalnya sendiri yang berlayar di atasnya. Relief batu di dinding Candi Borobudur, dipahat sekitar abad ke-8 M, mengabadikan gambar kapal bercadik ganda (dikenal sebagai Kolandiaphonta) lengkap dengan layar tanja dan penopang keseimbangan di kedua sisi lambung—bukti visual paling nyata bahwa peradaban Jawa kuno adalah bangsa pelaut, bukan sekadar petani sawah pedalaman sebagaimana sering digambarkan historiografi kolonial.

Yet long before Europe sought a shortcut east, the Nusantara already had its own toll road at sea — and its own ships sailing upon it. Stone reliefs on the walls of Borobudur, carved around the 8th century CE, preserve images of double-outrigger ships (known as Kolandiaphonta) complete with tanja sails and balancing floats on both sides of the hull — the clearest visual proof that ancient Javanese civilisation was a seafaring people, not merely inland rice farmers as colonial historiography so often portrayed them.

Kesaksian tertulis tertua justru datang dari seorang diplomat Tiongkok bernama Wan Chen, yang pada abad ke-3 M menulis naskah Nan-chou I-wu Chih (Catatan Aneka Benda dari Wilayah Selatan). Ia mendeskripsikan kapal-kapal raksasa yang disebutnya K'un-lun po—jung-jung samudra berbobot hingga 1.000 ton, dibangun tanpa satu paku besi pun, melainkan disambung dengan pasak kayu dan diikat tali ijuk berlapis, mampu mengangkut ratusan awak dan muatan melintasi lautan lepas. Kapal-kapal inilah yang menjadikan Nusantara, jauh sebelum istilah itu diciptakan, sebagai "penyedia truk samudra" bagi perdagangan Asia kuno: mengangkut lada dari Sumatra, cengkih dari Maluku, dan porselen dari Tiongkok, menjalin satu jaringan logistik yang menghubungkan India, Tiongkok, dan dunia Arab jauh sebelum satu pun kapal Eropa menyeberangi Tanjung Harapan.

The oldest written testimony comes from a Chinese diplomat named Wan Chen, who in the 3rd century CE wrote the Nan-chou I-wu Chih (Records of Strange Things from the Southern Regions). He described giant ships he called K'un-lun po — ocean-going jung weighing up to 1,000 tons, built without a single iron nail, joined instead with wooden dowels and bound with layered fibre rope, capable of carrying hundreds of crew and cargo across the open sea. It was these ships that made the Nusantara, long before the term existed, the "ocean trucking service" of ancient Asian trade: carrying pepper from Sumatra, cloves from Maluku, and porcelain from China, weaving a single logistics network linking India, China, and the Arab world long before a single European ship rounded the Cape of Good Hope.

III. Ke Barat, Menyeberangi Samudra HindiaIII. Westward, Across the Indian Ocean

Kapal-kapal sebesar itu, dengan pelaut sepiawai itu, pada akhirnya membawa sekelompok kecil manusia Nusantara ke tujuan yang nyaris tak terbayangkan: menyeberangi seluruh lebar Samudra Hindia hingga tiba di sebuah pulau raksasa di lepas pantai timur Afrika—Madagaskar. Bukti bagi perjalanan luar biasa ini tersimpan bukan dalam prasasti batu, melainkan dalam darah dan lidah manusia yang hidup hari ini: Bahasa Malagasy, bahasa ibu penduduk Madagaskar, memiliki kemiripan hingga 90 persen dengan Bahasa Ma'anyan—sebuah bahasa yang dituturkan komunitas kecil di pedalaman Kalimantan Selatan, di sepanjang Sungai Barito. Bukti genetik memperkuatnya: penanda mtDNA yang disebut Polynesian Motif (B4a1a1a) ditemukan pada populasi Malagasy, menautkan garis keturunan mereka langsung ke Kepulauan Nusantara, bukan ke Afrika daratan yang justru jauh lebih dekat secara geografis.

Ships that vast, crewed by sailors that skilled, eventually carried a small band of Nusantara people to a destination that beggars belief: crossing the entire breadth of the Indian Ocean to reach a giant island off the coast of East Africa — Madagascar. Evidence of this extraordinary journey survives not in any stone inscription, but in the blood and tongue of people living today: the Malagasy language, mother tongue of Madagascar's people, shares up to 90 percent similarity with Ma'anyan — a language spoken by a small community in the interior of South Kalimantan, along the Barito River. Genetic evidence reinforces it: the mtDNA marker known as the Polynesian Motif (B4a1a1a) is found in the Malagasy population, linking their lineage directly to the Nusantara islands rather than to mainland Africa, which is, geographically, far closer.

Pertanyaan yang paling menggelitik para peneliti bukanlah apakah migrasi ini terjadi—buktinya sudah terlalu kuat untuk disangkal—melainkan mengapa para pelaut itu berasal dari pedalaman sungai Kalimantan yang relatif terpencil, dan bukan dari Jawa atau Bali yang jauh lebih maju secara politik dan populasinya jauh lebih padat. Jawabannya kemungkinan justru terletak pada kepadatan itu sendiri: lahan agraris Jawa dan Bali yang subur menciptakan struktur politik yang jenuh dan terikat pada tanah, sementara komunitas Barito, yang hidup di pinggiran jaringan dagang sungai, memiliki mobilitas lebih tinggi dan lebih sedikit terikat pada kerajaan agraris manapun. Selain itu, sebagian ahli menduga migrasi ini tidak berlangsung secara spontan dan mandiri, melainkan terjadi di bawah naungan—atau setidaknya di sepanjang jaringan pelayaran—Hegemoni Sriwijaya, yang jaring pengaruh dagangnya telah membentang sedemikian luas hingga mampu menghubungkan pedalaman sungai Kalimantan dengan rute pelayaran lintas samudra.

The question that most intrigues researchers is not whether this migration happened — the evidence is already too strong to deny — but why these sailors came from the relatively remote river interior of Kalimantan, rather than from Java or Bali, which were far more politically advanced and vastly more densely populated. The answer may lie precisely in that density: the fertile agrarian land of Java and Bali produced political structures that were saturated and bound to the soil, while the Barito communities, living at the margins of river trade networks, had greater mobility and fewer ties to any agrarian kingdom. Some scholars also suspect this migration was not spontaneous and independent, but occurred under the umbrella — or at least along the shipping networks — of Srivijayan hegemony, whose trading influence had spread so widely that it could link the river interior of Kalimantan to trans-oceanic shipping routes.

Secara teknis, pelayaran sejauh itu dimungkinkan oleh pemahaman navigasi yang matang atas Arus Khatulistiwa Selatan (South Equatorial Current)—sebuah "jalan tol" arus laut yang mengalir stabil dari timur ke barat melintasi Samudra Hindia, membentuk pola sirkulasi melingkar (gyre navigation) yang bisa dimanfaatkan pelaut berpengalaman untuk menyeberang tanpa harus melawan arus. Perjalanan pulang pun bukan perjalanan buntu: para pelaut menggunakan Angin Muson Barat Daya untuk kembali, menyusuri pesisir Swahili di Afrika Timur, singgah di Maladewa, sebelum akhirnya kembali menuju Selat Malaka—sebuah rute dua arah yang menunjukkan bahwa pelayaran ini bukan insiden sekali jalan yang kebetulan terdampar, melainkan jalur pelayaran yang dipahami, direncanakan, dan kemungkinan diulang berkali-kali selama beberapa generasi.

Technically, a voyage of such distance was made possible by a mature understanding of the South Equatorial Current — an ocean "toll road" flowing steadily from east to west across the Indian Ocean, forming a circular gyre pattern that experienced sailors could ride across without fighting the current. Nor was the return journey a dead end: sailors used the Southwest Monsoon to come back, tracing the Swahili coast of East Africa, calling at the Maldives, before finally returning to the Strait of Malacca — a two-way route showing this was no one-off accident of being swept off course, but a shipping lane that was understood, planned, and likely repeated many times over several generations.

Jejak para pelaut itu tidak pernah benar-benar hilang di tanah baru mereka. Di Dataran Tinggi Imerina, jantung Madagaskar, keturunan mereka—kini dikenal sebagai Suku Merina—mengembangkan budi daya sawah berundak yang secara mencolok mengingatkan pada teknik pertanian di Nusantara, lengkap dengan ritual Famadihana (upacara membalik jenazah leluhur) yang menggemakan penghormatan pada arwah leluhur yang juga hidup subur dalam kepercayaan Nusantara kuno. Dua belas abad kemudian, seorang keturunan pelaut Barito yang tak pernah tahu nama leluhurnya sendiri masih hidup di pulau yang jauh itu, berbahasa dengan akar kata yang sama seperti nenek moyangnya di tepi Sungai Barito—sebuah monumen hidup yang jauh lebih senyap, namun tak kalah megah, dari batu-batu Borobudur.

The trace of those sailors never truly vanished in their new land. On the Imerina Plateau, the heart of Madagascar, their descendants — now known as the Merina people — developed terraced rice cultivation strikingly reminiscent of Nusantara farming techniques, complete with the Famadihana ritual (the turning of the ancestors' bones), echoing an ancestor-reverence that also flourished richly in ancient Nusantara belief. Twelve centuries later, a descendant of Barito sailors who never knew his own ancestors' names still lives on that distant island, speaking words rooted in the same tongue as his forebears on the banks of the Barito River — a living monument far quieter, yet no less magnificent, than the stones of Borobudur.

Jejak Komoditas — Sebelum Rempah, Ada GaramCommodity Trail — Before Spice, There Was Salt

Jauh sebelum lada dan cengkih menjadi rebutan dunia, komoditas paling fundamental dalam sejarah manusia justru sesuatu yang kini dianggap remeh: garam. Sebagai satu-satunya cara mengawetkan protein sebelum era pendingin, garam menjadi barang strategis yang memicu pembangunan jalur dagang seperti Via Salaria di Italia kuno—begitu vitalnya hingga tentara Romawi kadang dibayar dengan jatah garam, asal-usul kata salary (gaji) dalam bahasa Inggris modern. Logika yang sama—komoditas kecil yang menentukan nasib besar—akan berulang terus sepanjang epos ini: dari garam ke lada, dari lada ke kopi, dari kopi ke karet.

Long before pepper and cloves became objects of global desire, the most fundamental commodity in human history was something now considered trivial: salt. As the only means of preserving protein before refrigeration, salt became a strategic good that drove the building of trade routes such as the Via Salaria in ancient Italy — so vital that Roman soldiers were sometimes paid in salt rations, the origin of the modern English word salary. The same logic — a small commodity determining a great fate — will recur again and again throughout this epic: from salt to pepper, from pepper to coffee, from coffee to rubber.

Mataram Kediri dan Legenda Jangka JayabayaMataram Kediri and the Legend of Jayabaya's Prophecy

Perpindahan pusat peradaban ke Jawa Timur melahirkan era baru yang berpusat di sepanjang Sungai Brantas. Raja Airlangga mendirikan Kahuripan dan berhasil menyatukan kembali wilayah yang terpecah pasca-serangan Srivijaya (Mahapralaya tahun 1016 M).

The shift of civilisation's centre to East Java gave birth to a new era centred along the Brantas River. King Airlangga founded Kahuripan and succeeded in reuniting a realm fractured by the Srivijayan raid (the Mahapralaya of 1016 CE).

Tragedi kemanusiaan dan politik kembali melanda di akhir hayat Airlangga. Putri mahkotanya, Sangramavijaya Tunggadewi, menolak takhta dan memilih menjadi pertapa dengan gelar Dewi Kilisuci. Perang saudara yang mengerikan mengancam antara dua putra Airlangga lainnya: Sri Samarawijaya dan Mapanji Garakan.

Human and political tragedy struck again at the end of Airlangga's life. His crown princess, Sangramavijaya Tunggadewi, refused the throne and chose to become an ascetic under the title Dewi Kilisuci. A terrible civil war threatened between Airlangga's two other sons: Sri Samarawijaya and Mapanji Garakan.

Demi mencegah pertumpahan darah antarsaudara, Airlangga meminta bantuan Mpu Bharada, seorang pendeta sakti. Dalam naskah Calon Arang dan Prasasti Mahaksubhya, Mpu Bharada terbang menumpahkan air suci dari kendinya, membelah wilayah kerajaan menjadi dua secara fisik dan ghaib: Jenggala dan Kediri (Panjalu). Pembagian ini bukannya mendatangkan kedamaian, melainkan menyulut perang saudara abadi yang mengilhami roman-roman klasik cerita Panji.

To prevent bloodshed between brothers, Airlangga sought the help of Mpu Bharada, a sacred priest. In the Calon Arang manuscript and the Mahaksubhya Inscription, Mpu Bharada is said to have flown and poured sacred water from his vessel, splitting the kingdom's territory in two, both physically and mystically: Jenggala and Kediri (Panjalu). Rather than bringing peace, this division ignited an enduring civil war that would inspire the classic Panji romances.

II. Zaman Emas Sastra dan Nubuat JayabayaII. The Golden Age of Literature and the Prophecy of Jayabaya

Kediri tumbuh menjadi kekuatan agraris dan maritim yang makmur di bawah pimpinan Raja Jayabaya (1135–1157 M), yang akhirnya berhasil menundukkan Jenggala (Panjalu Jayati). Di istana Kediri, sastra Jawa Kuna mencapai puncak keemasannya melalui Kakawin Bharatayuddha karya Mpu Sedah dan Mpu Panuluh.

Kediri grew into a prosperous agrarian and maritime power under King Jayabaya (1135–1157 CE), who ultimately succeeded in subduing Jenggala (Panjalu Jayati). At the Kediri court, Old Javanese literature reached its golden peak through the Kakawin Bharatayuddha, composed by Mpu Sedah and Mpu Panuluh.

Dalam tradisi lisan dan manuskrip Jawa, Jayabaya dikenang sebagai nabi kosmologis yang menyusun Jangka Jayabaya—ramalan tentang siklus zaman dari Kalabendu (zaman kegelapan) menuju Kalasuba (zaman keemasan) serta munculnya sosok penyelamat spiritual, Ratu Adil.

In Javanese oral tradition and manuscripts, Jayabaya is remembered as a cosmological prophet who composed the Jangka Jayabaya — a prophecy of the cycle of ages from Kalabendu (the age of darkness) to Kalasuba (the golden age), and of the coming of a spiritual saviour figure, the Ratu Adil (Just King).

At the western edge of the world, European knights had just returned empty-handed from the Second Crusade (1147–1149), having failed to retake Edessa. Twelfth-century Southeast Asia, with Kediri and Angkor as its two poles, was in fact enjoying the height of literary and architectural prosperity — while Christian Europe sank into the failure of its own holy war.

Singhasari: Belati Ken Arok dan Tinta MongolSinghasari: Ken Arok's Dagger and Mongol Ink

Pada tahun 1222 M, tatanan lama Kediri diruntuhkan oleh munculnya seorang petualang dari kelas bawah: Ken Arok. Melalui kudeta berdarah terhadap Tunggul Ametung di Tumapel, Ken Arok mendirikan Kerajaan Singhasari.

In 1222 CE, the old order of Kediri was overturned by the rise of a low-born adventurer: Ken Arok. Through a bloody coup against Tunggul Ametung in Tumapel, Ken Arok founded the Kingdom of Singhasari.

I. Intrik Takhta dan Kutukan Mpu GandringI. Throne Intrigue and the Curse of Mpu Gandring

Kitab Pararaton membuka kisah Ken Arok bukan sebagai manusia biasa, melainkan sebagai titisan dewa. Dikisahkan, ibunya, Ken Endok, tengah menyeberangi hutan ketika didatangi Dewa Brahma dalam wujud cahaya dan mengandunginya secara ghaib. Anak hasil hubungan ilahi itu dibuang oleh ayah tirinya di sebuah kuburan, ditemukan dan dipungut oleh seorang pencuri bernama Lubdaka, lalu diasuh berpindah-pindah dari satu penjahat ke penjahat lain—seorang perampok, seorang penjudi bernama Bango Samparan yang mengangkatnya sebagai anak setelah keberuntungan judinya berbalik tiap kali Ken Arok berada di dekatnya. Masa muda Ken Arok dilukiskan penuh kekerasan jalanan: ia dikenal sebagai berandal yang meneror desa-desa di kaki Gunung Kawi, hingga suatu hari seorang resi sakti bernama Lohgawe—brahmana pendatang dari India yang membaca tanda-tanda langit—mengenalinya sebagai manusia titisan Wisnu yang ditakdirkan menjadi raja pemersatu tanah Jawa.

The Pararaton chronicle opens Ken Arok's story not as an ordinary man, but as a divine incarnation. It is told that his mother, Ken Endok, was crossing a forest when the god Brahma came to her in the form of light and made her conceive by mystical means. The child of this divine union was abandoned by his stepfather at a graveyard, found and taken in by a thief named Lubdaka, then passed from one criminal to another — a bandit, and a gambler named Bango Samparan, who adopted him as a son after his gambling luck turned every time Ken Arok was near him. Ken Arok's youth is painted as one of street violence: he was known as a thug who terrorised the villages at the foot of Mount Kawi, until one day a powerful sage named Lohgawe — a Brahmin newcomer from India who read the signs of the heavens — recognised him as an incarnation of Vishnu, destined to become the king who would unite the land of Java.

Babad Tanah Jawi dan Pararaton merekam bagaimana ambisi Ken Arok lahir dari hasrat dan mistisisme. Ia memesan keris sakti kepada empu pandai besi termasyhur, Mpu Gandring, dengan permintaan agar selesai dalam waktu satu tahun. Namun karena tak sabar menunggu, Ken Arok mendatanginya pada bulan kelima, dan ketika Mpu Gandring menjelaskan bahwa keris itu belum sempurna, Ken Arok merampas dan menikam sang pembuat keris dengan bilah yang belum jadi itu juga. Sebelum tewas, Mpu Gandring mengutuk bahwa keris itu—yang kelak bergerigi tujuh lekuk (luk)—akan meminta korban tujuh nyawa keturunan Ken Arok sendiri, sebuah nubuat yang akan terbukti mengerikan tepat sesuai hitungannya.

Babad Tanah Jawi and Pararaton record how Ken Arok's ambition was born of desire and mysticism. He commissioned a sacred keris from the renowned smith Mpu Gandring, asking that it be finished within a year. Too impatient to wait, Ken Arok came to him in the fifth month, and when Mpu Gandring explained the blade was not yet finished, Ken Arok seized it and stabbed the smith with the unfinished weapon itself. Before dying, Mpu Gandring cursed the blade — later marked with seven wavy curves (luk) — declaring it would claim seven lives among Ken Arok's own descendants, a prophecy that would prove terrifyingly precise.

Nafsu kekuasaan Ken Arok makin membara ketika, dalam sebuah perjalanan menuju Tumapel, ia melihat betis Ken Dedes (istri Tunggul Ametung) tersingkap dari kereta kudanya dan memancarkan cahaya mistis (rahasya)—penanda bahwa Ken Dedes adalah seorang Nareswari, perempuan suci yang dari rahimnya akan melahirkan raja-raja Jawa turun-temurun. Ken Arok, dibakar hasrat sekaligus ambisi politik, meminjamkan keris Mpu Gandring kepada Kebo Ijo—seorang pengawal yang gemar memamerkan senjata—agar semua orang menyaksikan dan mengingat keris itu di tangannya. Setelah itu, secara diam-diam, Ken Arok sendiri yang menikam Tunggul Ametung dengan keris tersebut, lalu memfitnah Kebo Ijo sebagai pembunuhnya. Kebo Ijo dihukum mati atas tuduhan palsu itu, dan Ken Arok pun naik menjadi penguasa Tumapel serta menikahi Ken Dedes yang tengah mengandung anak Tunggul Ametung (kelak lahir sebagai Anusapati).

Ken Arok's lust for power burned hotter still when, on a journey to Tumapel, he glimpsed Ken Dedes's calf (wife of Tunggul Ametung) exposed from her carriage, radiating a mystical light (rahasya) — a sign that Ken Dedes was a Nareswari, a sacred woman destined to give birth to a line of Javanese kings. Burning with both desire and political ambition, Ken Arok lent Mpu Gandring's keris to Kebo Ijo — a guard fond of showing off weapons — so that everyone would see and remember the blade in his hand. Then, in secret, Ken Arok himself stabbed Tunggul Ametung with that same keris, and framed Kebo Ijo as the killer. Kebo Ijo was executed on the false charge, and Ken Arok rose to rule Tumapel, marrying Ken Dedes while she was still carrying Tunggul Ametung's child (later born as Anusapati).

Kutukan Mpu Gandring kemudian menuntut janjinya satu demi satu, membentuk rantai kematian yang oleh para pujangga Jawa dianggap sebagai hukum karma (tumimbal wilasa) yang tak bisa dielakkan siapa pun, sekalipun seorang titisan dewa: Anusapati, setelah dewasa dan mengetahui rahasia kematian ayah kandungnya, membalas dendam dengan menikam Ken Arok memakai keris yang sama; Anusapati sendiri kemudian dibunuh oleh Tohjaya (putra Ken Arok dari selirnya, Ni Ndang Pengawat) dengan keris pusaka itu pula, sambil disuguhi hidangan saat sedang menyabung ayam—sebuah pengkhianatan dalam momen paling santai dan tak curiga. Kutukan itu terus berjalan hingga generasi ketiga, meninggalkan Singhasari sebagai dinasti yang lahir dari darah dan tak pernah benar-benar lepas dari bayang keris yang memulainya.

Mpu Gandring's curse then collected its debt one life at a time, forming a chain of deaths that Javanese poets regarded as an inescapable law of karma (tumimbal wilasa), one that spared not even a god's incarnation: Anusapati, once grown and aware of the secret behind his true father's death, took revenge by stabbing Ken Arok with the same keris; Anusapati himself was then killed by Tohjaya (Ken Arok's son by his concubine, Ni Ndang Pengawat) with that same heirloom blade, while being served a meal during a cockfight — a betrayal in the most relaxed, unsuspecting of moments. The curse ran on into a third generation, leaving Singhasari as a dynasty born of blood, never truly free of the shadow of the keris that had begun it all.

II. Amarah Kubilai Khan dan Tinta KertanegaraII. The Wrath of Kublai Khan and Kertanegara's Insult

Secara geopolitik, puncak Singhasari dicapai di bawah Kertanegara. Ia mencetuskan wawasan Nusantara awal melalui Ekspedisi Pamalayu (1275 M) untuk mengimbangi ekspansi Kekaisaran Mongol di bawah Kubilai Khan. Ketika utusan Mongol, Meng Chi, datang membawa tuntutan tunduk dari Sang Khan pada tahun 1289 M, Kertanegara yang sedang mabuk ritual Tantra merasa terhina. Ia memotong telinga Meng Chi dan mencap mukanya dengan besi panas—sebuah penghinaan internasional yang memicu Kubilai Khan mengirimkan 20.000 prajurit ekspedisi Mongol ke tanah Jawa.

Geopolitically, Singhasari's peak was reached under Kertanegara. He forged an early vision of Nusantara unity through the Pamalayu Expedition (1275 CE) to counterbalance the expansion of the Mongol Empire under Kublai Khan. When the Mongol envoy, Meng Chi, arrived in 1289 CE bearing a demand for submission from the Great Khan, Kertanegara, immersed in Tantric ritual, took it as an insult. He cut off Meng Chi's ear and branded his face with a hot iron — an international insult that provoked Kublai Khan to send 20,000 Mongol expeditionary troops to the land of Java.

Penghinaan yang diterima utusan Kubilai Khan di istana Kertanegara bukanlah insiden kecil di pinggiran dunia. The insult dealt to Kublai Khan's envoy at Kertanegara's court was no minor incident at the edge of the world. At that time, the Mongol Empire was the largest land empire in human history, stretching from Korea to the gates of Vienna — a power that had razed Baghdad (1258) and toppled the Song Dynasty in China. In Europe, English barons had just forced King John to sign the Magna Carta (1215), laying the foundation for limits on absolute royal power. A young Venetian merchant named Marco Polo was, at that very time, serving at Kublai Khan's court in China, and would soon — on his return journey around 1292 CE — call at Sumatra and record the kingdoms of the Nusantara in his travel accounts, making him one of the first Europeans to write firsthand about the world that would soon give birth to Majapahit.

I. Pengkhianatan Jayakatwang dan Jebakan MusuhI. Jayakatwang's Betrayal and a Trap for the Enemy

In 1292 CE, before the Mongol fleet ever landed, Singhasari's fate had already collapsed from within. Jayakatwang, the Duke of Kediri who nursed an ancestral grudge, rebelled and stormed the Singhasari palace while Kertanegara was performing a religious ritual. Kertanegara was killed. In Madura, a high-stakes diplomatic game was played. Raden Wijaya pretended to surrender and pledge service to Jayakatwang. He asked for a stretch of scrubland at the mouth of the Brantas River to settle — a mangrove wilderness thick with bitter fruit, later named Majapahit.

Ketika armada pasukan Yuan-Mongol yang dipimpin Shi-bi, Ike Mese, dan Gaoxing mendarat pada awal 1293 M untuk membalas dendam pada Kertanegara, Raden Wijaya memanfaatkan kebodohan mereka. Setelah tentara Mongol menghancurkan Kediri dan menawan Jayakatwang, Raden Wijaya membujuk serdadu Mongol untuk datang ke istananya tanpa membawa senjata guna menerima upeti hamba. Di tengah pesta kemenangan, Raden Wijaya melancarkan pembantaian kilat atas pasukan Mongol yang lengah, memaksa sisa-sisa armada Tatar melarikan diri kembali ke kapal mereka.

When the Yuan-Mongol fleet under Shi-bi, Ike Mese, and Gaoxing landed in early 1293 CE to avenge Kertanegara, Raden Wijaya exploited their ignorance. He convinced the Mongols that Jayakatwang was Kertanegara's successor. After Mongol troops crushed Kediri and captured Jayakatwang, Raden Wijaya persuaded the Mongol soldiers to come to his palace unarmed to receive a vassal's tribute. In the midst of their victory celebration, Raden Wijaya launched a lightning massacre against the unguarded Mongol troops, forcing the remnants of the Tatar fleet to flee back to their ships.

Ironically, the most feared empire on earth in the 13th century — the Mongol war machine that had conquered half of Eurasia from China to Russia — was humiliated in the marshes of the Brantas delta by the cunning of an exiled prince with no great army. This small defeat is barely a footnote in world history, but for the Nusantara it was a foundation: out of that Mongol defeat, on the bitter-fruited forest land of Tarik, Majapahit was born.

Gajah Mada dan Ekonomi Maritim GlobalGajah Mada and the Global Maritime Economy

Masa keemasan Majapahit tercapai pada abad ke-14 di bawah pemerintahan Raja Hayam Wuruk dan Mahapatih Gajah Mada. Dalam sidang pelantikannya (1336 M), Gajah Mada mengucapkan Sumpah Palapa—tekad untuk tidak mengonsumsi kenikmatan duniawi (amukti palapa) sebelum menyatukan Nusantara di bawah panji Majapahit.

Majapahit's golden age was reached in the 14th century under King Hayam Wuruk and Prime Minister Gajah Mada. At his inauguration (1336 CE), Gajah Mada swore the Palapa Oath — a vow to abstain from worldly pleasures (amukti palapa) until he had united the Nusantara under Majapahit's banner.

I. Dari Bhayangkara ke MahapatihI. From Royal Guard to Prime Minister

Asal-usul Gajah Mada diselimuti misteri yang justru memperbesar legendanya. Berbeda dari kebanyakan bangsawan Jawa, tak ada satu pun prasasti atau naskah yang menyebut garis keturunannya—seolah ia muncul dari kekosongan sejarah, hanya bermodalkan kecerdasan dan kesetiaan. Ia pertama kali tercatat sebagai perwira pasukan pengawal raja, Bhayangkara, pada masa pemberontakan Ra Kuti (1319 M), sebuah kudeta berdarah yang memaksa Raja Jayanagara melarikan diri dari ibu kota Majapahit dalam kegelapan malam, dikawal segelintir prajurit setia. Dalam pelarian itu, Gajah Madalah yang merancang siasat dan menjamin keselamatan sang raja hingga kembali merebut takhta—jasa yang mengangkatnya dari perwira biasa menjadi patih Kahuripan, lalu patih Kediri, sebelum akhirnya dilantik sebagai Mahapatih Amangkubhumi Majapahit pada 1336 M, jabatan tertinggi kedua setelah raja itu sendiri.

Gajah Mada's origins are wrapped in a mystery that only magnifies his legend. Unlike most Javanese nobles, no inscription or manuscript records his lineage — as if he emerged from a historical void, armed with nothing but intelligence and loyalty. He first appears as an officer of the royal guard, Bhayangkara, during the Ra Kuti rebellion (1319 CE), a bloody coup that forced King Jayanagara to flee the Majapahit capital under cover of night, escorted by a handful of loyal soldiers. In that flight, it was Gajah Mada who devised the strategy and secured the king's safety until the throne was recaptured — a service that lifted him from ordinary officer to regent of Kahuripan, then regent of Kediri, before he was finally installed as Prime Minister (Mahapatih Amangkubhumi) of Majapahit in 1336 CE, the second-highest office after the king himself.

Menurut tradisi Pararaton, Sumpah Palapa diucapkan Gajah Mada di hadapan para menteri dalam sidang pelantikan dengan nada yang nyaris menantang: ia bersumpah tidak akan mencicipi palapa—ditafsirkan sebagian pujangga sebagai rempah-rempah tertentu, sebagian lain sebagai kenikmatan duniawi secara umum—sebelum seluruh Nusantara (disebutkannya satu per satu: Gurun, Seram, Tanjungpura, Haru, Pahang, Dompo, Bali, Sunda, Palembang, hingga Tumasik) tunduk pada Majapahit. Sejumlah menteri senior yang hadir dikabarkan menahan tawa mendengar sumpah muluk dari seorang patih baru—namun dalam dua dekade berikutnya, Gajah Mada membuktikan sumpahnya lewat serangkaian ekspedisi militer dan diplomasi yang benar-benar menundukkan hampir seluruh wilayah yang disebutnya.

According to Pararaton tradition, Gajah Mada swore the Palapa Oath before the ministers at his inauguration in an almost defiant tone: he vowed not to taste palapa — interpreted by some scholars as a certain spice, by others as worldly pleasure in general — until the entire Nusantara (which he named one by one: Gurun, Seram, Tanjungpura, Haru, Pahang, Dompo, Bali, Sunda, Palembang, and Tumasik) submitted to Majapahit. Several senior ministers present reportedly stifled laughter at such a grandiose oath from a newly appointed regent — yet over the following two decades, Gajah Mada made good on his vow through a series of military expeditions and diplomacy that genuinely subdued nearly every territory he had named.

Behind the empire's splendour lay hidden its most heartbreaking diplomatic tragedy: the Pasunda Bubat Incident (1357 CE). Hayam Wuruk intended to marry Princess Dyah Pitaloka Citraresmi of the Sunda Kingdom out of love and inter-state friendship. The retinue of Sunda's King Maharaja Linggabuana arrived at the Field of Bubat without full battle armament, expecting a wedding celebration.

Namun, Gajah Mada memandang kedatangan Kerajaan Sunda dari kacamata politik imperialis: Sunda tidak boleh menikah sebagai kawan setara, melainkan harus menyerahkan putrinya sebagai persembahan tanda ketundukan vasal pada Majapahit. Penolakan bermartabat dari Raja Sunda memicu pengerahan pasukan Bhayangkara. Pertempuran tidak seimbang meletus di Bubat; seluruh rombongan bangsawan Sunda tewas membela kehormatan mereka, dan Putri Dyah Pitaloka melakukan pati obong (bunuh diri). But Gajah Mada viewed Sunda's arrival through an imperial lens: Sunda could not marry as an equal, but must surrender its princess as an offering of vassal submission to Majapahit. The Sundanese king's dignified refusal triggered the deployment of the Bhayangkara guard. An unequal battle broke out at Bubat; the entire Sundanese noble retinue died defending their honour, and Princess Dyah Pitaloka committed pati obong (ritual self-immolation). Keputusan Pati Unus mempertaruhkan armada dan nyawanya sendiri demi merebut kembali sebuah kota di seberang lautan, yang bahkan bukan bagian dari wilayah kekuasaan Demak, sering luput dipertanyakan pembaca sejarah modern. Modern readers of history often overlook why Pati Unus staked his fleet and his own life to retake a city across the sea that was not even part of Demak's own territory. The answer lies in three interlocking layers of interest. First, a religious interest: Malacca, as traced in its lineage in Chapter 10B, was the foremost Islamic sultanate in Southeast Asia — a centre for spreading and safeguarding Islam whose network of scholars and merchants reached directly into Jepara, Tuban, and Gresik. Its fall to the cross-bearing Portuguese was not merely the military defeat of a neighbouring kingdom, but was seen by Java's coastal Muslim community as a direct assault on the Islamic world order of which they were a part — a call to jihad that no Muslim ruler in the region could easily ignore.

Kedua, kepentingan ekonomi yang jauh lebih konkret: Selat Malaka adalah leher botol bagi seluruh arus rempah dari Maluku dan lada dari Sumatra menuju pasar India, Arab, dan Eropa. Ketika Portugis menguasai dan memungut cukai berat di pelabuhan itu, jaringan dagang Muslim yang selama ini menopang kemakmuran Demak, Tuban, dan Gresik—termasuk kekayaan pribadi Pati Unus sendiri sebagai penguasa Jepara yang piawai berdagang—terancam langsung. Long before a single European ship reached the Nusantara, Spain and Portugal — the two earliest Catholic maritime powers to explore the oceans — had already divided the entire world they had not even seen. Through the mediation of the Pope in Rome, the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) drew an imaginary line splitting the Atlantic Ocean, granting Spain rights to all land west of the line and Portugal all land to its east. But the line said nothing of what would happen when the two nations, sailing in opposite directions around the globe, eventually met again on the far side of the planet — right at the heart of the Nusantara, around the clove-rich Maluku Islands. That dispute was resolved by the Treaty of Zaragoza (1529): Portugal retained its rights to Maluku and most of the eastern Nusantara, while Spain — which had already gained a foothold in Maluku through the remnants of Magellan's expedition — agreed to withdraw and shift its focus north, to the islands later named the Philippines in honour of King Felipe II. This Zaragoza line, quite by accident, became one reason why the Philippines today differs so sharply in language and religion from its Nusantara neighbours: it fell to a different coloniser, in a division decided without a single Southeast Asian representative present in the negotiating room.

This is how the modern geopolitics of the Nusantara was formed: not solely by the will of its own kings and peoples, as we have witnessed throughout this epic, but also by lines drawn from afar, in European negotiating rooms that never truly understood the land they were dividing.

Kita telah menyaksikan bagaimana Balaputradewa, pangeran Syailendra yang kalah di Ratu Boko, melarikan diri ke Sriwijaya dan mewarisi takhtanya (Bab 2B). Sriwijaya yang diwarisinya bertahan sebagai kekuatan maritim dominan hingga abad ke-11, namun kemudian meredup perlahan digerogoti serangan Chola dari India Selatan dan pergeseran jalur dagang. Pukulan terakhir datang justru dari saudara jauhnya sendiri di Jawa: pada 1377 M, dalam babak akhir dari semangat Sumpah Palapa yang telah diucapkan Gajah Mada empat dekade sebelumnya, Majapahit mengirimkan ekspedisi militer ke Palembang untuk menundukkan sisa-sisa kekuatan Sriwijaya yang masih bertahan di sana—babak penutup yang jarang mendapat sorotan sebesar Sumpah Palapa itu sendiri, namun secara efektif memadamkan nyala terakhir imperium maritim tertua di Nusantara.

Before tracing Brawijaya V's lineage toward Mataram, there is an older strand of genealogy less often told in full: how the three great Nusantara kingdoms — Srivijaya, Majapahit, and Demak — were in fact bound together in a single chain of political cause and effect spanning more than six centuries. We have already seen how Balaputradewa, the defeated Shailendra prince of Ratu Boko, fled to Srivijaya and inherited its throne (Chapter 2B). The Srivijaya he inherited endured as a dominant maritime power into the 11th century, then slowly faded, gnawed at by Chola raids from South India and shifting trade routes. The final blow, ironically, came from its own distant kin in Java: in 1377 CE, in a late chapter of the very spirit of the Palapa Oath sworn by Gajah Mada four decades earlier, Majapahit sent a military expedition to Palembang to subdue the remnants of Srivijayan power still holding on there — a closing chapter rarely given the spotlight the Palapa Oath itself receives, yet one that effectively extinguished the last flame of the Nusantara's oldest maritime empire.

Dari reruntuhan politik Palembang pasca-1377 itulah muncul sosok yang akan mengubah peta dagang dunia: Parameswara, seorang pangeran yang mengklaim garis keturunan dari darah bangsawan Sriwijaya-Palembang. Terusir dari tanah leluhurnya, ia mengungsi ke Temasek (kelak Singapura), namun kembali terusir—kali ini oleh serangan dari Kerajaan Ayutthaya di Siam—sebelum akhirnya menetap di sebuah kampung nelayan kecil di tepi selat sempit yang memisahkan Sumatra dari Semenanjung Malaya. Di tempat itulah, sekitar 1400 M, Parameswara mendirikan apa yang kelak menjadi Kesultanan Malaka—pelabuhan yang dalam waktu satu abad akan tumbuh menjadi bandar dagang paling ramai di seluruh Asia Tenggara, sekaligus pusat penyebaran Islam yang jaringannya menjangkau hingga pesisir utara Jawa. Dengan demikian, kejatuhan Sriwijaya bukanlah akhir cerita, melainkan benih bagi kelahiran Malaka—kerajaan yang, seperti akan kita saksikan pada Bab 8B, nasibnya akan terjalin erat dengan Demak ketika Portugis merebutnya pada 1511 M.

From the political ruins of post-1377 Palembang emerged a figure who would change the world's trade map: Parameswara, a prince claiming descent from Srivijayan-Palembang nobility. Driven from his ancestral land, he fled to Temasek (later Singapore), but was driven out again — this time by an attack from the Ayutthaya Kingdom of Siam — before finally settling in a small fishing village on the narrow strait separating Sumatra from the Malay Peninsula. There, around 1400 CE, Parameswara founded what would become the Sultanate of Malacca — a port that within a century would grow into the busiest trading city in all of Southeast Asia, and a centre for the spread of Islam whose network reached as far as the north coast of Java. Srivijaya's fall, then, was not the end of the story, but the seed of Malacca's birth — a kingdom whose fate, as we shall see in Chapter 8B, would become closely entwined with Demak when the Portuguese seized it in 1511 CE.

Sementara itu, di Jawa, Majapahit yang telah menghabisi Sriwijaya justru menyemai kehancurannya sendiri lewat perang saudara Paregreg dan pertumbuhan kekuatan Islam di kota-kota pesisirnya (Bab 7). Menurut tradisi Babad, Raden Patah, pendiri Kesultanan Demak, adalah putra Brawijaya V sendiri dari seorang selir keturunan Tionghoa bernama Siu Ban Ci (dalam catatan Portugis Tomé Pires disebut dengan nama yang berbeda-beda, Pate Rodim atau Pate Cu-Cu)—namun karena posisi ibunya yang bukan permaisuri utama, Raden Patah dibesarkan jauh dari istana, dititipkan ke lingkungan pesisir yang telah memeluk Islam, dan justru dari jarak itulah ia tumbuh menjadi penguasa pesisir yang berhasil mewarisi Wahyu Keprabon Majapahit tanpa harus mewarisi agama Hindu-Buddhanya. Dengan kata lain: Sriwijaya melahirkan Malaka lewat pengungsian seorang pangerannya yang kalah; Majapahit melahirkan Demak lewat pengakuan seorang putranya yang tersisih. Dua kerajaan Islam pesisir yang lahir dari dua kekalahan kerajaan besar Hindu-Buddha inilah yang kelak akan berdiri berdampingan—dan saling membantu—ketika Portugis datang mengguncang keduanya sekaligus di awal abad ke-16.

Meanwhile in Java, Majapahit, having finished off Srivijaya, was sowing the seeds of its own destruction through the Paregreg civil war and the growth of Islamic power in its coastal cities (Chapter 7). By babad tradition, Raden Patah, founder of the Demak Sultanate, was Brawijaya V's own son by a concubine of Chinese descent named Siu Ban Ci (recorded under various names by the Portuguese chronicler Tomé Pires, such as Pate Rodim or Pate Cu-Cu) — but because his mother was not the chief consort, Raden Patah was raised far from the palace, placed with a coastal community that had already embraced Islam, and it was precisely from that distance that he grew into a coastal ruler who managed to inherit Majapahit's Wahyu Keprabon without inheriting its Hindu-Buddhist religion. In other words: Srivijaya gave birth to Malacca through the exile of a defeated prince; Majapahit gave birth to Demak through the recognition of a marginalised son. These two coastal Islamic kingdoms, each born from the defeat of a great Hindu-Buddhist realm, would later stand side by side — and help one another — when the Portuguese came to shake both at once in the early 16th century.

II. Silsilah Utama Menuju Mataram dalam Babad Tanah JawiII. The Main Lineage to Mataram in Babad Tanah Jawi

Babad merekam garis keturunan lurus dari raja terakhir Majapahit menuju pendiri Mataram: Prabu Brawijaya V dari Majapahit menurunkan Bondan Kejawen (dari seorang putri bernama Putri Vandhan); Bondan Kejawen menurunkan Ki Ageng Getas Pandawa; Ki Ageng Getas Pandawa menurunkan Ki Ageng Selo, tokoh legendaris yang menurut cerita rakyat pernah menangkap petir dengan tangan kosong; Ki Ageng Selo menurunkan Ki Ageng Henis; Ki Ageng Henis menurunkan Ki Ageng Pamanahan; dan Ki Ageng Pamanahan pada akhirnya menurunkan Danang Sutawijaya, yang kelak dikenal sebagai Panembahan Senapati, pendiri Mataram Islam.

The babad records a direct line of descent from Majapahit's last king to Mataram's founder: Prabu Brawijaya V of Majapahit fathered Bondan Kejawen (by a princess named Putri Vandhan); Bondan Kejawen fathered Ki Ageng Getas Pandawa; Ki Ageng Getas Pandawa fathered Ki Ageng Selo, a legendary figure said in folklore to have once caught lightning with his bare hands; Ki Ageng Selo fathered Ki Ageng Henis; Ki Ageng Henis fathered Ki Ageng Pamanahan; and Ki Ageng Pamanahan finally fathered Danang Sutawijaya, later known as Panembahan Senapati, founder of Islamic Mataram.

Sejarawan kolonial Belanda, lewat catatan Portugis dan arsip VOC, memberi warna berbeda pada beberapa simpul silsilah ini. Brawijaya V, misalnya, dalam catatan Tomé Pires justru dikenal dengan nama Girisawardhana atau Ranawijaya yang memerintah sekitar 1474–1498—sedikit bergeser dari citra tunggal "raja terakhir yang moksa" dalam Babad. Ki Ageng Selo sendiri, di luar legenda penangkap petirnya, lebih tepat dipahami sebagai kepala kelompok agraris lokal di wilayah Grobogan, bukan bangsawan istana. Sementara Sutawijaya, yang oleh Babad digambarkan sebagai anak angkat Sultan Pajang sekaligus pendiri Mataram, tercatat dalam Daghregisters VOC sebagai "Senopati Ingalaga"—pemimpin pemberontakan yang secara paksa menundukkan Pajang dan pengaruh Demak di pedalaman Jawa. Sejarawan H.J. de Graaf menjelaskan bahwa konstruksi silsilah semacam ini sengaja diciptakan para pujangga keraton sebagai mekanisme kesinambungan dinasti (dynastic continuity): dengan menyambungkan darah Senapati ke Brawijaya V—dan karenanya, secara tidak langsung, ke seluruh rantai Sriwijaya-Majapahit-Demak yang telah kita telusuri di atas—Mataram Islam yang baru lahir dari kekerasan dan pemberontakan itu bisa memposisikan dirinya bukan sebagai perusak tatanan lama, melainkan sebagai pewaris sah dari seluruh keagungan kerajaan-kerajaan besar yang pernah berdiri di tanah Jawa sebelumnya.

Dutch colonial historians, drawing on Portuguese records and VOC archives, give a different colour to several links in this genealogy. Brawijaya V, for instance, appears in Tomé Pires's records under the name Girisawardhana or Ranawijaya, ruling roughly 1474–1498 — a slight departure from the babad's singular image of "the last king who vanished into moksa." Ki Ageng Selo himself, aside from his lightning-catching legend, is more accurately understood as the head of a local agrarian community in the Grobogan region, not a court noble. Sutawijaya, meanwhile, whom the babad portrays as the Sultan of Pajang's adopted son and Mataram's founder, is recorded in the VOC's Daghregisters as "Senopati Ingalaga" — the leader of a rebellion that forcibly subdued Pajang and Demak's influence in inland Java. Historian H.J. de Graaf explains that such genealogical constructions were deliberately crafted by court poets as a mechanism of dynastic continuity: by linking Senapati's blood to Brawijaya V — and therefore, indirectly, to the entire Srivijaya–Majapahit–Demak chain traced above — the newly born Islamic Mataram, born of violence and rebellion, could position itself not as a destroyer of the old order, but as the rightful heir to the full grandeur of every great kingdom that had ever stood on Javanese soil.

Sultan Agung: Ambing Antara Imperium dan KompeniSultan Agung: Suspended Between Empire and the Company

Mataram mencapai puncak ekspansi teritorial dan konsolidasi kebudayaan di bawah pemerintahan cucu Senapati, Sultan Agung Senapati ing Alaga Abdurrahman (1613–1645 M).

Mataram reached the peak of its territorial expansion and cultural consolidation under Senapati's grandson, Sultan Agung Senapati ing Alaga Abdurrahman (1613–1645 CE).

I. Tragedi Penaklukan Surabaya dan Eksekusi Pangeran PekikI. The Tragic Conquest of Surabaya and the Fate of Pangeran Pekik

Sultan Agung menundukkan adipati-adipati pesisir Jawa Timur yang membangkang melalui perang ekspansi yang kejam. Dalam pengepungan Surabaya (1620–1625 M), Sultan Agung menugaskan Tumenggung Bahureso untuk menyumbat aliran Sungai Brantas dan meracuni pasokan air kota dengan bangkai binatang serta buah jarak. Surabaya runtuh akibat kelaparan dan penyakit. Adipati Surabaya, Pangeran Pekik, ditawan ke Mataram. Meskipun kemudian dinikahkan dengan adik Sultan Agung (Ratu Pandansari), Pangeran Pekik dan seluruh keturunannya tetap berada di bawah pengawasan ketat kecurigaan istana Mataram.

Sultan Agung subdued the rebellious dukes of the East Javanese coast through a brutal war of expansion. During the siege of Surabaya (1620–1625 CE), Sultan Agung ordered Tumenggung Bahureso to dam the Brantas River and poison the city's water supply with animal carcasses and castor fruit. Surabaya collapsed from famine and disease. Its duke, Pangeran Pekik, was taken captive to Mataram. Though later married to Sultan Agung's sister (Ratu Pandansari), Pangeran Pekik and all his descendants remained under the Mataram court's tight, suspicious watch.

II. Bentrokan Akbar di BataviaII. The Great Clash at Batavia

Sultan Agung menyadari ancaman baru yang tumbuh di pesisir utara: VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie) yang telah menduduki Jayakarta dan mengubah namanya menjadi Batavia pada tahun 1619 M. Pada tahun 1628 dan 1629 M, Sultan Agung meluncurkan dua gelombang serangan massal untuk mengusir VOC dari Batavia.

Sultan Agung recognised a new threat growing on the north coast: the VOC (Dutch East India Company), which had occupied Jayakarta and renamed it Batavia in 1619 CE. In 1628 and 1629 CE, Sultan Agung launched two massive waves of attack to drive the VOC out of Batavia.

Catatan Daghregisters Batavia di bawah Gubernur Jenderal Jan Pieterszoon Coen merekam betapa intimnya ancaman pasukan Mataram. Pengepungan ini gagal terutama karena keunggulan armada laut VOC yang membakar lumbung-lumbung beras pasokan Mataram di Cirebon dan Tegal. Kegagalan ini dibayar mahal secara internal: Tumenggung Bahureso dan para perwira Mataram yang retret dieksekusi mati oleh Sultan Agung karena dianggap tidak menjalankan titah hingga tuntas.

The Daghregisters Batavia records under Governor-General Jan Pieterszoon Coen show just how close the Mataram threat came. The siege failed mainly because of the VOC's naval superiority, which burned the rice granaries supplying Mataram's forces at Cirebon and Tegal. The failure carried a heavy internal cost: Tumenggung Bahureso and the retreating Mataram officers were executed by Sultan Agung for failing to carry out his command to the end.

Meskipun gagal secara militer di Batavia, Sultan Agung berhasil mengukuhkan kebudayaan Jawa: ia menciptakan Kalender Jawa (sintesis antara Kalender Hijriah dan Saka) serta menggubah karya sastra keagamaan Sastra Gendhing.

Though he failed militarily at Batavia, Sultan Agung succeeded in cementing Javanese culture: he created the Javanese Calendar (a synthesis of the Hijri and Saka calendars) and composed the religious literary work Sastra Gendhing.

Sepanjang masa pemerintahan Sultan Agung, Eropa tenggelam dalam Perang Tiga Puluh Tahun (1618–1648) — salah satu konflik paling mematikan dalam sejarah benua itu, yang menewaskan hingga delapan juta jiwa dan meluluhlantakkan wilayah-wilayah Jerman. Kedua pengepungan Batavia oleh Sultan Agung (1628 dan 1629) terjadi persis di tengah kekacauan Eropa itu — pada saat yang sama, VOC di Batavia sesungguhnya adalah sempalan kecil dari sebuah republik yang sedang berjuang mati-matian mempertahankan kemerdekaannya sendiri di ujung dunia yang lain. Skala kekerasan di Eropa pada abad ke-17 mengingatkan bahwa dunia ini, di mana pun, sedang sama-sama diuji oleh perang agama, perang dagang, dan perang takhta.

Throughout Sultan Agung's reign, Europe was sinking into the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648) — one of the deadliest conflicts in the continent's history, killing up to eight million people and devastating the German lands. Sultan Agung's two sieges of Batavia (1628 and 1629) occurred right in the middle of that European chaos — at the very same time, the VOC at Batavia was, in truth, a small outpost of a republic fighting desperately for its own independence at the other end of the world. The scale of violence in 17th-century Europe is a reminder that this world, everywhere, was being tested at once by wars of religion, wars of trade, and wars of the throne.

Perlawanan Rempah dari Timur: Ternate, Tidore, dan Ayam Jantan dari MakassarSpice Resistance from the East: Ternate, Tidore, and the Rooster of Makassar

Sementara perhatian epos ini banyak tertuju pada pergulatan kekuasaan di Jawa, di ujung timur Nusantara sebuah perang yang jauh lebih lama dan tak kalah berdarah tengah berkecamuk—perang yang bukan diperebutkan demi takhta atau Wahyu Keprabon, melainkan demi cengkih dan pala, dua rempah yang hanya tumbuh di sepetak kecil bumi ini dan tak ditemukan di tempat lain mana pun di dunia.

While much of this epic's attention has fallen on the power struggles of Java, at the eastern edge of the Nusantara a far longer and no less bloody war was raging — a war fought not over a throne or the Wahyu Keprabon, but over cloves and nutmeg, two spices that grew only in one small patch of the earth and nowhere else in the world.

I. Baabullah dan Pengusiran Portugis dari TernateI. Baabullah and the Expulsion of the Portuguese from Ternate

Sejak awal abad ke-16, Portugis telah bercokol di Ternate lewat aliansi dagang dengan sultannya, namun hubungan itu membusuk seiring ambisi Portugis mengendalikan penuh perdagangan cengkih. Titik didihnya tiba pada 1570, ketika Portugis secara licik membunuh Sultan Hairun dari Ternate dalam sebuah perundingan yang mestinya damai. Putranya, Sultan Baabullah, bersumpah membalas dendam sekaligus mengusir Portugis selamanya dari tanah leluhurnya. Dalam pengepungan panjang yang berlangsung lima tahun (1570–1575), Baabullah berhasil memaksa seluruh garnisun Portugis menyerah dan angkat kaki dari Benteng Kastela di Ternate—salah satu kekalahan telak paling awal yang diderita kekuatan kolonial Eropa di tangan penguasa pribumi Asia, jauh mendahului perlawanan-perlawanan besar lain yang akan dikisahkan epos ini di abad-abad berikutnya. Di bawah kepemimpinannya, pengaruh Ternate meluas hingga disebut-sebut sebagai kesultanan Islam terluas di Nusantara pada masanya, membentang dari Mindanao di utara hingga Nusa Tenggara di selatan.

Since the early 16th century, the Portuguese had held a foothold in Ternate through a trade alliance with its sultan, but the relationship rotted as Portuguese ambition grew to fully control the clove trade. It came to a boil in 1570, when the Portuguese treacherously murdered Sultan Hairun of Ternate during what should have been a peaceful negotiation. His son, Sultan Baabullah, swore revenge and vowed to expel the Portuguese forever from his ancestral land. In a long five-year siege (1570–1575), Baabullah forced the entire Portuguese garrison to surrender and abandon Fort Kastela in Ternate — one of the earliest crushing defeats suffered by a European colonial power at the hands of an Asian native ruler, well ahead of the other great resistances this epic will recount in later centuries. Under his leadership, Ternate's influence spread so far it was said to be the largest Islamic sultanate in the Nusantara of its time, stretching from Mindanao in the north to Nusa Tenggara in the south.

II. Monopoli Berdarah: Hongi Tochten dan Politik Ekstirpasi VOCII. Bloody Monopoly: Hongi Tochten and the VOC's Policy of Extirpation

Kemenangan atas Portugis ternyata hanya jeda sesaat. VOC, yang datang menggantikan Portugis pada awal abad ke-17, menerapkan kontrol yang jauh lebih sistematis dan kejam atas Maluku. Untuk mempertahankan harga cengkih dan pala tetap tinggi di pasar Eropa, VOC menerapkan kebijakan ekstirpasi (extirpatie): menebang dan memusnahkan paksa pohon-pohon rempah di pulau-pulau yang dianggap "kelebihan produksi" atau berada di luar kendali monopoli mereka, memusatkan seluruh produksi cengkih dunia hanya di Ambon dan pala hanya di Banda. Penegakan kebijakan brutal ini dilakukan lewat Hongi Tochten (ekspedisi hongi)—patroli armada perahu kora-kora bersenjata yang secara rutin menyisir seluruh kepulauan Maluku, menghukum mati siapa pun yang ketahuan menanam atau memperdagangkan rempah di luar izin VOC. Di Pulau Banda sendiri, pada 1621, Gubernur Jenderal Jan Pieterszoon Coen—nama yang telah kita kenal lewat kekejamannya di Batavia—memerintahkan pembantaian hampir seluruh penduduk asli Banda yang menolak menyerahkan monopoli pala mereka, salah satu babak genosida kolonial paling awal dan paling kejam dalam sejarah Nusantara.

Victory over the Portuguese proved only a brief pause. The VOC, arriving to replace them in the early 17th century, imposed a far more systematic and brutal control over Maluku. To keep clove and nutmeg prices high in the European market, the VOC enforced a policy of extirpation (extirpatie): felling and forcibly destroying spice trees on islands deemed to have "excess production" or that lay outside their monopoly's control, concentrating the world's entire clove production in Ambon alone and nutmeg in Banda alone. This brutal policy was enforced through the Hongi Tochten (hongi expeditions) — armed kora-kora fleet patrols that routinely swept the whole Maluku archipelago, executing anyone found growing or trading spices outside VOC permission. On Banda Island itself, in 1621, Governor-General Jan Pieterszoon Coen — a name we already know from his brutality at Batavia — ordered the massacre of nearly the entire native population of Banda for refusing to surrender their nutmeg monopoly, one of the earliest and most brutal chapters of colonial genocide in Nusantara history.

III. Ayam Jantan dari Timur: Sultan Hasanuddin dan Jatuhnya MakassarIII. The Rooster of the East: Sultan Hasanuddin and the Fall of Makassar

Di selatan Sulawesi, Kesultanan Gowa-Tallo (Makassar) tumbuh menjadi bandar transit terbesar bagi rempah Maluku yang berhasil lolos dari blokade monopoli VOC—sebuah pelabuhan bebas yang secara terbuka menampung pedagang dari segala bangsa, termasuk musuh-musuh VOC seperti Inggris, Portugis, dan Denmark. Di bawah kepemimpinan Sultan Hasanuddin, yang oleh VOC sendiri dijuluki dengan penuh rasa hormat sekaligus waswas sebagai "De Haantjes van het Oosten" (Ayam Jantan dari Timur), Makassar menjadi benteng terakhir perdagangan bebas yang menolak tunduk pada logika monopoli VOC.

In southern Sulawesi, the Gowa-Tallo Sultanate (Makassar) grew into the largest transit port for Maluku spices that had slipped past the VOC's monopoly blockade — a free port openly welcoming traders of every nation, including VOC enemies such as the English, Portuguese, and Danish. Under Sultan Hasanuddin, whom the VOC itself nicknamed, with both respect and unease, "De Haantjes van het Oosten" (the Rooster of the East), Makassar became the last bastion of free trade that refused to bow to the logic of VOC monopoly.

VOC akhirnya melancarkan perang besar-besaran (1666–1669), memanfaatkan perpecahan internal kerajaan Bugis-Makassar dengan menggandeng Arung Palakka, bangsawan Bugis dari Bone yang menyimpan dendam pribadi terhadap Gowa. Setelah pengepungan panjang dan berdarah atas Benteng Somba Opu, Hasanuddin terpaksa menandatangani Perjanjian Bongaya (1667)—salah satu perjanjian paling merugikan dalam sejarah kolonial Nusantara, yang memaksa Makassar menyerahkan seluruh benteng pertahanannya, mengusir semua pedagang asing non-VOC, dan mengakui VOC sebagai satu-satunya kekuatan dagang sah di kawasan itu. Jatuhnya Makassar menandai berakhirnya era terakhir ketika sebuah pelabuhan Nusantara masih bisa berdiri sebagai pasar bebas sejati, terbuka bagi siapa pun tanpa harus tunduk pada satu kongsi dagang tunggal.

The VOC finally launched a full-scale war (1666–1669), exploiting internal divisions within the Bugis-Makassar kingdoms by allying with Arung Palakka, a Bugis noble from Bone who harboured a personal grudge against Gowa. After a long, bloody siege of Fort Somba Opu, Hasanuddin was forced to sign the Treaty of Bongaya (1667) — one of the most damaging treaties in Nusantara colonial history, forcing Makassar to surrender all its defensive forts, expel every foreign trader outside the VOC, and recognise the VOC as the region's sole legitimate trading power. Makassar's fall marked the end of the last era in which a Nusantara port could still stand as a true free market, open to anyone without submitting to a single trading company.

Perlawanan di timur tidak pernah benar-benar padam. Satu abad kemudian, Sultan Nuku dari Tidore akan kembali mengangkat senjata melawan VOC dan sekutu-sekutu Belandanya (1780–1805), membangun aliansi lintas kepulauan dengan Inggris dan kerajaan-kerajaan Papua—sebuah perjuangan panjang yang, meski akhirnya juga dipadamkan, membuktikan bahwa hasrat mempertahankan kedaulatan atas rempah sendiri tidak pernah benar-benar padam di ujung timur Nusantara, bahkan ketika Jawa di ujung barat sedang tenggelam dalam intrik-intrik keratonnya sendiri.

Resistance in the east was never truly extinguished. A century later, Sultan Nuku of Tidore would again take up arms against the VOC and its Dutch allies (1780–1805), building a cross-island alliance with the British and Papuan kingdoms — a long struggle that, though ultimately also crushed, proved that the desire to defend sovereignty over one's own spices was never truly snuffed out at the eastern edge of the Nusantara, even as Java at the western edge sank into its own court intrigues.

Tahun yang sama ketika Hasanuddin menandatangani Perjanjian Bongaya, Eropa juga sedang mengatur ulang peta kolonialnya sendiri lewat Perjanjian Breda (1667), yang mengakhiri Perang Inggris-Belanda Kedua: Inggris menyerahkan Suriname kepada Belanda, sementara Belanda menyerahkan New Amsterdam—yang kemudian diganti nama Inggris menjadi New York—kepada Inggris. Dunia pada 1667 sedang sama-sama disusun ulang oleh kongsi-kongsi dagang Eropa yang saling tukar-menukar wilayah jajahan bagai kartu, dari pesisir Amerika Utara hingga benteng-benteng Sulawesi Selatan—sebuah pengingat bahwa nasib Makassar bukan peristiwa terisolasi, melainkan satu titik kecil dalam permainan geopolitik global yang jauh lebih besar.

In the very same year Hasanuddin signed the Treaty of Bongaya, Europe was also redrawing its own colonial map through the Treaty of Breda (1667), which ended the Second Anglo-Dutch War: England ceded Suriname to the Netherlands, while the Dutch ceded New Amsterdam — soon renamed by the English as New York — to England. The world of 1667 was being reshuffled all at once by European trading powers swapping colonial territories like playing cards, from the coast of North America to the forts of South Sulawesi — a reminder that Makassar's fate was no isolated event, but one small point in a far larger game of global geopolitics.

I. Warisan yang Menggigit: Asal-Usul Paranoia Sang Penerus TakhtaI. A Biting Inheritance: The Origins of a Successor's Paranoia

Kematian Sultan Agung pada tahun 1645 M mewariskan takhta kepada putranya, Raden Mas Sayyidin, yang kelak bergelar Amangkurat I (memerintah 1646–1677 M). Namun yang diwarisinya bukan sekadar mahkota, melainkan juga bayang-bayang raksasa dan kas kerajaan yang nyaris kosong. Sultan Agung meninggalkan legenda kebesaran—penakluk hampir seluruh Jawa, pemegang gelar Sultan langsung dari Mekkah—tetapi juga dua kekalahan memalukan dalam pengepungan Batavia (1628 dan 1629) yang menguras ribuan nyawa prajurit dan lumbung pangan tanpa hasil, serta perang penaklukan tanpa henti yang membuat kas dan tenaga rakyat Mataram keropos dari dalam.

The death of Sultan Agung in 1645 CE passed the throne to his son, Raden Mas Sayyidin, later titled Amangkurat I (reigned 1646–1677 CE). But what he inherited was not merely a crown, but also a giant's shadow and a treasury nearly empty. Sultan Agung left behind a legend of greatness — conqueror of nearly all of Java, holder of the title Sultan granted directly from Mecca — but also two humiliating defeats in the sieges of Batavia (1628 and 1629), which drained thousands of soldiers' lives and food stores for nothing, and an unending war of conquest that hollowed out Mataram's treasury and manpower from within.

Amangkurat I naik takhta tanpa modal kharisma militer maupun kewibawaan spiritual sebesar ayahnya. Ia mewarisi ambisi sentralisasi kekuasaan absolut ala Sultan Agung, tetapi tanpa legitimasi yang menyertainya. Dari titik lemah inilah tumbuh sebuah watak yang akan mendefinisikan seluruh masa pemerintahannya: kecurigaan yang nyaris patologis terhadap siapa pun yang dianggap berpotensi merebut atau menandingi kekuasaannya—baik itu ulama yang memiliki basis massa keagamaan sendiri, adipati pesisir yang memiliki kemandirian ekonomi dari jalur dagang leluhur Demak, maupun, yang paling tragis, saudaranya sendiri.

Amangkurat I ascended the throne without the military charisma or spiritual authority his father commanded. He inherited Sultan Agung's ambition for absolute centralised power, but without the legitimacy that had accompanied it. From this point of weakness grew a trait that would define his entire reign: an almost pathological suspicion toward anyone judged capable of seizing or rivalling his power — whether religious scholars with their own mass following, coastal dukes with economic independence inherited from Demak's trade routes, or, most tragically, his own brother.

II. Perselisihan dengan Pangeran Alit dan Pemberontakan yang GagalII. The Conflict with Pangeran Alit and a Failed Rebellion

Menurut Babad Tanah Jawi, adik tiri Amangkurat I, Pangeran Alit, adalah sosok yang jauh lebih dicintai rakyat dan kalangan santri-ulama dibanding sang kakak. Ia dikenal saleh, rendah hati, dan dekat dengan jaringan pesantren serta guru-guru agama di seantero Mataram—sebuah kedekatan yang, bagi seorang raja yang haus kepastian atas kekuasaannya, terasa lebih seperti ancaman ketimbang kebanggaan keluarga. Konon perselisihan meletus dari persoalan yang tampak sepele di mata istana—sebuah pelanggaran tata krama, sebuah tuduhan, sebuah gosip—namun berakar jauh lebih dalam pada kecemburuan kekuasaan yang telah lama membara.

According to Babad Tanah Jawi, Amangkurat I's half-brother, Pangeran Alit, was a figure far more beloved by the people and by the santri-ulama community than his older brother. He was known as pious, humble, and close to the network of pesantren and religious teachers across Mataram — a closeness that, to a king hungry for certainty over his own power, felt more like a threat than a source of family pride. It is said the conflict erupted from a matter that seemed trivial at court — a breach of etiquette, an accusation, a rumour — but rooted far deeper in a jealousy over power that had long been simmering.

Versi-versi babad berbeda-beda dalam merinci detailnya: sebagian menyebut perkelahian terbuka di dalam keraton yang berujung pada kematian Pangeran Alit di tangan kakaknya sendiri, sebagian lain menyebut eksekusi yang lebih terencana. Yang lebih dapat dipastikan adalah dampaknya: kematian Pangeran Alit memicu gelombang kemarahan di kalangan santri dan ulama yang selama ini menaruh harapan padanya. Desas-desus tentang rencana pemberontakan balas dendam dari lingkaran pendukung Pangeran Alit sampai ke telinga istana—sebuah pemberontakan yang gagal terwujud karena keburu tercium dan ditumpas dalam benihnya sendiri. Namun bagi Amangkurat I, ancaman yang gagal ini justru menjadi pembenaran sempurna untuk melakukan sesuatu yang jauh lebih besar dan lebih mengerikan: membasmi tuntas seluruh jaringan ulama yang dianggapnya sebagai sumber ancaman abadi terhadap takhta.

Versions of the babad differ on the details: some describe an open brawl inside the palace ending in Pangeran Alit's death at his own brother's hand, others a more premeditated execution. What is more certain is the aftermath: Pangeran Alit's death triggered a wave of anger among the santri and ulama who had long placed their hopes in him. Rumours of a planned revenge rebellion from Pangeran Alit's circle of supporters reached the palace's ears — a rebellion that failed to materialise because it was uncovered and crushed while still in its infancy. But for Amangkurat I, this failed threat became the perfect justification for something far larger and more terrible: the wholesale destruction of the entire ulama network he saw as an eternal source of threat to his throne.

III. Putusnya Hubungan dengan Ulama: Horor Pembantaian di Alun-Alun PleredIII. The Rupture with the Ulama: The Horror of the Plered Square Massacre

Dalam waktu kurang dari dua jam di suatu pagi tahun 1648 M, tembakan meriam memberi sinyal kepada prajurit keraton untuk membantai—menurut catatan babad yang jumlahnya diperdebatkan sejarawan modern—lebih dari 5.000 ulama beserta anak istri mereka di Alun-Alun Plered. Peristiwa ini bukan sekadar ledakan amarah sesaat, melainkan puncak dari kecurigaan yang telah lama dipupuk Amangkurat I terhadap otoritas keagamaan yang berjalan sejajar—dan kadang lebih dihormati rakyat—dibanding otoritas kerajaan itu sendiri.

In under two hours one morning in 1648 CE, a cannon shot signalled palace soldiers to massacre — according to babad accounts whose numbers modern historians dispute — more than 5,000 ulama along with their wives and children at Plered Square. This was no mere momentary outburst of rage, but the culmination of a suspicion Amangkurat I had long nursed toward a religious authority that ran parallel to — and was sometimes more respected by the people than — the authority of the throne itself.

Dampaknya jauh melampaui hitungan korban jiwa. Pembantaian ini secara efektif memutus ikatan berabad-abad antara takhta dan jaringan ulama yang sejak era Wali Sanga dan Kesultanan Demak menjadi salah satu pilar legitimasi kekuasaan Jawa Islam. Guru-guru agama yang selamat memilih bersembunyi atau menjauh dari lingkaran istana; kepercayaan rakyat kepada raja sebagai pelindung sekaligus penjaga keimanan runtuh seketika. Mataram di bawah Amangkurat I kehilangan salah satu sumber legitimasi moralnya yang paling penting—sebuah kekosongan yang, tiga dekade kemudian, akan diisi oleh kemarahan rakyat yang berpindah dukungan kepada siapa pun yang berani melawan sang raja.

Its impact far exceeded the death toll. This massacre effectively severed a centuries-old bond between the throne and the ulama network that, since the era of the Wali Sanga and the Demak Sultanate, had been one of the pillars of Islamic-Javanese royal legitimacy. Surviving religious teachers chose to hide or distance themselves from court circles; the people's trust in the king as both protector and guardian of the faith collapsed instantly. Mataram under Amangkurat I lost one of its most important sources of moral legitimacy — a void that, three decades later, would be filled by popular anger shifting its support to anyone bold enough to defy the king.

IV. Penutupan Pelabuhan dan Kesengsaraan di Pesisir UtaraIV. The Closure of the Ports and Suffering on the North Coast

Jika pembantaian ulama memutus urat nadi spiritual kerajaan, kebijakan berikutnya memutus urat nadi ekonominya sendiri. Amangkurat I memandang para adipati pesisir utara—pewaris kejayaan maritim Demak dan Pati Unus yang telah ditelusuri sejak Bab 8B—sebagai pusat kekuasaan tandingan yang berbahaya: mereka memiliki kapal, kekayaan dari perdagangan internasional, dan jaringan yang independen dari istana pedalaman. Untuk menjinakkan mereka, Amangkurat I memerintahkan pembatasan ketat, bahkan penutupan paksa sejumlah pelabuhan strategis di pesisir utara Jawa, termasuk galangan kapal bersejarah Jepara yang dahulu pernah melahirkan jung-jung raksasa Pati Unus.

If the massacre of the ulama severed the kingdom's spiritual lifeline, the next policy severed its own economic one. Amangkurat I viewed the north-coast dukes — heirs to the maritime glory of Demak and Pati Unus traced back in Chapter 8B — as a dangerous rival centre of power: they had ships, wealth from international trade, and networks independent of the inland court. To tame them, Amangkurat I ordered strict restrictions, even the forced closure of a number of strategic ports along Java's north coast, including the historic Jepara shipyard that had once given birth to Pati Unus's giant jung.

Kebijakan ini secara sistematis mencabut sumber penghidupan puluhan ribu nelayan, pelaut, saudagar, dan pengrajin kapal di sepanjang pesisir. Kota-kota pelabuhan yang dahulu ramai oleh kapal dagang dari Tiongkok, India, dan Timur Tengah perlahan sepi dan miskin. Sebagian penduduk dipaksa pindah ke pedalaman untuk bekerja di lahan-lahan pertanian di bawah pengawasan langsung istana, sementara ekonomi maritim yang telah dibangun sejak era Sriwijaya dan Majapahit dibiarkan layu demi satu tujuan tunggal: memastikan tidak ada kekuatan ekonomi pesisir yang cukup besar untuk menandingi kekuasaan absolut Plered. Kesengsaraan yang timbul—kelaparan, pengangguran massal, dan migrasi paksa—menjadi bara dalam sekam yang menunggu percikan untuk menyala.

This policy systematically stripped away the livelihoods of tens of thousands of fishermen, sailors, merchants, and shipwrights along the coast. Port towns once bustling with trading ships from China, India, and the Middle East grew quiet and poor. Some residents were forced inland to work farmland under the court's direct supervision, while the maritime economy built since the eras of Srivijaya and Majapahit was left to wither for a single purpose: ensuring no coastal economic power grew large enough to rival Plered's absolute rule. The resulting suffering — famine, mass unemployment, and forced migration — became embers under ash, waiting for a spark to catch fire.

V. Pemberontakan Trunajaya dan Kejatuhan KeratonV. The Trunajaya Rebellion and the Fall of the Court

Akumulasi kekejaman dan kesengsaraan inilah yang akhirnya memicu pemberontakan berskala besar yang dipimpin oleh bangsawan Madura, Trunajaya, yang didukung oleh sisa-sisa pasukan Makassar pimpinan Kraeng Galesong. Rakyat pesisir yang kehilangan mata pencaharian, jaringan santri yang kehilangan guru dan sanak keluarga mereka di Plered, dan para bangsawan daerah yang muak dengan tirani pusat, menemukan dalam Trunajaya sebuah saluran untuk membalas dendam kolektif. Pada pertengahan 1677 M, pasukan Trunajaya menembus benteng Plered. Keraton dijarah habis, dan peti harta karun keagungan Mataram disita. Amangkurat I yang sekarat melarikan diri ke arah barat ditemani putra mahkotanya (Amangkurat II) sebelum tewas tragis di Tegalwangi.

This accumulation of cruelty and suffering finally sparked a large-scale rebellion led by the Madurese noble Trunajaya, backed by the remnants of Makassarese forces under Kraeng Galesong. Coastal people who had lost their livelihoods, santri networks who had lost their teachers and kin at Plered, and regional nobles sick of central tyranny all found in Trunajaya an outlet for collective revenge. In mid-1677 CE, Trunajaya's forces breached the Plered fortress. The court was thoroughly looted, and the treasure chests of Mataram's grandeur were seized. A dying Amangkurat I fled westward, accompanied by his crown prince (Amangkurat II), before dying tragically at Tegalwangi.

Putra mahkota yang melarikan diri tidak memiliki pilihan lain selain menjual kedaulatan ekonomi dan teritorial Mataram kepada VOC demi merebut kembali takhtanya. Melalui Perjanjian 1677, VOC bersedia menumpas pemberontakan Trunajaya dengan imbalan monopoli perdagangan tekstil dan candu, penguasaan pelabuhan-pelabuhan pesisir utara, serta ganti rugi biaya perang yang mencekik kas keraton. Dengan demikian, lingkaran kekejaman yang dimulai dari paranoia seorang raja terhadap adik dan ulamanya sendiri berakhir pada sesuatu yang jauh lebih menentukan nasib bangsa: pintu gerbang yang dibuka lebar-lebar bagi kekuasaan asing untuk pertama kalinya benar-benar berdiri di atas tanah pedalaman Jawa.

The fleeing crown prince had no choice but to sell Mataram's economic and territorial sovereignty to the VOC in exchange for reclaiming his throne. Through the Treaty of 1677, the VOC agreed to crush the Trunajaya rebellion in return for a monopoly on the textile and opium trade, control of the north-coast ports, and war reparations that strangled the court's treasury. Thus the cycle of cruelty that began with one king's paranoia toward his own brother and his own ulama ended in something far more decisive for the nation's fate: a gateway thrown wide open for a foreign power to, for the first time, truly plant itself on the inland soil of Java.

Di dua ujung dunia yang berbeda, gagasan tentang kekuasaan raja yang tak tersentuh sedang sama-sama diuji: satu oleh pedang paranoid seorang raja terhadap rakyatnya, satu lagi oleh pedang rakyat yang berbalik kepada rajanya.

Amangkurat I's massacre of thousands of ulama at Plered Square (1648) fell almost exactly at the peak of the English Civil War: in January 1649, King Charles I of England was publicly beheaded by order of a parliament led by Oliver Cromwell — for the first time in modern European history, a reigning king was tried and executed by his own people. At two opposite ends of the world, the idea of untouchable royal power was being tested in the same age: in one place by the paranoid sword of a king turned against his people, in the other by the sword of a people turned against their king.

Sebelum namanya menggemakan ketakutan di Batavia, Surapati hanyalah seorang anak budak asal Bali bernama Si Untung. Dijual oleh majikannya kepada pejabat Kompeni di Batavia, Pieter Cnoll, Surapati muda tumbuh di tengah lingkungan Eropa yang angkuh. Before his name echoed with fear through Batavia, Surapati was merely an enslaved Balinese child known as Si Untung. Sold by his owner to Company official Pieter Cnoll in Batavia, the young Surapati grew up amid an arrogant European household. But racial mistreatment and the bitter trials of life kindled his rebellious spirit. After a forbidden love affair with Suzanne Cnoll — his owner's daughter — Untung was imprisoned and tortured.

His escape from the prison of Kasteel Batavia began his transformation from fugitive to charismatic guerrilla leader. Together with a band of Balinese and Javanese runaways, he ambushed Company patrols on the outskirts of Batavia. His conflict with the Company came to a head when VOC soldiers under Pangeran Gugur and Officer Kuffeler treated him with contempt during a battle escorting Cirebon nobility. Surapati struck back: he led a massacre of Company troops at a river mouth, then fled east toward the heart of Mataram.

III. Dari Rempah ke Komoditas Emas: Kopi, Tebu, Teh, dan TembakauIII. From Spice to Golden Commodity: Coffee, Sugar, Tea, and Tobacco

Sebelum kopi menjadi primadona, lada (Piper nigrum) adalah "mata uang" transisi yang menjembatani era rempah Maluku dengan era komoditas perkebunan Jawa. Sepanjang abad ke-16 dan ke-17, lada dari Banten, Aceh, dan Lampung menjadi rebutan setiap kongsi dagang Eropa yang singgah di Nusantara—komoditas yang, tidak seperti cengkih dan pala yang hanya tumbuh di Maluku, bisa dibudidayakan lebih luas dan lebih sulit dimonopoli sepenuhnya oleh satu kekuatan kolonial. Namun menjelang akhir abad ke-17, pasar lada dunia mulai jenuh—terlalu banyak pemasok, harga jatuh—sementara di Eropa, sebuah minuman hitam pahit dari Yaman perlahan berubah dari obat eksotis menjadi candu sosial kelas atas. VOC, yang membaca pergeseran selera ini lebih cepat daripada pesaingnya, mengambil keputusan yang akan mengubah wajah pedalaman Jawa selamanya: berhenti hanya mengandalkan pelabuhan pesisir, dan mulai menaklukkan pegunungan.

Before coffee became the star commodity, pepper (Piper nigrum) was the transitional "currency" bridging the Maluku spice era with the Java plantation commodity era. Through the 16th and 17th centuries, pepper from Banten, Aceh, and Lampung was fought over by every European trading company that called at the Nusantara — a commodity that, unlike cloves and nutmeg which grew only in Maluku, could be cultivated more widely and was harder for any single colonial power to fully monopolise. But toward the end of the 17th century, the world pepper market grew saturated — too many suppliers, falling prices — while in Europe, a bitter black drink from Yemen was slowly transforming from an exotic medicine into an upper-class social addiction. The VOC, reading this shift in taste faster than its rivals, made a decision that would change the face of inland Java forever: stop relying solely on coastal ports, and start conquering the mountains.

Jejak Komoditas — Spionase Biji Kopi Pertama di DuniaCommodity Trail — The World's First Coffee-Bean Espionage

Kisah bagaimana kopi bisa tumbuh di Jawa dimulai dari sebuah aksi spionase biologis yang nyaris tak tercatat dalam sejarah populer. Pada 1616, seorang pedagang VOC bernama Pieter van den Broecke menyelundupkan beberapa biji dan bibit pohon kopi dari Pelabuhan Mocha di Yaman—satu-satunya sumber kopi dunia saat itu, yang dijaga ketat oleh penguasa Arab agar tak ada satu pun bibit hidup keluar dari jazirah mereka. Bibit curian itu pertama kali didomestikasi di rumah kaca Hortus Botanicus di Amsterdam sebagai tanaman percobaan, sebelum akhirnya berhasil ditanam dan berbuah di dataran tinggi Jawa pada 1696. Dalam waktu kurang dari satu abad, monopoli kopi dunia yang semula sepenuhnya berada di tangan Yaman berpindah ke pelabuhan-pelabuhan VOC—sebuah pembajakan bio-komoditas yang, ironisnya, akan terulang persis dua abad kemudian pada komoditas teh, namun kali ini Belanda yang menjadi korbannya.

The story of how coffee came to grow in Java begins with an act of biological espionage almost forgotten in popular history. In 1616, a VOC trader named Pieter van den Broecke smuggled several coffee beans and saplings out of the port of Mocha in Yemen — the world's sole source of coffee at the time, guarded jealously by Arab rulers to keep a single living seedling from ever leaving their peninsula. The stolen seedlings were first domesticated as experimental plants in the Hortus Botanicus greenhouse in Amsterdam, before finally being successfully planted and fruiting in the highlands of Java in 1696. In less than a century, the world coffee monopoly, once entirely in Yemen's hands, had shifted to the VOC's ports — a bio-commodity piracy that, ironically, would repeat almost exactly two centuries later with tea, only this time with the Dutch as the victim.

Ekspansi kopi ke pedalaman memerlukan infrastruktur yang belum pernah ada di Jawa sebelumnya. Di dataran tinggi Priangan, VOC—dan kelak pemerintah kolonial Hindia Belanda—menerapkan sistem Preangerstelsel: kewajiban tanam kopi bagi setiap kepala keluarga pribumi, cikal-bakal langsung dari kekejaman sistematis Cultuurstelsel yang akan melanda seluruh Jawa satu abad kemudian. Untuk mengangkut hasil panen dari pegunungan terpencil ke pelabuhan, Gubernur Jenderal Herman Willem Daendels—tokoh yang telah kita kenal lewat kekejamannya membangun benteng-benteng pertahanan Jawa—memerintahkan pembangunan Jalan Raya Pos (De Grote Postweg) sepanjang lebih dari seribu kilometer dari Anyer hingga Panarukan, dikerjakan dengan kerja rodi yang menelan puluhan ribu nyawa rakyat Jawa, namun sekaligus untuk pertama kalinya menyatukan pulau ini dengan satu jalur darat yang bisa dilalui dari ujung ke ujung.

Coffee's expansion into the interior required infrastructure Java had never had before. Di balik kekejaman sistem tanam paksa ini, rakyat Priangan justru menemukan celah kreatif untuk bertahan dan bahkan menciptakan legenda kulinernya sendiri. Jauh di seberang pulau, di ranah Minangkabau Sumatra Barat, budaya kopi berkembang dengan wajah yang berbeda sama sekali lewat tradisi Aia Kawa—seduhan daun kopi (bukan bijinya) yang diminum turun-temurun sebagai penghangat tubuh dan pengikat silaturahmi warga surau, sebuah pengingat bahwa bahkan dalam bayang-bayang monopoli kolonial yang paling ketat, kreativitas kuliner rakyat selalu menemukan jalannya sendiri.

Behind the cruelty of this forced-cultivation system, the people of Priangan found a creative loophole to survive, and even created their own culinary legend. Dalam Babad Diponegoro (ditulis oleh Peter Carey dalam The Power of Prophecy), Diponegoro menggambarkan panggilannya melalui penglihatan spiritual di Gua Selarong dari Ratu Adil (Erucakra) untuk memimpin perang suci melawan Kafir Siwer dan memulihkan tatanan Islam-Jawa. Tradisi lisan Yogyakarta juga mewariskan kisah bahwa, sebagaimana leluhurnya Panembahan Senopati dan Sultan Agung berabad-abad sebelumnya, Diponegoro menerima pertemuan ghaib dengan Ratu Kidul, penguasa Laut Selatan, yang menjanjikan restunya bagi perjuangan sang pangeran—sebuah klaim legitimasi kosmologis yang menyambungkan perang ini langsung ke mitos pendirian dinasti Mataram itu sendiri. Ia juga dikaitkan dengan dua pusaka legendaris yang menyertainya sepanjang perang: kuda putih bernama Kyai Gentayu dan tombak pusaka Kyai Rondhan, keduanya diyakini rakyat memiliki kekuatan yang melindungi sang pangeran dari peluru musuh.

In Babad Diponegoro (as recorded by Peter Carey in The Power of Prophecy), Diponegoro describes his calling through a spiritual vision at Selarong Cave from the Ratu Adil (Erucakra) to lead a holy war against the Kafir Siwer and restore the Islamic-Javanese order. Yogyakarta oral tradition also passes down the story that, like his ancestors Panembahan Senopati and Sultan Agung centuries before, Diponegoro received an unseen meeting with Ratu Kidul, the ruler of the Southern Sea, who promised her blessing for the prince's struggle — a claim of cosmological legitimacy directly linking this war to the very founding myth of the Mataram dynasty. He is also associated with two legendary heirlooms that accompanied him throughout the war: a white horse named Kyai Gentayu and a sacred spear, Kyai Rondhan, both believed by the people to hold powers that protected the prince from enemy bullets.

Perang ini menjadi perang paling berdarah dan mahal dalam sejarah kolonial Belanda. This war became the bloodiest and most costly in Dutch colonial history. Diponegoro used guerrilla tactics fully backed by the common people and religious leaders (such as Kyai Mojo and Sentot Alibasya Prawirodirjo). Perang berakhir pada 28 Maret 1830 ketika Diponegoro dijebak dalam perundingan damai yang penuh tipu muslihat di rumah Residen Magelang dan langsung ditangkap. Perang Jawa menelan korban jiwa sedikitnya 200.000 rakyat Jawa dan 15.000 serdadu Belanda serta menghancurkan keuangan kas kolonial.

The war ended on 28 March 1830 when Diponegoro was lured into deceitful peace negotiations at the Magelang Resident's house and immediately arrested. The Java War claimed the lives of at least 200,000 Javanese and 15,000 Dutch soldiers, and shattered the colonial treasury's finances.

Kisah penangkapan di Magelang menjadi salah satu momen paling dikenang dalam memori kolektif Jawa: Jenderal de Kock, yang telah berjanji akan berunding dengan aman, justru mengepung rumah pertemuan dengan pasukan bersenjata begitu Diponegoro melangkah masuk. Menurut catatan pribadi Diponegoro sendiri yang ditulis dalam pengasingan, ia sempat menghunus keris sesaat sebelum ditangkap, namun memilih menyerah demi menghindari pertumpahan darah lebih lanjut di antara para pengikutnya yang mengelilingi gedung. Ia diasingkan berpindah-pindah—dari Batavia ke Manado, lalu akhirnya ke Makassar—hingga wafat di Benteng Rotterdam pada 1855, jauh dari tanah leluhurnya. Namun kepergiannya secara fisik tak pernah benar-benar mengakhiri kehadirannya dalam ingatan rakyat: nama Diponegoro kelak diabadikan sebagai nama jalan protokol di hampir setiap kota di Indonesia, universitas, dan Komando Daerah Militer, sementara di kampung-kampung Jawa Tengah, kisah Perang Jawa terus dituturkan turun-temurun sebagai bukti bahwa perlawanan terhadap penindasan—sekalipun berakhir dengan penangkapan, bukan kemenangan—tetap dapat menjadi fondasi kehormatan bagi generasi berikutnya.

The story of the Magelang capture became one of the most remembered moments in Javanese collective memory: General de Kock, who had promised safe negotiations, instead surrounded the meeting house with armed troops the moment Diponegoro stepped inside. According to Diponegoro's own personal account written in exile, he briefly drew his keris just before his arrest, but chose to surrender to avoid further bloodshed among his followers surrounding the building. He was exiled from place to place — from Batavia to Manado, and finally to Makassar — until his death at Fort Rotterdam in 1855, far from his ancestral land. Yet his physical departure never truly ended his presence in the people's memory: the name Diponegoro would later be immortalised as the name of a main avenue in nearly every city in Indonesia, of a university, and of a Regional Military Command, while in the villages of Central Java, the story of the Java War continued to be told from generation to generation as proof that resistance against oppression — even one ending in capture rather than victory — can still become a foundation of honour for the generations that follow.

Cultuurstelsel: Air Mata dan Timbunan GuldenThe Cultivation System: Tears and Mountains of Guilders

Untuk menutupi kebangkrutan kas negara akibat Perang Jawa dan Perang Kemerdekaan Belgia, Gubernur Jenderal Johannes van den Bosch memperkenalkan sistem Cultuurstelsel (Tanam Paksa) pada tahun 1830 M. Petani Jawa dipaksa menanam komoditas ekspor pasar Eropa—terutama kopi, tebu, nila, dan tembakau—di sebagian tanah mereka atau menyerahkan tenaga kerja gratis selama berbulan-bulan.

To cover the state's bankrupt treasury following the Java War and the Belgian War of Independence, Governor-General Johannes van den Bosch introduced the Cultuurstelsel (Forced Cultivation System) in 1830 CE. Javanese farmers were forced to grow European export commodities — chiefly coffee, sugarcane, indigo, and tobacco — on part of their land, or to surrender months of unpaid labour.

Laporan resmi Colonial Office di Den Haag mencatat bahwa Cultuurstelsel menghasilkan Batig Slot (laba bersih) ratusan juta gulden yang membangun infrastruktur rel kereta api dan melunasi utang Kerajaan Belanda. Official Colonial Office reports in The Hague record that the Cultuurstelsel generated a Batig Slot (net profit) of hundreds of millions of guilders, which built railway infrastructure and paid off the Kingdom of the Netherlands' debts. But in Java, this exploitation triggered horrific systematic famine, such as that in Demak and Grobogan in 1849–1850 CE, where thousands of villages emptied out and corpses lay scattered along the roadsides.

Kelaparan di Demak dan Grobogan bukan tragedi tunggal di abad kolonial. Pada waktu yang hampir bersamaan, Bencana Kelaparan Besar Irlandia (1845–1852) — dipicu gagal panen kentang di bawah kekuasaan kolonial Inggris — menewaskan sekitar satu juta orang dan memaksa jutaan lainnya bermigrasi. The famine in Demak and Grobogan was not an isolated tragedy of the colonial century. At nearly the same time, the Great Irish Famine (1845–1852) — triggered by potato crop failure under British colonial rule — killed roughly a million people and forced millions more to migrate. Meanwhile in Britain, the Industrial Revolution was giving birth to railways, steam-powered textile mills, and the wealth of metropolitan cities — prosperity that, from Java's sugar fields as much as Ireland's potato fields, was partly built on colonial suffering.

Right in the midst of Cultuurstelsel's suffering, another act of espionage was unfolding in a very different corner of the world — and this time, it was the Dutch who found themselves the ones robbed, not the robbers. In 1848, a Scottish botanist named Robert Fortune, hired by the British East India Company, infiltrated deep into the interior of Qing-dynasty China disguised as a Mandarin merchant — head shaved, a false queue attached — to penetrate territory tightly closed to foreigners. He successfully smuggled out roughly 20,000 Camellia sinensis (tea plant) seedlings and seeds, along with the secret knowledge of their processing technique, carrying them to Assam, India — mirroring precisely what Pieter van den Broecke had done with Yemeni coffee two centuries earlier, but on a far greater industrial scale.

Sejak saat itu, dua rute dagang komoditas global saling berpacu memperebutkan meja-meja Eropa: jalur Java–Amsterdam yang mengangkut kopi lewat birokrasi perkebunan teritorial VOC yang lamban namun mapan, melawan jalur Canton–London yang mengangkut teh lewat balapan kapal Clipper tercepat di dunia—termasuk yang paling legendaris, Cutty Sark—di mana kapal pertama yang tiba di London dengan teh musim pertama meraih harga jual tertinggi. Kompetisi berkecepatan tinggi dan bertaruh nyawa inilah yang, secara tak terduga, ikut melahirkan industri asuransi maritim modern seperti Lloyd's of London—karena setiap kapal yang berlomba menerjang badai demi kecepatan membutuhkan seseorang yang bersedia menanggung risiko jika ia karam di tengah jalan.

From then on, two global commodity trade routes raced to win Europe's tables: the Java–Amsterdam route, carrying coffee through the slow but well-established territorial plantation bureaucracy of the VOC, against the Canton–London route, carrying tea aboard the fastest Clipper ships in the world — including the most legendary of all, the Cutty Sark — where the first ship to reach London with the season's first tea fetched the highest price. This high-speed, life-risking competition unexpectedly helped give birth to the modern maritime insurance industry, such as Lloyd's of London — because every ship racing through storms for the sake of speed needed someone willing to bear the risk if it sank along the way.

Etis, Kereta Api, dan Penguasa tanpa MahkotaThe Ethical Policy, the Railway, and the Uncrowned King

By the late 19th century, moral pressure from liberal critics in the Netherlands (such as Multatuli/Eduard Douwes Dekker through his novel Max Havelaar, 1860) pushed for the introduction of the Ethical Policy (1901), resting on three pillars: Education, Irrigation, and Emigration.

Masuknya modal swasta Eropa mengubah lanskap fisik Jawa. Jaringan rel kereta api (Nederlandsch-Indische Spoorweg Maatschappij) membelah lembah-lembah kuno tempat kerajaan Sanjaya dan Mataram pernah berdiri. Keraton Surakarta dan Yogyakarta secara bertahap dilumpuhkan kekuatan politisnya, diubah menjadi pusat pelestarian kebudayaan (museum hidup) yang diawasi ketat oleh Residen Belanda. Birokrasi Pangreh Praja tradisional diubah menjadi pamong praja kolonial berpakaian seragam. The influx of private European capital reshaped Java's physical landscape. The railway network (Nederlandsch-Indische Spoorweg Maatschappij) cut through the ancient valleys where the kingdoms of Sanjaya and Mataram once stood. The courts of Surakarta and Yogyakarta had their political power steadily neutralised, transformed into centres of cultural preservation (a living museum) closely watched by the Dutch Resident. The traditional Pangreh Praja bureaucracy became a uniformed colonial civil service. Yet from its new schools (such as STOVIA) was born an educated Bumi Putera class that would later lead the modern nationalist movement.

Di tengah berlakunya Politik Etis, sebuah peristiwa jauh di utara mengguncang seluruh kesadaran kolonial Asia: dalam Perang Rusia-Jepang (1904–1905), Jepang — sebuah negara Asia yang beberapa dekade sebelumnya masih tertutup dari dunia luar — mengalahkan telak Kekaisaran Rusia, sebuah kekuatan besar Eropa. Untuk pertama kalinya dalam era modern, bangsa Asia mengalahkan bangsa Eropa dalam perang terbuka. Berita ini menjalar cepat ke seluruh Hindia Belanda, ikut membakar semangat kaum terpelajar Bumi Putera bahwa dominasi kulit putih bukanlah takdir abadi. Tiga puluh tujuh tahun kemudian, bangsa Asia yang sama itu akan mendarat di pantai utara Jawa dan meruntuhkan tatanan kolonial dalam hitungan hari.

In the midst of the Ethical Policy era, an event far to the north shook the whole of Asia's colonial consciousness: in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905), Japan — an Asian nation that only decades earlier had been closed to the outside world — decisively defeated the Russian Empire, a great European power. For the first time in the modern era, an Asian nation had beaten a European one in open war. The news spread quickly across the Dutch East Indies, helping to kindle among the educated Bumi Putera a belief that white domination was not an eternal fate. Thirty-seven years later, that same Asian nation would land on the north coast of Java and topple the colonial order within days.

III. Emigrasi yang Sesungguhnya: Koeli Kontrak dari Deli hingga SurinameIII. Emigration in Truth: Contract Coolies from Deli to Suriname

Dari tiga pilar Politik Etis—Edukasi, Irigasi, Emigrasi—pilar terakhir inilah yang paling jarang dibicarakan dengan jujur, sebab di baliknya tersembunyi salah satu babak paling kelam dari eksploitasi tenaga kerja Hindia Belanda. "Emigrasi" dalam bahasa resmi kolonial berarti pemindahan penduduk untuk meringankan kepadatan Jawa; dalam praktiknya, ia sering berarti pengapalan ribuan orang miskin desa ke perkebunan tembakau dan karet di Sumatra Timur (Deli), bahkan hingga ke koloni Belanda yang jauh di benua lain: Suriname, di pesisir utara Amerika Selatan.

Of the Ethical Policy's three pillars — Education, Irrigation, Emigration — this last pillar is the one least honestly discussed, for hidden behind it lies one of the darkest chapters of labour exploitation in the Dutch East Indies. "Emigration" in official colonial language meant relocating population to ease Java's overcrowding; in practice, it often meant shipping thousands of poor villagers to tobacco and rubber plantations in East Sumatra (Deli), and even as far as a Dutch colony on another continent entirely: Suriname, on the north coast of South America.

Sejak pertengahan abad ke-19, tanah perkebunan Deli tumbuh pesat menjadi salah satu sumber devisa terbesar Hindia Belanda berkat tembakau berkualitas tinggi yang laris di pasar cerutu Eropa. Kebutuhan tenaga kerja murah dalam jumlah masif ini dipenuhi melalui sistem koeli kontrak: para calo perekrut (werver) berkeliling desa-desa miskin Jawa Tengah dan Jawa Timur, menawarkan uang muka, janji upah tinggi, dan kehidupan baru di "tanah seberang." Banyak yang berangkat karena terlilit utang atau tertipu bujuk rayu, tanpa benar-benar memahami isi kontrak yang mereka tanda tangani—kontrak yang, berkat Koelie Ordonnantie (Ordonansi Kuli) dan klausul Poenale Sanctie di dalamnya, mengikat mereka secara hukum pidana: pekerja yang melarikan diri atau menolak bekerja dapat dipenjara atau dicambuk, sementara pelanggaran oleh pihak perkebunan nyaris tak pernah ditindak.

From the mid-19th century, the Deli plantation lands grew rapidly into one of the Dutch East Indies' largest sources of foreign exchange, thanks to high-quality tobacco that sold briskly in Europe's cigar-wrapper market. This need for vast amounts of cheap labour was met through the contract coolie system: recruiting agents (werver) toured the poor villages of Central and East Java, offering cash advances, promises of high wages, and a new life "across the sea." Many left because they were trapped in debt or lured by false promises, without truly understanding the contracts they signed — contracts that, thanks to the Koelie Ordonnantie (Coolie Ordinance) and its Poenale Sanctie (Penal Sanction) clause, bound them under criminal law: workers who fled or refused to work could be imprisoned or whipped, while violations by the plantations themselves were almost never punished.

Laporan Rhemrev (1902–1904), hasil penyelidikan seorang inspektur kolonial yang dikirim menyelidiki kondisi buruh Deli, mengungkap kekerasan sistematis yang terjadi di balik kebun-kebun tembakau yang menghasilkan laba besar bagi maskapai-maskapai Belanda: hukuman fisik brutal, kematian akibat penyakit dan kelelahan kerja, serta angka kematian pekerja yang sangat tinggi—sebuah kenyataan yang sangat kontras dengan slogan "politik balas budi" yang menjadi dasar filosofis Politik Etis itu sendiri.

The Rhemrev Report (1902–1904), the findings of a colonial inspector sent to investigate labour conditions in Deli, exposed the systematic violence occurring behind the tobacco estates that generated large profits for Dutch companies: brutal physical punishment, deaths from disease and exhaustion, and an extremely high worker mortality rate — a reality starkly at odds with the "debt of honour" slogan that formed the philosophical basis of the Ethical Policy itself.

Nasib yang sama, bahkan lebih jauh dari tanah kelahiran, menimpa para koeli yang dikirim ke Suriname. Sejak tahun 1890 hingga 1939, sedikitnya 32.000 orang Jawa diangkut lewat pelayaran berbulan-bulan menyeberangi dua samudra untuk bekerja di perkebunan gula dan kopi Suriname, mengisi kekosongan tenaga kerja setelah penghapusan perbudakan di koloni itu pada 1863. Banyak dari mereka tidak pernah kembali ke Jawa; sebagian menikah, beranak-pinak, dan menetap di tanah baru itu hingga membentuk komunitas Jawa-Suriname yang bertahan sampai hari ini—kini dikenal sebagai salah satu kelompok etnis terbesar di Suriname, menjaga bahasa, wayang, dan gamelan Jawa di belahan bumi yang nyaris tak pernah didengar leluhur mereka sebelum kapal itu berlayar.

The same fate, even farther from home, befell the coolies sent to Suriname. From 1890 to 1939, at least 32,000 Javanese were transported on months-long voyages across two oceans to work the sugar and coffee plantations of Suriname, filling the labour gap left after slavery was abolished there in 1863. Many never returned to Java; some married, raised families, and settled in that new land, forming a Javanese-Surinamese community that endures to this day — now recognised as one of the largest ethnic groups in Suriname, preserving the Javanese language, wayang, and gamelan on a side of the globe their ancestors had scarcely heard of before the ship ever sailed.

Di balik jargon "balas budi" dan pilar "Emigrasi" yang tampak manusiawi di atas kertas kebijakan Den Haag, tersembunyi kenyataan pahit yang sama dengan yang selalu berulang dalam epos ini: tubuh manusia Nusantara dipindahkan melintasi lautan bukan demi kesejahteraan mereka sendiri, melainkan demi memenuhi permintaan pasar dunia akan tembakau, gula, dan karet.

Behind the "debt of honour" jargon and the seemingly humane pillar of "Emigration" as it appeared on paper in The Hague, lay hidden the same bitter reality that recurs throughout this epic: the bodies of Nusantara people were moved across oceans not for their own welfare, but to meet the world market's demand for tobacco, sugar, and rubber.

I. Belanda yang Netral, Hindia yang TerguncangI. A Neutral Netherlands, a Shaken Indies

When the guns began to roar in the trenches of Flanders and the Somme in August 1914, the Kingdom of the Netherlands chose neutrality in the First World War — a decision that spared the mother country the physical devastation that ravaged Belgium and France. Tjokroaminoto, nicknamed by the people the Uncrowned King of Java, gave speeches in town squares across Java blending Islamic rhetoric, economic justice, and sharp criticism of Dutch colonial capitalism — a voice that found its strongest echo precisely amid the hardship of a distant war the Javanese people never saw, yet whose impact they felt keenly in their own kitchens. Within the ranks of Sarekat Islam grew the seeds of an ideology that would later split: the Islamic-right wing led by Tjokroaminoto and Agus Salim, and the revolutionary-left wing led by Semaun and Darsono in the Semarang branch — the seed that would give birth to the Communist Party of Indonesia (PKI).

To dampen rising social unrest, the colonial government finally established the Volksraad (People's Council) in 1918 — a quasi-representative body, some of whose members were elected, but whose authority was limited to giving advice, with no real legislative power over the Governor-General. To the more radical movement figures, the Volksraad was seen as nothing but colonial trickery — a promise of cosmetic reform amid the very real wartime economic pressure felt by the common people.

Ironically, amid the economic turmoil pressing down on the common people, two new commodities from the Dutch East Indies were becoming strategic prizes in a world at war: rubber and petroleum. After the vulcanisation process was discovered in the 19th century, rubber became an essential material for motor-vehicle tyres, military trucks, and field piping — a material that let modern mechanised warfare, with trucks and armoured vehicles, move in ways entirely unlike the cavalry warfare of the generation before. Rubber plantations in East Sumatra and Kalimantan multiplied many times in value once Europe was gripped by wartime demand. At the same time, the oil fields of Langkat (North Sumatra), operated by a company that would become Shell, supplied fuel for Allied ships and vehicles. From this moment on, the Dutch East Indies was no longer seen merely as a granary of spices and sugar, but as a strategic energy and material asset whose value would keep rising throughout the 20th century — including, later, becoming one of the reasons Japan so badly wanted to seize it in 1942.

World War I (1914–1918) killed roughly 17 million people and brought down four great European empires at once: the German, Austro-Hungarian, and Ottoman Empires, and, most shocking of all to the world, the Russian Empire, which fell to the Bolshevik Revolution led by Vladimir Lenin in October 1917, giving birth to the world's first communist state. That revolution's echo crossed continents faster than any colonial government could have imagined: a Dutch socialist teacher named Henk Sneevliet carried Marxist ideas directly to Semarang, founding the Indische Sociaal-Democratische Vereeniging (ISDV) in 1914 — the seed of Asia's first communist party. Meanwhile, US President Woodrow Wilson, in his "Fourteen Points" speech at war's end (1918), proclaimed the principle of self-determination for colonised peoples — words that, though originally aimed at Eastern Europe, were quoted and echoed by movements across Asia and Africa as moral justification for their own dreams of independence.

Sumpah Pemuda menjadi penanda penting: untuk pertama kalinya, identitas "Indonesia" yang melampaui suku, agama, dan kedaerahan diikrarkan secara resmi dan sadar oleh generasi muda yang lahir dan dididik di bawah kolonialisme itu sendiri.

This decade's peak came on 28 October 1928, when young people from various regional organisations — Jong Java, Jong Sumatranen Bond, Jong Celebes, Jong Ambon, and others — gathered at the Second Youth Congress in Batavia and swore the Youth Pledge: one homeland, one nation, one language, Indonesia. At the same congress, Indonesia Raya, composed by Wage Rudolf Soepratman, was played publicly for the first time in instrumental form only — its lyrics calling for independence were deemed too dangerous to sing in full before colonial observers. The Youth Pledge became a landmark: for the first time, an "Indonesian" identity transcending ethnicity, religion, and region was formally and consciously proclaimed by a young generation born and educated under colonialism itself.

The 1920s were an age of anti-colonial nationalist waves across Asia. The 1928 Youth Pledge in Batavia was no isolated event, but part of this same global wave: educated young generations across Asia's colonies, raised within colonial education systems yet absorbing modern ideas of nationhood and sovereignty, began turning back to demand the right to self-determination from the very colonisers who had educated them.

The fall of the Dutch East Indies in March 1942 was one chapter of a far larger wave of Japanese conquest. Only three months earlier, Imperial Japan's air fleet had bombed Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941), dragging the United States directly into World War II. In almost the same span of time, Singapore — the British fortress once called "impregnable" — also fell to Japan (15 February 1942), just two weeks before the Kalijati Capitulation. The whole of colonial Southeast Asia, from the Philippines to the Dutch East Indies, collapsed within months — a collapse of the world order so swift that both colonisers and colonised alike were left reeling. Berita kekalahan Jepang sengaja ditutup-tutupi oleh pemerintah militer Jepang di Hindia Belanda dari rakyat dan bahkan dari tokoh-tokoh pergerakan selama beberapa hari. Namun kaum pemuda radikal—terutama kelompok yang berkumpul di sekitar Sutan Sjahrir dan aktivis muda seperti Chaerul Saleh, Wikana, dan Sukarni—berhasil menangkap kabar itu lewat siaran radio gelap dan segera mendesak Soekarno-Hatta untuk memproklamasikan kemerdekaan sesegera mungkin, sebelum pasukan Sekutu (yang akan diwakili kedatangan tentara Inggris dan Belanda/NICA) sempat mendarat kembali dan memulihkan kekuasaan kolonial. Ketegangan antara kaum tua yang menghendaki kehati-hatian prosedural dan kaum muda yang mendesak tindakan segera memuncak dalam Peristiwa Rengasdengklok (16 Agustus 1945): sekelompok pemuda "menculik" Soekarno dan Hatta ke Rengasdengklok, Karawang, untuk mendesak mereka segera memproklamasikan kemerdekaan tanpa menunggu restu Jepang.

News of Japan's defeat was deliberately kept from the public — and even from movement leaders — by the Japanese military government in the Dutch East Indies for several days. But radical youths — especially the group gathered around Sutan Sjahrir and young activists such as Chaerul Saleh, Wikana, and Sukarni — managed to catch the news through underground radio broadcasts, and quickly pressed Soekarno and Hatta to proclaim independence as soon as possible, before Allied forces (represented by the arrival of British and Dutch/NICA troops) could land and restore colonial rule. Tension between the older generation, who wanted procedural caution, and the youth, who demanded immediate action, came to a head in the Rengasdengklok Affair (16 August 1945): a group of youths "kidnapped" Soekarno and Hatta to Rengasdengklok, Karawang, to press them to proclaim independence immediately, without waiting for Japan's blessing.

Momen antara jatuhnya bom atom dan penyerahan Jepang adalah salah satu jendela sejarah paling singkat sekaligus paling menentukan abad ke-20—dan bukan hanya bagi Indonesia. Di seluruh Asia yang sebelumnya diduduki Jepang, kekosongan kekuasaan yang sama membuka peluang serupa: di Vietnam, Ho Chi Minh memproklamasikan kemerdekaan Republik Demokratik Vietnam hanya dua minggu setelah Soekarno-Hatta, pada 2 September 1945—hari yang sama dengan penandatanganan resmi penyerahan Jepang di atas geladak USS Missouri di Teluk Tokyo. Baik Indonesia maupun Vietnam memanfaatkan celah waktu yang sama antara runtuhnya kekuasaan pendudukan Jepang dan kembalinya kekuatan kolonial lama (Belanda dan Prancis)—sebuah pelajaran yang menunjukkan bahwa kemerdekaan, ketika akhirnya tiba, sering kali direbut dalam hitungan hari yang genting, bukan diberikan dalam hitungan bulan yang tenang.

The moment between the atomic bombings and Japan's surrender was one of the shortest yet most decisive windows in 20th-century history — and not only for Indonesia. Across all of previously Japanese-occupied Asia, the same power vacuum opened similar opportunities: in Vietnam, Ho Chi Minh proclaimed the independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam just two weeks after Soekarno and Hatta, on 2 September 1945 — the very same day Japan's formal surrender was signed aboard the USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay. Both Indonesia and Vietnam seized the same narrow window between the collapse of Japanese occupying power and the return of the old colonial powers (the Dutch and the French) — a lesson showing that independence, when it finally comes, is often seized in a handful of critical days, not granted over calm months.

17 Agustus 1945: Proklamasi Kemerdekaan17 August 1945: The Proclamation of Independence

I. Malam Perumusan di Rumah Laksamana MaedaI. The Night of Drafting at Admiral Maeda's House

Setelah didesak kembali ke Jakarta oleh para pemuda dan mendapat jaminan keamanan dari Laksamana Muda Jepang Tadashi Maeda—seorang perwira yang secara pribadi bersimpati pada cita-cita kemerdekaan Indonesia—Soekarno, Hatta, dan Achmad Soebardjo merumuskan naskah proklamasi pada dini hari 17 Agustus 1945 di ruang makan rumah Maeda di Jakarta. Naskah singkat itu, hanya terdiri dari dua kalimat, ditulis tangan oleh Soekarno berdasarkan gagasan bersama, lalu diketik ulang oleh Sayuti Melik dengan sejumlah perubahan kecil namun bermakna—termasuk penggantian kata "wakil-wakil bangsa Indonesia" menjadi "Atas nama bangsa Indonesia".

After being pressed to return to Jakarta by the youths and receiving a guarantee of safety from Japanese Rear Admiral Tadashi Maeda — an officer personally sympathetic to Indonesia's independence cause — Soekarno, Hatta, and Achmad Soebardjo drafted the proclamation text in the early hours of 17 August 1945, in the dining room of Maeda's house in Jakarta. That brief text, only two sentences long, was handwritten by Soekarno based on their shared ideas, then retyped by Sayuti Melik with several small but meaningful changes — including replacing the phrase "representatives of the Indonesian people" with "In the name of the Indonesian people."

II. Proklamasi di Pegangsaan TimurII. The Proclamation at Pegangsaan Timur

Pada pukul 10 pagi, 17 Agustus 1945, di halaman rumah Soekarno di Jalan Pegangsaan Timur No. 56, Jakarta, di hadapan sekelompok kecil rakyat dan tokoh pergerakan, Soekarno membacakan teks Proklamasi Kemerdekaan Indonesia—tanpa iring-iringan militer, tanpa sorak-sorai massa besar, hanya disaksikan beberapa ratus orang yang berkumpul mendadak setelah kabar tersiar dari mulut ke mulut semalam sebelumnya. Bendera Merah Putih, dijahit tangan oleh Fatmawati, dikibarkan Suhud dan Latief Hendraningrat di tiang bambu sederhana, diiringi lagu Indonesia Raya yang kini boleh dinyanyikan penuh dengan liriknya, delapan belas tahun setelah pertama kali diperdengarkan secara diam-diam dalam Sumpah Pemuda.

At 10 o'clock in the morning on 17 August 1945, in the front yard of Soekarno's house at Jalan Pegangsaan Timur No. 56, Jakarta, before a small gathering of ordinary people and movement figures, Soekarno read out the text of Indonesia's Proclamation of Independence — with no military procession, no roaring crowds, witnessed only by a few hundred people who had gathered hastily after word spread by mouth the night before. The Red-and-White flag, hand-sewn by Fatmawati, was raised by Suhud and Latief Hendraningrat on a simple bamboo pole, accompanied by Indonesia Raya, now sung in full with its lyrics for the first time, eighteen years after it was first played quietly at the Youth Pledge.

"Kami bangsa Indonesia dengan ini menjatakan kemerdekaan Indonesia. Hal-hal jang mengenai pemindahan kekoeasaan d.l.l., diselenggarakan dengan tjara seksama dan dalam tempo jang sesingkat-singkatnja.""We, the people of Indonesia, hereby declare the independence of Indonesia. Matters concerning the transfer of power, etc., will be carried out in an orderly manner and in the shortest possible time."

Dengan dua kalimat sederhana yang dibacakan dalam waktu kurang dari dua menit itu, siklus panjang yang dimulai oleh Sanjaya di lembah Progo dua belas abad sebelumnya—perjuangan mengendalikan ruang, waktu, sumber daya, dan nasib sendiri di kepulauan ini—menemukan penyelesaiannya yang baru, bukan sebagai akhir cerita, melainkan sebagai pembuka babak yang sama sekali baru: sebuah bangsa yang, untuk pertama kalinya dalam sejarahnya yang panjang dan berlapis-lapis, menyebut dirinya sendiri dengan satu nama—Indonesia—dan mengambil alih kendali atas kisahnya sendiri.

With those two simple sentences, read aloud in under two minutes, the long cycle begun by Sanjaya in the Progo valley twelve centuries earlier — the struggle to control space, time, resources, and one's own fate across this archipelago — found a new resolution: not as the end of the story, but as the opening of an entirely new chapter — a nation that, for the first time in its long, layered history, called itself by a single name, Indonesia, and took control of its own story.

Anekdot PenutupClosing Anecdote

Jangka Jayabaya, yang telah dikutip berulang sepanjang epos ini sejak Bab 3, meramalkan datangnya "wong cebol wulangan" (bangsa kerdil seumur jagung) yang akan menguasai Jawa sebentar sebelum akhirnya "Jawa bakal mardika" — Jawa akan merdeka. Tiga setengah tahun pendudukan Jepang, persis seumur jagung dalam skala sejarah panjang Nusantara, menjadi jembatan pendek namun menentukan antara tiga setengah abad kolonialisme Eropa dan fajar kemerdekaan yang telah lama dinanti sejak zaman Sanjaya, Ken Arok, Gajah Mada, hingga Diponegoro—setiap dari mereka, dengan caranya masing-masing, telah menitipkan sepotong mimpi yang sama: sebuah tanah yang berdaulat atas dirinya sendiri.

The Jangka Jayabaya, quoted repeatedly throughout this epic since Chapter 3, foretold the coming of a "short people who would rule for the span of a maize crop" over Java before, at last, "Java shall be free." Three and a half years of Japanese occupation — almost exactly the span of a maize crop on the long scale of Nusantara history — became a brief but decisive bridge between three and a half centuries of European colonialism and the dawn of independence long awaited since the days of Sanjaya, Ken Arok, Gajah Mada, and Diponegoro — each of them, in his own way, entrusting the same shared dream: a land sovereign over itself.

Dunia yang Dibentuk oleh KomoditasA World Shaped by Commodities

I. Dari Papan Catur Global ke Meja Makan ModernI. From the Global Chessboard to the Modern Dinner Table

Bila epos ini dibaca ulang dari awal—dari lingga kemenangan Sanjaya di 732 M hingga bendera Merah Putih yang naik di Pegangsaan Timur pada 1945—satu benang merah lain akan tampak, tersembunyi di balik nama-nama raja dan tanggal-tanggal peperangan: hampir setiap perpindahan kekuasaan besar dalam kisah ini digerakkan, cepat atau lambat, oleh sebuah komoditas. Garam yang mengawetkan pangan sebelum ada pendingin. Lada dan cengkih yang mengundang jung-jung raksasa dari Tiongkok dan kapal-kapal Eropa menembus dua samudra. Kopi yang diselundupkan dalam kantong seorang pedagang VOC, lalu diam-diam dipungut dari kotoran luwak oleh pekerja paksa yang dilarang mencicipinya sendiri. Teh yang dibajak balik oleh mata-mata Inggris dari jantung Tiongkok. Karet dan minyak yang mengubah tanah jajahan menjadi aset strategis perang dunia. Selera makan, dorongan mencari kafein, dan kebutuhan akan sehelai kain mewah—hal-hal yang tampak remeh dalam kehidupan sehari-hari manusia—ternyata adalah kekuatan yang sama yang menggambar ulang garis perbatasan negara-negara modern, menentukan siapa menjajah siapa, dan pada akhirnya, siapa yang harus berjuang untuk merdeka dari siapa.

If this epic is read again from the beginning — from Sanjaya's victory lingga in 732 CE to the Red-and-White flag raised at Pegangsaan Timur in 1945 — another common thread appears, hidden behind the names of kings and the dates of wars: almost every great shift of power in this story was driven, sooner or later, by a commodity. Salt, which preserved food before refrigeration. Pepper and cloves, which drew giant Chinese jung and European ships across two oceans. Coffee, smuggled in a VOC trader's pocket, then quietly gathered from civet droppings by a forced labourer forbidden to taste it himself. Tea, pirated back by a British spy from the heart of China. Rubber and oil, which turned colonised land into a strategic asset of world war. An appetite, a craving for caffeine, a need for a length of fine cloth — things that seem trivial in everyday human life — turn out to be the very same forces that redrew the borders of modern nations, decided who colonised whom, and, in the end, who had to fight for freedom from whom.

Namun benang merah ini tidak selalu berwujud kapal dan pelabuhan yang gemerlap. Ia juga mengalir diam-diam lewat kekejaman yang tampaknya murni personal atau politis. Ketika Amangkurat I menutup paksa pelabuhan-pelabuhan pesisir utara pada abad ke-17 (Bab 12), yang ia perjuangkan sesungguhnya bukan sekadar rasa aman dari ancaman politik semata, melainkan kendali tunggal atas arus komoditas yang dahulu membuat para adipati pesisir sekaya dan sekuat itu—kendali yang, ironisnya, ia hancurkan sampai ke akar, dan yang kelak dijual habis kepada VOC oleh putranya sendiri demi harga sebuah takhta. Ketika ribuan orang Jawa diangkut sebagai koeli kontrak ke perkebunan tembakau Deli dan gula Suriname di ujung dunia lain (Bab 18), tubuh dan tenaga mereka pun menjadi bagian dari rantai pasok global yang sama—rantai yang menghubungkan cerutu di kafe-kafe Amsterdam dengan pundak seorang buruh yang tak pernah melihat kembali tanah kelahirannya. Dan ketika jutaan rakyat dikerahkan sebagai Romusha oleh sang "saudara tua" dari Timur (Bab 19B), yang mereka gali dan bangun—rel kereta, lapangan terbang, ladang minyak Palembang—sekali lagi adalah infrastruktur untuk memindahkan komoditas dan sumber daya, kali ini demi menghidupi mesin perang yang sedang sekarat.

But this thread does not always appear as glittering ships and ports. It also runs quietly through cruelty that seems purely personal or political. When Amangkurat I forced the closure of the north-coast ports in the 17th century (Chapter 12), what he was truly fighting for was not merely security from political threat, but sole control over the flow of commodities that had once made the coastal dukes so rich and so strong — control that, ironically, he destroyed down to its roots, and which his own son would later sell off entirely to the VOC for the price of a throne. When thousands of Javanese were shipped as contract coolies to the tobacco plantations of Deli and the sugar fields of Suriname on the far side of the world (Chapter 18), their bodies and labour, too, became part of that same global supply chain — a chain linking cigars in Amsterdam's cafés to the shoulders of a labourer who would never see his homeland again. And when millions were mobilised as Romusha by the "elder brother" from the East (Chapter 19B), what they dug and built — railways, airfields, the oil fields of Palembang — was, once again, infrastructure for moving commodities and resources, this time to keep a dying war machine alive.

Tiga kisah yang tampak berjarak berabad-abad ini—takhta pedalaman yang paranoid, kapal-kapal koeli yang berlayar ke benua asing, dan rel-rel maut zaman pendudukan—sesungguhnya adalah wajah yang sama diulang dalam kostum yang berbeda. Dinamika kemanusiaan, betapapun rumit dan personalnya ia tampak dalam catatan sejarah—cemburu seorang kakak kepada adiknya, ambisi seorang gubernur jenderal, propaganda seorang penguasa militer asing—hampir selalu, pada akarnya, berhubungan dengan satu dorongan purba yang sama: dorongan untuk menguasai, memindahkan, dan memenuhi kebutuhan akan komoditas. Sejarah Nusantara, dan barangkali sejarah manusia secara umum, bukanlah kisah raja-raja dan pahlawan yang berdiri sendiri; ia adalah kisah tentang siapa yang mengendalikan garam, rempah, kopi, karet, minyak, dan tenaga tubuh manusia yang memproduksinya—serta apa yang rela dilakukan manusia, baik yang mulia maupun yang mengerikan, demi mengendalikannya.

These three stories, seemingly centuries apart — a paranoid inland throne, coolie ships sailing to a foreign continent, and the deadly rails of an occupation era — are in truth the same face repeated in different costumes. Human dynamics, however complex and personal they appear in the historical record — a brother's jealousy toward his sibling, a governor-general's ambition, a foreign military ruler's propaganda — almost always, at root, trace back to one and the same primal drive: the drive to control, move, and satisfy the demand for commodities. The history of the Nusantara, and perhaps human history in general, is not a story of kings and heroes standing alone; it is a story of who controls salt, spice, coffee, rubber, oil, and the human labour that produces them — and what people are willing to do, both noble and terrible, to control it.

II. Warisan Manusia Nusantara: Bukan Sekadar Objek PenjajahanII. The Legacy of the Nusantara's People: Never Merely Objects of Colonisation

Jika ada satu kesimpulan yang ingin ditinggalkan epos ini kepada pembacanya, itu adalah penolakan halus terhadap satu narasi lama yang keliru: bahwa Nusantara hanyalah objek pasif yang ditemukan, direbut, dan dieksploitasi oleh kekuatan-kekuatan dari luar. Kisah kapal K'un-lun po yang disaksikan Wan Chen sejak abad ke-3, kisah para pelaut Barito yang menyeberangi seluruh Samudra Hindia hingga Madagaskar berabad-abad sebelum satu pun kapal Portugis menemukan jalan ke timur, dan kisah pekerja perkebunan yang mengubah keterbatasan menjadi Kopi Luwak—semuanya menunjukkan sebaliknya. Manusia Nusantara bukan sekadar penerima pasif arus sejarah dunia; mereka adalah salah satu perintis paling awal jalur maritim antarbenua, produsen yang menggerakkan mesin perdagangan global jauh sebelum istilah "globalisasi" diciptakan, dan—bahkan dalam masa-masa paling kelam penjajahan, tirani pedalaman, maupun pendudukan asing—tetap menjadi manusia yang berpikir, berakal, dan menciptakan, bukan sekadar tenaga kerja yang dipaksa dan tanah yang diperas.

If there is one conclusion this epic wishes to leave with its reader, it is a quiet rejection of one old, mistaken narrative: that the Nusantara was merely a passive object found, seized, and exploited by outside powers. The story of the K'un-lun po ships witnessed by Wan Chen as early as the 3rd century, the story of Barito sailors crossing the entire Indian Ocean to Madagascar centuries before a single Portuguese ship found its way east, and the story of plantation workers turning constraint into Kopi Luwak — all show the opposite. The people of the Nusantara were never merely passive recipients of the currents of world history; they were among the earliest pioneers of intercontinental maritime routes, producers who drove the engine of global trade long before the term "globalisation" was ever coined, and — even in the darkest ages of colonisation, inland tyranny, or foreign occupation — remained thinking, reasoning, creating human beings, not merely forced labour and squeezed land.

Dari sinilah epos ini menutup lingkarannya: bukan dengan Proklamasi sebagai titik akhir, melainkan dengan pengakuan bahwa jauh sebelum kata "merdeka" diucapkan Soekarno pada pagi 17 Agustus 1945, kedaulatan atas laut, atas rute dagang, dan atas nasib sendiri sesungguhnya telah lama menjadi milik manusia kepulauan ini—hilang untuk sementara dalam tiga setengah abad kolonialisme, namun tidak pernah benar-benar padam, dan pada akhirnya, direbut kembali. Dan barangkali, di situlah letak pelajaran paling jujur yang bisa diwariskan epos ini: selama manusia masih membutuhkan garam untuk mengawetkan makanannya, rempah untuk membumbui mejanya, dan minyak untuk menyalakan mesinnya, dinamika kekuasaan dan kekejaman yang sama akan terus mengulang dirinya sendiri—hanya menunggu komoditas berikutnya, dan tuan berikutnya, untuk memberinya nama baru.

Here, then, is where this epic closes its circle: not with the Proclamation as an ending point, but with the recognition that long before the word "free" was spoken by Soekarno on the morning of 17 August 1945, sovereignty over the sea, over trade routes, and over one's own fate had, in truth, long belonged to the people of this archipelago — lost for a time across three and a half centuries of colonialism, but never truly extinguished, and in the end, reclaimed. And perhaps that is the most honest lesson this epic can leave behind: for as long as human beings still need salt to preserve their food, spice to season their table, and oil to fuel their engines, the same dynamics of power and cruelty will keep repeating themselves — waiting only for the next commodity, and the next master, to give it a new name.

This manuscript retells the long history of the Nusantara through frameworks of ecology, political economy, the technology of power, and the psychology of legitimacy. This edition adds "World Mirror" boxes (synchronic comparisons with contemporaneous world events), "Connecting Anecdote" boxes (narrative bridges between chapters), and "Commodity Trail" boxes (a thematic thread of global trade — salt, spices, coffee, tea, rubber, and oil) at key story nodes, so readers grasp the context, magnitude, and global-economic connections of Nusantara events. Chapters 2B, 8E, 11B, 18B, 18C, 19B, 20, 21, and 22 are narrative expansions extending the chronology to the 1945 Proclamation of Independence, deepening the Srivijaya–Majapahit–Demak lineage, examining spice-era resistance in Ternate, Tidore, and Makassar, and tracing how European colonial treaties shaped the modern geopolitical map of the Nusantara. Chapter 12 deepens the account of Amangkurat I's tyranny and fall; Chapter 18 adds the story of indentured coolie labourers shipped to Deli and Suriname; Chapter 19B enriches the story of Romusha under the "Elder Brother" framing; and Chapter 22 closes the epic with a synthesis that human dynamics are, again and again, bound to the drive to obtain and control commodities. All ASCII diagrams from the original manuscript have been removed and replaced with flowing narrative for better readability across devices. This manuscript is an interpretive narrative history; dates, casualty figures, and attributions in the comparison boxes are drawn from general historical knowledge and the babad tradition, and remain open to further academic verification, as explained in the Methodological Note at the start of the manuscript.
